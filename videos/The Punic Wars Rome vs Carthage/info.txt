# The Punic Wars: Rome vs. Carthage (264–146 BC)  
**Comprehensive Historical Research Dossier**

---

## 1. BACKGROUND

### 1.1 Historical Context
*   **Mediterranean Power Vacuum (3rd Century BC):** Following the death of Alexander the Great (323 BC) and the fragmentation of his empire, the Western Mediterranean lacked a single hegemon.
*   **Two Rising Powers:** 
    *   **Rome:** Completed the conquest of the Italian peninsula (Pyrrhic War ended 275 BC). Possessed a massive, highly organized citizen-soldier manpower pool and a unique system of alliances (*socii*).
    *   **Carthage:** A Phoenician colony (founded c. 814 BC) turned thalassocracy. Dominated maritime trade routes, controlled North Africa, Southern Spain, Sardinia, Corsica, and Western Sicily. Relied on mercenary armies and a professional navy.
*   **The "Cold War" Phase:** A series of treaties existed (509 BC, 348 BC, 306 BC) defining spheres of influence. The 306 BC treaty explicitly forbade Romans from sailing past the "Fair Promontory" (Cape Bon) unless forced by weather, acknowledging Carthaginian naval supremacy.

### 1.2 Causes of the Conflict
1.  **The "Sicily Question":** Strategic grain basket; gateway between East/West Mediterranean. Neither power could tolerate the other controlling it entirely.
2.  **Mamertine Incident (264 BC):** The immediate *casus belli*. Campanian mercenaries (Mamertines) seized Messana (NE Sicily). Pressured by Syracuse (Hiero II), they appealed to *both* Carthage and Rome.
3.  **Roman *Fides* & Expansionism:** Roman aristocracy driven by glory (*gloria*), plunder, and the need to settle veterans. The Senate feared a Carthaginian foothold in Messana threatening Italy.
4.  **Carthaginian Overextension:** Carthage accepted the Mamertine appeal, garrisoned Messana, but then failed to withdraw when Rome intervened, viewing it as a police action, not a declaration of total war.

### 1.3 Political Situation
*   **Rome:** Republic. **Senate** (advisory, *de facto* executive) dominated by noble families (*nobiles*: Fabii, Cornelii, Claudii). **Consuls** (2, annual) held *imperium* (military command). **Centuriate Assembly** declared war. **Tribunes of the Plebs** protected commoners. *Conflict of the Orders* largely resolved (287 BC *Lex Hortensia*), but tension remained.
*   **Carthage:** Mixed Constitution (Aristotle praised it). **Suffetes** (2, annual, judicial/executive). **Council of 104** (judicial oversight). **Popular Assembly** (limited power). **Two Factions:** 
    *   *Land/Oligarchic Party* (Hanno the Great): Focus on African territory, oppose war costs.
    *   *Maritime/Commercial Party* (Hamilcar Barca, later Hannibal): Focus on trade, naval power, Iberian expansion.

### 1.4 Economic Situation
*   **Rome:** Agrarian base. Wealth from booty, indemnities, and *ager publicus* (public land). Coinage introduced late (c. 300 BC bronze, 211 BC silver *denarius*). No standing army; soldiers were property-owning citizens serving seasonally (until Marius, 107 BC).
*   **Carthage:** Commercial empire. Wealth from tribute (Libyans, Numidians), trade monopolies (tin, silver, purple dye, grain), and customs duties. Minted high-quality gold (staters), silver (shekels), and bronze. Could hire mercenaries instantly but suffered cash-flow crises during long wars.

### 1.5 Social Situation
*   **Rome:** Strict hierarchy: *Patricians* vs. *Plebeians* (legal equality achieved, but social distinction remained). *Clientela* (patronage) bound society. *Socii* (Italian allies) provided ~50% of manpower but lacked citizenship. Slavery increasing (Sicily, war captives).
*   **Carthage:** Citizen body small (~few thousand adult males). **Libyphoenicians** (mixed descent). **Libyans** (subject peasantry, heavily taxed). **Mercenaries** (Gauls, Iberians, Libyans, Greeks, Balearic slingers). **Numidian Allies** (cavalry). Deep social cleavage between merchants and subject populations.

### 1.6 Religious Situation
*   **Rome:** State religion contractual (*do ut des*). Gods: Jupiter Optimus Maximus, Mars, Quirinus. **Sibylline Books** consulted for new cults (e.g., Magna Mater/Cybele imported 204 BC). *Flamines*, *Vestals*, *Augurs*. *Evocatio* (calling enemy gods).
*   **Carthage:** Phoenician pantheon. **Baal Hammon** (Lord of Heaven/Weather, identified with Cronus/Saturn) & **Tanit** (Face of Baal, celestial goddess). **Melqart** (Heracles, patron of Tyre/Carthage, crucial for Barcid dynasty). **Tophet:** Sacred precinct for child sacrifice (molk) – debated historicity/extent, but Romans used it for propaganda (*Punica fides*).

## 2. TIMELINE: KEY DATES & CHRONOLOGY

### First Punic War (264–241 BC)
*   **264 BC:** Romans cross to Messana (Appius Claudius Caudex). Siege of Agrigentum.
*   **261 BC:** Rome decides to build a fleet (100 quinqueremes, 20 triremes) based on captured Carthaginian wreck.
*   **260 BC:** **Battle of Mylae** – First Roman naval victory (Duilius, *corvus*).
*   **256 BC:** **Battle of Cape Ecnomus** – Largest naval battle in history (330+ ships/side). Roman invasion of Africa (Regulus).
*   **255 BC:** **Battle of Tunis/Bagradas** – Xanthippus (Spartan) destroys Roman army; Regulus captured. Roman fleet destroyed in storm (1st major loss).
*   **254–250 BC:** Stalemate in Sicily. Romans capture Panormus (Palermo).
*   **249 BC:** **Battle of Drepana** – P. Claudius Pulcher ignores auspices, loses 93 ships. 2nd Roman fleet lost in storm. Rome nearly bankrupt.
*   **247 BC:** Hamilcar Barca takes command in Sicily (guerrilla warfare from Mt. Ercte/Hercte).
*   **242 BC:** Rome builds *final* fleet (200 ships) via private donations (Patriotic loan).
*   **241 BC, March 10:** **Battle of the Aegates Islands** – Lutatius Catulus destroys Carthaginian relief fleet. **Treaty of Lutatius:** Carthage evacuates Sicily, pays 3,200 talents over 10 years.

### Interwar Period (241–218 BC)
*   **241–237 BC:** **Mercenary War (Truceless War)** – Carthage nearly destroyed by unpaid mercenaries (Mathos, Spendius) + Libyan revolt. Hamilcar Barca saves Carthage. Rome seizes Sardinia/Corsica (238 BC) opportunistically; Carthage pays 1,200 talents extra.
*   **237 BC:** Hamilcar Barca departs for **Spain** (Gadir/Cádiz). Begins Barcid conquest of Iberia (silver mines, manpower).
*   **226 BC:** **Ebro Treaty** – Hasdrubal the Fair agrees not to cross Ebro River northwards.
*   **221 BC:** Hannibal (26) elected Commander-in-Chief in Spain.
*   **219 BC:** **Siege of Saguntum** (8 months). South of Ebro, but allied to Rome. Rome declares war.

### Second Punic War (218–201 BC)
*   **218 BC:** 
    *   March: Hannibal leaves New Carthage (Qart Hadasht).
    *   July: Crosses Pyrenees.
    *   October: Crosses Rhône (battle with Volcae).
    *   Late Oct: **Crosses Alps** (Col de la Traversette? Montgenèvre? Little St Bernard?). Arrives Italy with ~20k infantry, 6k cavalry, few elephants.
    *   Nov: **Battle of Ticinus** (Scipio wounded).
    *   Dec: **Battle of Trebia** – Hannibal destroys Sempronius Longus.
*   **217 BC:** 
    *   June: **Battle of Lake Trasimene** – Ambush destroys Flaminius' army (15k dead).
    *   Fabius Maximus appointed **Dictator** ("Cunctator" – Delayer). Strategy of attrition.
*   **216 BC:** 
    *   Aug 2: **Battle of Cannae** – Double envelopment. ~50-70k Romans killed (Consuls Paullus + Varro). **Strategic Turning Point:** Capua, Tarentum, Southern Italy defect. Philip V of Macedon allies with Hannibal.
*   **215–212 BC:** 
    *   Syracuse defects (Hieronymus). Marcellus sieges Syracuse (Archimedes' defenses).
    *   Scipio brothers (Publius & Gnaeus) fight in Spain.
*   **211 BC:** 
    *   Rome refuses to ransom Cannae prisoners. **Capua falls**.
    *   Scipio brothers killed in Spain (Battle of Upper Baetis).
*   **210 BC:** **P. Cornelius Scipio (Africanus)** (25) given *proconsular imperium* for Spain.
*   **209 BC:** Scipio captures **New Carthage** (logistical masterstroke).
*   **208 BC:** Baecula (Scipio beats Hasdrubal Barca).
*   **207 BC:** **Battle of the Metaurus** – Hasdrubal Barca (marching from Spain to join Hannibal) intercepted and killed by Nero & Livius Salinator. Hannibal learns of brother's death via thrown head.
*   **206 BC:** **Battle of Ilipa** – Scipio expels Carthage from Spain.
*   **205 BC:** Scipio elected Consul. Prepares invasion of Africa. "Pacification" of Sicily.
*   **204 BC:** Scipio lands at **Utica**. Besieges Utica. Burns Carthaginian/Numidian camps.
*   **203 BC:** **Battle of the Great Plains** – Scipio/Massinissa crush Syphax/Hasdrubal Gisco. Hannibal recalled from Italy.
*   **202 BC, Oct 19:** **Battle of Zama** – Scipio + Massinissa defeat Hannibal. **Treaty:** Carthage loses Spain, fleet (10 ships), pays 10,000 talents over 50 years, forbid war without Rome's permission. Massinissa gets Numidian kingdom.

### Third Punic War (149–146 BC)
*   **150 BC:** Carthage pays off indemnity. Sends embassy to Rome; Cato the Elder ends every speech *"Ceterum censeo Carthaginem esse delendam"*.
*   **149 BC:** Rome declares war (pretext: Carthage armed vs. Massinissa without permission). Consuls Manilius & Censorinus land at Utica. Carthage surrenders hostages, arms, warships. Rome demands **abandon city, move 10 miles inland**. Carthage refuses; prepares defense.
*   **149–148 BC:** Roman incompetence. Disease. Scipio Aemilianus (adopted grandson of Africanus) distinguishes self as tribune.
*   **147 BC:** Scipio Aemilianus elected Consul *underage* (lex annulled). Takes command. Builds **mole/breakwater** to block harbor. Isolates city.
*   **146 BC, Spring:** Final assault. **6 days of street fighting** (Byrsa citadel). 
*   **146 BC, Feb:** **Fall of Carthage**. 50,000 survivors sold into slavery. City razed (ritual plowing? debated). **Province of Africa** created (capital Utica). Corinth destroyed same year.

## 4. LOCATIONS

### 4.1 Major Theaters
1.  **Sicily:** Primary theater 1st War. Key cities: **Messana, Syracuse, Agrigentum, Panormus, Lilybaeum, Drepana, Aegates Islands.** Terrain: Mountainous interior, coastal plains. Volcanic (Etna).
2.  **Italy (Mainland):** Primary theater 2nd War. **Po Valley (Cisalpine Gaul), Etruria, Latium, Campania (Capua), Apulia (Cannae), Lucania, Bruttium.** Rivers: Po, Ticinus, Trebia, Arno, Trasimene, Aufidus.
3.  **Iberia (Hispania):** Barcid base / Scipio's conquest. **Gadir (Cádiz), Malaca, Carthago Nova (Cartagena), Saguntum, Tarraco, Baecula, Ilipa, Castulo.** Rivers: Ebro, Baetis (Guadalquivir), Tagus. Silver mines: **Sierra Morena, Cartagena.**
4.  **North Africa:** Carthaginian heartland / Scipio's invasion. **Carthage (Tunis), Utica, Hippo Regius, Zama (Naraggara?), Great Plains (Bagradas Valley), Cirta (Constantine).** Cape Bon, Cape Farina.
5.  **Sardinia & Corsica:** Seized by Rome 238 BC. Grain supply, naval bases.
6.  **Greece/Macedon:** Philip V alliance (2nd War). Illyria.

### 4.2 Key Cities & Strongholds
*   **Rome:** Capital. Servian Wall (4th C). *Campus Martius* (muster). *Forum* (politics). *Temple of Jupiter Optimus Maximus* (Capitoline).
*   **Carthage:** **Byrsa** (Citadel/Acropolis). **Cothon** (Artificial double harbor: circular military harbor w/ admiral's island + rectangular merchant harbor). **Tophet** (Salammbo). **Megara** (suburb). Walls: 37 km circuit (4.5m thick, 9m high), barracks for 200 elephants, 4,000 horses, 24,000 men.
*   **Syracuse:** Fortress city. **Ortygia** (island citadel). **Euryalus Fortress** (Dionysian walls). Archimedes' defenses.
*   **New Carthage (Qart Hadasht):** Peninsular harbor. Silver mines nearby. Best harbor in W. Med. Captured by Scipio 209 BC.
*   **Capua:** 2nd largest city in Italy. Defected to Hannibal 216 BC. Recaptured 211 BC. Punished severely (citizenship lost, land confiscated).
*   **Numantia:** (Later, 134 BC) Celtiberian stronghold destroyed by Scipio Aemilianus.

## 5. MAJOR EVENTS (Detailed Analysis)

### FIRST PUNIC WAR (264–241 BC)

#### **Outbreak & Land War in Sicily (264–260 BC)**
*   **What:** Rome intervenes in Messana. Alliance with Hiero II of Syracuse (263). Siege of Agrigentum (262) – Roman victory but Carthaginian garrison escapes.
*   **Why:** Fear of Carthaginian bridgehead at Messana (Strait of Messina).
*   **Consequence:** Rome realizes it cannot win in Sicily without naval power to interdict supply lines.

#### **Roman Naval Construction & The *Corvus* (261–260 BC)**
*   **What:** Rome builds 120 ships in 60 days (copied Carthaginian "five"). Invents **corvus** (boarding bridge, 11m long, 1.2m wide, iron spike).
*   **Why:** Romans poor sailors; *corvus* turns sea fight into land fight (legionaries vs marines).
*   **Consequence:** Tactical surprise. Victory at **Mylae (260)**. Duilius gets 1st naval triumph. *Corvus* makes ships top-heavy/unseaworthy (contributed to later storm losses).

#### **Invasion of Africa & Regulus Disaster (256–255 BC)**
*   **What:** Massive fleet (330 ships) wins **Cape Ecnomus**. Regulus lands at Aspis/Clupea. Ravages countryside. Wins **Adys**. Offers harsh terms (Carthage surrenders Sicily, Sardinia, Corsica, pays indemnity, no navy, vassalage). Carthage refuses. Hires **Xanthippus** (Spartan). Reforms army: Phalanx + Elephants + Cavalry. Crushes Regulus at **Bagradas/Tunis**. Regulus captured. Roman rescue fleet wins at **Cape Hermaeum** but **destroyed in storm** (184 ships lost, 100k+ men).
*   **Why:** Rome seeks knockout blow.
*   **Consequence:** Rome abandons African theater. Focus returns to Sicily. Carthage learns Roman infantry beatable with combined arms.

#### **Sicily Stalemate & Hamilcar Barca (254–241 BC)**
*   **What:** Rome captures **Panormus (254)**. Failed siege of **Lilybaeum** (main Carth base). **Drepana (249)** – Pulcher disaster (93 ships lost). 2nd fleet lost in storm. Rome bankrupt, exhausted. **Hamilcar Barca** lands (247). Guerrilla war from **Mt. Ercte (Hercte)** then **Mt. Eryx**. Raids Italy (Bruttium).
*   **Why:** Carthage holds western fortress ports. Rome lacks fleet.
*   **Consequence:** War of attrition. Hamilcar undefeated but isolated. Rome makes one final financial effort.

#### **Battle of the Aegates Islands & Treaty (241 BC)**
*   **What:** Rome builds 200 quinqueremes (private donations, modeled on Rhodian "fives" - faster, no *corvus*). Trains crews rigorously. Catulus blocks Lilybaeum. Carthaginian relief fleet (Hanno) laden with supplies/grain, undermanned. **March 10, 241:** Catulus attacks with wind advantage. **Carthage loses 50 sunk, 70 captured.** Hamilcar cut off in Sicily, forced to negotiate.
*   **Treaty Terms (Lutatius):** 
    1.  Evacuate Sicily & islands between Sicily/Africa.
    2.  Release prisoners without ransom.
    3.  Pay **3,200 talents** (800 immediate, 2,200 over 10 yrs).
    4.  No war on Syracuse/allies.
*   **Consequence:** **Rome gains first province (Sicily).** Carthage humiliated, financially ruined, mercenaries unpaid -> Mercenary War.

---

### INTERWAR PERIOD (241–218 BC)

#### **Mercenary War / Truceless War (241–237 BC)**
*   **What:** 20,000+ mercenaries (Libyans, Iberians, Celts, Greeks, Balearics) gather at Sicca Veneria. Carthage (Hanno) mishandles pay dispute. **Mathos (Libyan) & Spendius (Campanian)** lead revolt. Libyans join (oppressive taxation). Cities: Utica, Hippacra defect. Carthage besieged. **Hamilcar Barca** given command. Breaks siege. **"Saw" battle** (canyon ambush). Crucifies Spendius. **Battle of Leptis Parva (237)** – Final victory.
*   **Atrocities:** Torture, mutilation, crucifixion, trampling by elephants on both sides. "War without pity."
*   **Rome's Role:** Initially neutral/returns prisoners. **238 BC:** Mercenaries in Sardinia appeal to Rome. Rome **annexes Sardinia & Corsica**, demands **1,200 talents** extra indemnity. Carthage too weak to resist.
*   **Consequence:** **Barcid hatred of Rome cemented.** Hamilcar goes to Spain to rebuild. Carthage loses strategic depth (Sardinia/Corsica).

#### **Barcid Spain (237–219 BC)**
*   **Hamilcar (237–228):** Lands Gadir. Conquers Turdetani, silver mines (Sierra Morena). Founds **Akra Leuke (Alicante)**. Drowns crossing Jucar.
*   **Hasdrubal the Fair (228–221):** Diplomatic consolidation. Founds **Qart Hadasht (227)**. **Ebro Treaty (226)** with Rome (likely defines spheres, not just "no crossing"). Assassinated.
*   **Hannibal (221–219):** Completes conquest N. of Ebro (Vaccaei, Olcades). **Siege of Saguntum (219)** – 8 months. City south of Ebro but "friend of Rome." Rome debates, sends embassy to Carthage. Carthage supports Hannibal. Rome declares war.

---

### SECOND PUNIC WAR (218–201 BC)

#### **Hannibal's March & Alps Crossing (218 BC)**
*   **Forces:** ~90k infantry, 12k cavalry, 37 elephants (start Spain). Leaves 15k garrison (Hanno), sends 10k home.
*   **Route:** New Carthage -> Ebro -> Pyrenees (col de Banyuls?) -> Gaul (friendly Volcae/Tectosages) -> Rhône crossing (opposite Avignon, boats/rafts for elephants) -> **Alps (Late Oct)**.
*   **Alps Debate:** **Col de la Traversette** (highest, 2950m, matches Polybius "bare rock", "landslide", "white snow year-round", archaeological carbon dating of horse manure 218 BC) vs. Montgenèvre / Little St Bernard.
*   **Losses:** ~50% attrition. Arrives Po Valley: **20k infantry, 6k cavalry, few elephants.**
*   **Consequence:** Strategic surprise. Romans (Scipio at Massilia, Sempronius at Lilybaeum) wrong-footed.

#### **Battles of Ticinus, Trebia, Trasimene (218–217 BC)**
*   **Ticinus (Nov 218):** Cavalry skirmish. Scipio wounded, saved by son (Africanus). Romans retreat to Placentia.
*   **Trebia (Dec 218):** Sempronius lured across freezing river. **Mago's ambush (1,000 horse/1,000 foot in drainage ditch)**. Roman center breaks through but isolated. **~30k Roman casualties.** Gauls join Hannibal.
*   **Trasimene (June 217):** Flaminius chases Hannibal into narrow defile by lake. **Fog + Ambush (hills)**. Flaminius killed. **15k killed, 15k captured.** Hannibal releases Latin allies (propaganda). Rome panics. **Fabius Dictator.**

#### **Fabian Strategy & "War of Attrition" (217–216 BC)**
*   **What:** Fabius refuses battle. Shadows Hannibal. Burns crops ("scorched earth"). Hannibal ravages allies' land to provoke battle. Fabius stays on heights. **Minucius (Master of Horse)** wins minor skirmish (Geronium), made co-equal, trapped, saved by Fabius.
*   **Politics:** Roman public impatient. **"Delayer" mocked.** Varro/Paullus elected Consuls 216 on platform of "Finish the war."

#### **Battle of Cannae (Aug 2, 216 BC) – The Tactical Masterpiece**
*   **Forces:** Rome: **80-87k infantry, 6-8k cavalry** (8 legions + allies - largest army ever). Hannibal: **40k infantry, 10k cavalry** (Libyans, Iberians, Gauls, Numidians).
*   **Deployment:** Hannibal: **Convex crescent** (Gauls/Iberians center, Libyans on wings refused). Varro: **Deep manipular column** (dense, less maneuverable). Roman cavalry (right/Aufidus) vs Numidians; Allied cavalry (left) vs Iberians/Gauls (Hasdrubal).
*   **Action:** 
    1.  Hasdrubal routs Roman cavalry (left), sweeps behind Roman line, hits Allied cavalry (rear) -> Allied cavalry flees (Varro flees).
    2.  Roman center pushes back Gauls (crescent becomes concave).
    3.  **Libyan phalanxes (wings) wheel inward** -> Attack Roman flanks.
    4.  Hasdrubal's cavalry returns -> **Attacks Roman rear**.
    5.  **Double Envelopment (Kesselschlacht).** Romans packed so tight cannot use swords.
*   **Casualties:** **50,000–70,000 Romans killed** (Consul Paullus, 2 Quaestors, 29 Tribunes, 80 Senators). ~6,000 captured. Hannibal losses: ~6,000 (mostly Gauls).
*   **Immediate Consequence:** **Moral collapse.** Capua, Tarentum, Thurii, Metapontum, most Samnites, Lucanians, Bruttians defect. **Philip V of Macedon** allies with Hannibal. **Hannibal refuses to march on Rome** (Maharbal: "You know how to win, not how to use victory").
*   **Roman Response:** **Refused to negotiate.** Forbade mourning. Ransom refused. **Dictator M. Junius Pera** enrolls slaves, criminals, boys. **Fabius strategy reinstated.**

#### **The War in Italy (215–208 BC) – Stalemate**
*   **Hannibal:** Based at **Capua**. Tries to take **Nola** (fails 3x vs Marcellus). Takes **Tarentum (212)** (treachery) but citadel holds. Cannot take Roman Latin colonies (Venusia, Cosa, etc.).
*   **Rome:** Recaptures **Syracuse (212)** (Marcellus/Archimedes). Recaptures **Capua (211)** (starvation). **Scipio brothers die in Spain (211).**
*   **Hasdrubal's March (207 BC):** Leaves Spain early (Spring). Crosses Alps easier (Gauls friendly now). **Message to Hannibal intercepted.** **Consuls Nero & Livius Salinator** combine forces secretly. **Battle of Metaurus.** Hasdrubal trapped against river. **Killed. Head thrown into Hannibal's camp.**

#### **Scipio in Spain (210–206 BC) – The Turning Point**
*   **Appointment:** P. Cornelius Scipio (25) given *imperium pro consule* by Popular Assembly (unprecedented).
*   **New Carthage (209):** **Amphibious assault.** Exploits low tide/lagoon. Captures harbor, silver, hostages, stores. **Logistical masterstroke.**
*   **Baecula (208):** Defeats Hasdrubal Barca. Uses "reverse Cannae" (flank attack on deployed enemy).
*   **Ilipa (206):** **Masterpiece.** 45k Romans vs 50-70k Carthaginians (Mago, Hasdrubal Gisco). **Oblique order / Refused center.** Waits until noon (enemy unfed). Wings (Roman/Italian) smash Carthaginian wings (Iberians). Center holds. **Total victory.** Carthage expelled from Iberia. **Gades (Cádiz) falls.**
*   **Consequence:** Spain lost = No silver, no mercenaries, no reinforcement route for Hannibal.

#### **Invasion of Africa & Zama (204–202 BC)**
*   **Preparation:** Scipio elected Consul 205. Given Sicily. Trains army (Cannae survivors + volunteers). Allies with **Massinissa** (Numidian cavalry).
*   **Landing (204):** Utica. Besieged. **Night attack / Fire** destroys Hasdrubal Gisco & Syphax camps.
*   **Great Plains (203):** Scipio/Massinissa crush Syphax/Hasdrubal. Syphax captured. Massinissa becomes King of unified Numidia. **Hannibal recalled.**
*   **Negotiations:** Carthage accepts harsh terms (fleet burned). Hannibal arrives, hardliners reject treaty.
*   **Zama (Oct 19, 202 BC):** 
    *   **Forces:** Rome: ~34k infantry, **6-9k cavalry (Superior Numidian)**. Carthage: ~40-50k infantry (Mercs, Levies, Hannibal's Vets), 4k cavalry (weak), **80 Elephants**.
    *   **Deployment:** Scipio: **Manipular lanes** (gaps for elephants). Cavalry on wings (Laelius right, Massinissa left).
    *   **Action:** Elephants charge -> Lanes channel them / missiles panic them -> Turn back on Carthaginian left. Cavalry engages -> **Roman/Numidian cavalry chases Carthaginian cavalry OFF FIELD** (decisive).
    *   **Infantry:** Three lines clash. Roman *hastati/principes* push back mercenaries/levies. **Veterans (3rd line) held back.** Scipio reforms single line (triplex acies -> single acies). **Cavalry RETURNS** -> Hits Carthaginian rear.
    *   **Result:** **Carthage annihilated.** 20k killed, 20k captured. Hannibal escapes.
*   **Treaty (201 BC):** 
    1.  Independence, but **no war without Rome's permission**.
    2.  **Fleet: 10 triremes only.**
    3.  **Indemnity: 10,000 talents over 50 years.**
    4.  Territory: Recognize Massinissa's Numidia.
    5.  Hostages (100 nobles).
*   **Consequence:** **Rome hegemon of W. Mediterranean.** Carthage reduced to client city-state.

### THIRD PUNIC WAR (149–146 BC)

#### **Prelude: "Delenda Est Carthago" (150s BC)**
*   **Massinissa's Aggression:** Repeatedly raids Carthaginian territory (Great Plains, Emporia). Carthage appeals to Rome (arbitration). Rome delays/backs Massinissa.
*   **151 BC:** Carthage pays off indemnity early. Sends embassy. **Cato** visits Carthage, sees prosperity/arms. Returns with Libyan fig ("Fresh from Carthage 3 days ago"). Ends speeches: **"Ceterum censeo Carthaginem esse delendam."**
*   **150 BC:** Carthage raises army (25k) under Hasdrubal (general) to repel Massinissa. **Defeated at Oroscopa.** Rome declares treaty breach (war w/o permission).

#### **Outbreak & Roman Incompetence (149–148 BC)**
*   **Consuls:** Manilius (Army) & Censorinus (Fleet). Land at Utica.
*   **Carthage Submits:** Gives 300 hostages (children of nobles). **Surrenders ALL arms** (200k suits armor, 2k catapults). **Surrenders warships.**
*   **Roman Ultimatum:** "Carthage must be abandoned. Population moves **10 miles inland**. City destroyed."
*   **Carthage Refuses:** Gates closed. **Total mobilization.** Women cut hair for catapult ropes. Factories produce 100 shields, 300 swords, 500 spears, 1000 catapult missiles *daily*.
*   **Roman Siege Fails:** Disease (marsh camp). Hasdrubal (general) inside commands. **Scipio Aemilianus** (tribune) saves foraging parties, leads rear guards.

#### **Scipio Aemilianus & The Final Siege (147–146 BC)**
*   **147 BC:** Scipio elected Consul *underage* (lex Villia annulled by Senate/People). Given Africa.
*   **Isolation:** Builds **Mole (Breakwater)** across harbor mouth (Cothon entrance) -> **Blocks naval sorties/supply.** Builds circumvallation wall (ditch + palisade).
*   **Assault (Spring 146):**
    *   Breaches wall near harbor (naval siege engines).
    *   **6 Days Street Fighting:** Roof-to-roof, planks across alleys. **Byrsa (Citadel)** holds out.
    *   **Hasdrubal surrenders** (with family) to Scipio. **Wife & children** curse him, throw selves into burning temple of Eshmun.
    *   **50,000 survivors** (out of ~200-400k?) sold into slavery.
*   **Destruction:** City burned **17 days**. **Razed.** **Curse (devotio):** Scipio invokes gods to curse anyone rebuilding. **Province of Africa** created (Utica capital). **Agri Decumani** (tax district) later.

## 6. MILITARY SYSTEMS & EVOLUTION

### 6.1 Roman Military (Polybian Legion - c. 218 BC)
*   **Recruitment:** Property-owning citizens (*adsidui*). 17-46 yrs. 16-20 yrs service (6 continuous in emergency). **Socii** provide equal infantry, **3x cavalry**.
*   **Organization (Legion ~4,200 inf / 300 cav):**
    *   **Velites (1,200):** Poor/Young. Skirmishers. *Hasta* (javelins), *gladius*, small round shield (*parma*), wolfskin headdress.
    *   **Hastati (1,200):** Young men. Front line. **2x Pilum** (heavy/light), *Gladius Hispaniensis* (short thrusting sword), *Scutum* (large oval/rectangular shield), Montefortino helmet, *Pectorale* (chest plate) or **Lorica Hamata** (mail - wealthier).
    *   **Principes (1,200):** Prime men. Second line. Same gear, better mail.
    *   **Triarii (600):** Veterans. Third line. **Hasta (thrusting spear)** instead of pilum. Kneeling reserve.
    *   **Cavalry (300):** Wealthy citizens. *Hasta*, round shield. **Weakness:** Poor quality vs Numidians.
*   **Tactics:** **Manipular System (Checkerboard - Quincunx).** 3 lines, gaps covered by next line. **Flexibility** vs Phalanx rigidity. *Pilum* volley -> Charge (*impactus*) -> *Gladius* work.
*   **Camp:** Standardized marching camp (*castra*) every night. Ditch, rampart, palisade. Grid streets. Command center (*principia*).
*   **Evolution (Scipio in Spain):** 
    *   Adopted **Gladius Hispaniensis** (standard).
    *   Improved **Pilum** (soft iron shank bends on impact).
    *   **Cohort** (tactical unit ~480 men) replaces Maniple as primary maneuver element (flexibility).
    *   **Cavalry Reform:** Relies on **Allied/Numidian cavalry** (Laelius, Massinissa). Roman citizen cavalry disappears.

### 6.2 Carthaginian Military
*   **Structure:** **Heterogeneous Mercenary/Subject Army.** No standing citizen army (except Sacred Band - 2,500 elite citizens, last used 310 BC).
*   **Components:**
    *   **Libyans (Subjects):** Heavy infantry. **Phalanx** (Sarissa) or Roman-style (captured gear at Cannae/Zama). White shields.
    *   **Iberians:** Scutari (heavy, scutum, falcata sword), Caetrati (light, caetra buckler, javelins). **White tunics with purple border.**
    *   **Gauls/Celts (Cisalpine/Transalpine):** Heavy infantry. **Long slashing swords**, oval shields, no armor (often naked/chest bare). Ferocious charge, low endurance.
    *   **Libyphoenicians:** Citizen militia/officers.
    *   **Balearic Slingers:** 3 slings (short/med/long). **Lead/Bronze/Stone bullets (glandes).** Devastating range/accuracy.
    *   **Numidian Cavalry (Light):** **No saddle/bridle** (rope neck). Javelins. Hit-and-run. **Best light cav in world.**
    *   **Heavy Cavalry (Iberian/Gallic/Carthaginian):** Shock action. Saddles (four-horn).
    *   **War Elephants:** **North African Forest Elephant** (extinct, smaller than Indian/African Bush). ~2.5m shoulder. Tower (howdah) + 3 crew (mahout, 2 warriors). Psychological terror. Vulnerable to missiles/lanes/pigs.
    *   **Sacred Band:** Elite citizen phalanx (rarely deployed).
*   **Command:** **Strategos** (General) elected by army/popular assembly. **No unified drill/manual.** Relies on **personal leadership** of General (Hannibal) to coordinate disparate ethnic contingents. **Multilingual officers.**
*   **Navy:** **Quinquereme** standard. **Cothon** harbor allowed rapid deployment. **Navarch** (Admiral). Crews: Citizens + Subjects (unlike Roman *socii* rowers). **Tactics:** Ramming (*embolon*), boarding (marines). **Corvus** neutralized Carthaginian seamanship advantage in 1st War.

## 7. POLITICS & DIPLOMACY

### 7.1 Roman Constitution & War Powers
*   **Senate (Senatus):** *Auctoritas*. Controlled finances (*aerarium*), foreign policy, province assignment (*provinciae*), religion. **De facto** ruler. Met in *Curia Hostilia*.
*   **Magistrates:** **Consuls (2):** *Imperium maius*, command armies, preside Senate/Assemblies. **Praetors:** Justice, propraetor governors. **Dictator:** 6 months, *Magister Equitum*, appointed by Consul on Senate advice (emergency). **Censors (every 5 yrs):** Census, citizen rolls, Senate list, public contracts (*publicani*).
*   **Assemblies:** **Centuriate (Comitia Centuriata):** Wealth-based voting (centuries). Declared war, elected Consuls/Praetors/Censors. **Tribal (Comitia Tributa):** Geographic tribes. Legislation, elected lower magistrates. **Plebeian Council (Concilium Plebis):** Tribunes, Plebeian laws (*plebiscita* binding 287 BC).
*   **Imperium:** Power of command. **Auspicia:** Divine will (auspices). Pulcher at Drepana ignored -> disaster.
*   **Provincia:** "Task/Sphere of duty" -> Geographic province. **Prorogation:** Extension of *imperium* (Proconsul/Propraetor) – crucial for long wars (Scipio in Spain).

### 7.2 Carthaginian Constitution
*   **Suffetes (Shophetim):** 2 annually elected. Judicial/Executive. Not commanders.
*   **Council of Elders (Adirim / Gerousia):** ~300 members. Life tenure. Real power. **Council of 104:** Judicial oversight of generals (crucifixion penalty for failure).
*   **Popular Assembly (M):** Voted on war/peace, elected Suffetes/Generals. Gained power during Barcid era.
*   **Factionalism:** **Hanno (Land/Oligarchs)** vs **Barcids (Merchants/Military/Popular)**. Generals often tried by 104 after defeats (Hanno crucified after Agrigentum? Xanthippus fled). **No unified command structure** like Rome.

### 7.3 Treaties & Alliances
1.  **509 BC:** 1st Treaty (Polybius 3.22). Friendship. Rome not to sail past Fair Promontory. Carthage not to harm Latin cities.
2.  **348 BC:** 2nd Treaty. Updates trade limits. Carthage gets Sardinia/Libya.
3.  **306 BC:** 3rd Treaty. Rome stays out of Sicily (except Carthage territory). **Acknowledges Carthage naval dominance.**
4.  **241 BC (Lutatius):** End 1st War. Sicily to Rome. Indemnity.
5.  **226 BC (Ebro):** Hasdrubal / Rome. **Boundary at Ebro.** Saguntum status ambiguous.
6.  **215 BC (Hannibal-Philip V):** Macedon-Carthage Alliance. Philip attacks Rome (1st Macedonian War). Rome counters with Aetolian League.
7.  **204 BC (Scipio-Syphax/Massinissa):** Shifting Numidian alliances.
8.  **201 BC (Zama Treaty):** Carthage disarmed, indemnity, no war w/o Rome. **Client State.**
9.  **149 BC:** Rome demands Carthage "surrender" (*deditio*) -> Utter destruction.

### 7.4 Roman *Socii* System (The "Secret Weapon")
*   **Latins:** Old allies. *Ius Latii* (trade, intermarriage, migration). **Latin Colonies** (citizen colonies).
*   **Socii (Allies):** Bound by *foedus* (treaty). **No tribute**, but **military obligation** (equal infantry, double/triple cavalry). **No say in Roman foreign policy.**
*   **Civitas Sine Suffragio:** Citizenship without vote (Capua, Cumae etc. - later).
*   **Loyalty:** **Remarkably resilient.** Only Capua, Tarentum, some Samnites/Lucanians defected post-Cannae. Latin colonies **refused** Hannibal. **Manpower pool:** ~700,000 men (Polybius 2.24).

## 8. SOCIETY, ECONOMY & TECHNOLOGY

### 8.1 Daily Life & Culture
*   **Rome:** 
    *   *Domus* (Atrium house). *Paterfamilias* absolute power (*patria potestas*). 
    *   **Clientela:** Morning *salutatio*. Mutual obligation.
    *   **Women:** Legal minors (guardianship), but high social visibility (Cornelia, mother of Gracchi). 
    *   **Slavery:** Chattel. *Servus*. War captives (Sicily, Greece). *Vernae* (home-born). Brutal (ergastulum, chains). Revolts later (135, 104, 73 BC).
    *   **Religion:** Public sacrifice, augury, *Pontifex Maximus*. **New cults:** Cybele (204 BC), Bacchus (suppressed 186 BC).
*   **Carthage:** 
    *   Cosmopolitan merchant city. Multi-ethnic (Punic, Libyan, Greek, Iberian, Celt).
    *   **Tophet:** Urns with cremated infants/animals. **Debate:** Child sacrifice (Diodorus, Plutarch) vs. Infant cemetery (natural mortality). **Propaganda value for Rome immense.**
    *   **Trade:** **Tin (Cornwall/Brittany), Silver (Spain), Purple Dye (Murex), Ivory, Gold (West Africa), Grain (Sicily/Africa).** Periplus of Hanno (West Africa voyage).
    *   **Agriculture:** **Mago's Treatise (28 books)** - translated by Rome after 146 BC. Advanced viticulture, olive, irrigation.

### 8.2 Economy & Finance
*   **Rome:** 
    *   *Aes Grave* (Heavy bronze) -> **Denarius (211 BC)** silver standard (4.5g). **Victoriatus, Sestertius, Quinarius.**
    *   **War Finance:** **Tributum** (property tax on citizens). **Publicani** (tax farmers) bid for contracts. **Loans from citizens** (242 BC fleet). **Booty/Indemnities** major revenue.
    *   **Logistics:** *Frumentum* (grain) requisitioned from allies/Sicily/Sardinia. *Naves onerariae* (grain ships).
*   **Carthage:** 
    *   **Gold Staters (7.2g), Silver Shekels (7.5g), Bronze.** High purity. "Hannibal" coinage (elephant/horse) in Spain/Italy.
    *   **Tribute:** Heavy on Libyans (1/2 produce + poll tax). Cause of Mercenary War.
    *   **Mercenary Pay:** 1 drachma/day (infantry), 2-3 (cavalry). Arrears = Mutiny.

### 8.3 Technology & Engineering
*   **Naval:** **Quinquereme (Five):** 3 banks oars, 5 men per file (300 rowers). **Corvus** (Roman). **Diekplous** (break through), **Periplous** (outflank), **Embolon** (Ram). **Cothon** (Artificial harbor: circular military w/ slipways for 220 ships + rectangular merchant).
*   **Siege:** **Torsion Catapults** (Palintone - stone, Euthytone - bolt). **Helepolis** (Siege tower). **Sambuca** (Ship-borne ladder). **Archimedes' Claw / Heat Ray / Steam Cannon** (Syracuse).
*   **Roman Camp:** Standardized, surveyed (*Decumanus/Kardo*). **Vallum** (rampart) + **Fossa** (ditch). Built in hours.
*   **Roads:** **Via Appia (312 BC)** - strategic military highway. Stone paved, drainage, bridges.
*   **Metallurgy:** **Norican Steel (Noricum)** - best for swords (Gladius). **Pilum** design (soft iron shank).
*   **Elephants:** **Towers (Howdahs).** Mahout (driver) + 2-3 archers/spearmen. **Anti-elephant:** Lanes, missiles, fire, pigs (squeal), axes (hamstring).

### 9.4 Economic Data

*   **Sardinia Fine:** **1,200 Talents**.
*   **2nd War Indemnity:** **10,000 Talents** (~60,000,000 Denarii / 240 tons silver) over 50 yrs.
*   **Annual Roman Revenue (pre-167 BC):** ~5-10 Million Denarii (mostly Tributum + Vectigalia).
*   **Cost of Legion/Year:** ~300,000-400,000 Denarii (pay, grain, equipment).
*   **Silver Mines (Spain):** Produced ~300 Talents/Year (18 tons) for Barcids/Rome later.

### 9.5 Distances & Logistics
*   **Rome to Carthage (Sea):** ~370 nm (2-3 days favorable wind, 10+ unfavorable).
*   **Rome to Lilybaeum (Sicily):** ~230 nm.
*   **New Carthage to Gades:** ~250 km (coastal).
*   **Hannibal's March (Spain to Italy):** ~1,500 km (1,000 miles) in 5 months (May-Oct).
*   **Alps Crossing:** ~250 km mountain terrain. 15 days.
*   **Marching Speed:** Standard 20 Roman miles/day (29 km). Forced march 30-35 miles.
*   **Grain Consumption:** Legionary ~1.5 kg wheat/day. Army of 40k = 60 tons/day. **Logistics = Strategy.**

## 10. INTERESTING VISUAL DETAILS (For Documentary Imagery)

### 10.1 Clothing & Appearance
*   **Roman Soldier (Hastatus/Principes, 218 BC):** 
    *   **Helmet:** Montefortino (bronze/iron, conical, cheek pieces, plume holder - **3 purple/black feathers**).
    *   **Armor:** **Lorica Hamata** (Chainmail, ~20,000 rings, 10-15 kg) OR **Pectorale** (Square bronze/iron plate "heart protector" - poorer hastati). **Greave** (Single, left leg - bronze).
    *   **Shield:** **Scutum** (Oval -> Rectangular later. Plywood layers, canvas/leather cover, **Boss (umbo) iron**. Unit symbols: Legion number, Cohort sign, **Animal badges** (Wolf, Boar, Horse, Minotaur).
    *   **Weapons:** **Pilum** (2m total, 60cm soft iron shank, pyramidal head). **Gladius Hispaniensis** (65cm, waisted blade, bone/ivory grip, wooden scabbard covered leather, **suspended on RIGHT side** via *balteus* belt). **Pugio** (Dagger, left side).
    *   **Tunic:** Red wool (off-white/undyed for training). **Caligae** (Hobnailed sandals).
*   **Carthaginian Infantry:**
    *   **Libyan Phalangite:** White linen **Linothorax** (layered glue), Bronze **Phalanx Helm** (Attic/Thracian style), **Sarissa** (5-6m pike, two-handed), Small bronze shield (pelte). **White tunic.**
    *   **Iberian Scutari:** **White tunic with PURPLE BORDER** (distinctive). **Caetra** (Small round buckler) or **Scutum** (Large oval). **Falcata** (Curved single-edged sword, forward weight - devastating hack). **Soliferreum** (Heavy all-iron javelin).
    *   **Gaul/Celt:** **Naked or Trousers (Braccae)**. **Torc** (Gold neck ring). **Long slashing sword** (poor temper). **Oval shield** (vertical spine). **Lime-washed hair** (spiked back). **Carnyx** (Boar-headed war trumpet - terrifying sound).
    *   **Balearic Slinger:** **Three slings** (head, waist, hand). **Lead Glandes** (Almond shaped, inscribed: "DEXAI" - "Take this", "FULGUR" - "Lightning"). **No armor.** Goat skin cape.
*   **Cavalry:**
    *   **Roman:** Small round shield (clipeus), **Hasta** (thrusting spear). **No stirrups, no saddle horn** (four-horn Celtic saddle adopted later). Red crest.
    *   **Numidian:** **No saddle, no bridle** (rope neck loop). **Javelins** (bundle). Small round leather shield. **Tunic** (red/white). **Hair braided/beads**. Extreme agility.
    *   **Iberian/Gallic Heavy Cav:** **Mail armor**, **Helmets**, **Long thrusting spears**, **Large oval shields**.
*   **Elephants:** **North African Forest Elephant** (Smaller ears, concave back, ~2.5m shoulder). **Howdah** (Wooden tower, 3 men). **Mahout** (Driver, seated on neck, hook/axe). **Tusk caps** (Bronze/iron). **Trumpeter** on tower. **Caparison** (Decorated cloth).
*   **Commanders:** **Paludamentum** (Red/Purple cloak, fastened fibula right shoulder). **Muscled Cuirass** (Bronze, anatomical). **Pteruges** (Leather strips at shoulders/waist). **Calcei** (Red leather boots - Senators/Consuls). **Lictors** (12 for Consul, 24 for Dictator) carrying **Fasces** (Axe in rods).

## 11. CONSEQUENCES & LEGACY

### 11.1 Immediate Consequences (146 BC)
*   **Geopolitical:** **Rome sole superpower Mediterranean.** No rival. **Macedon defeated (168 BC, 148 BC), Corinth destroyed (146 BC), Seleucids checked (190 BC Magnesia).**
*   **Territorial:** **Province of Africa** (Tunisia/E. Algeria) - Grain basket. **Hispania** (2 provinces eventually). **Gaul (Narbonensis)** secured route to Spain.
*   **Demographic:** **Massive influx of slaves** (Sicily, Spain, Greece, Carthage). **Latifundia** (Slave plantations) begin displacing peasant farmers -> **Social crisis (Gracchi 133 BC).**
*   **Cultural:** **Hellenization** accelerates. Greek art, philosophy, literature, medicine flood Rome. **Scipionic Circle** (Polybius, Terence, Lucilius). **Cato's resistance** (De Agri Cultura - practical Latin prose).
*   **Military:** **Professionalization trend** (Long service, loyalty to General > State). **Marian Reforms (107 BC)** inevitable outcome.

### 11.2 Long-Term Historical Impact
1.  **Roman Imperialism:** Wars transformed Rome from Italian hegemon to **Mediterranean Empire**. *Imperium* becomes permanent.
2.  **Destruction of Carthage:** **Genocide/Ethnic Cleansing** (modern debate). Erasure of Punic civilization (language, literature - only Mago's ag survives in Latin; Hanno's Periplus in Greek). **Tophet** becomes symbol of "Barbarism" vs "Civilization" (Roman propaganda).
3.  **Military Doctrine:** **Cannae** becomes "Perfect Battle" studied forever (Schlieffen Plan, WWI, WWII). **Fabian Strategy** = Asymmetric warfare/Attrition. **Combined Arms** (Infantry + Cavalry + Elephants/Artillery) -> Legions + Auxilia.
4.  **Naval Power:** Rome becomes **naval power** (Mare Nostrum). Shipbuilding, logistics, marine corps.
5.  **Provincial Administration:** **Sicily (241), Sardinia/Corsica (238), Spain (197), Africa (146), Macedonia (146), Asia (133).** **Governors (Proconsuls) = Proconsular Imperium.** **Publicani** tax farming. **Client Kings** (Massinissa, Herod etc.).
6.  **Economy:** **Silver standard (Denarius)** fuels Mediterranean trade. **Italian wine/oil** exports (amphorae Dressel 1) replace Punic/Greek. **Grain supply (Annona)** from Africa/Sicily/Egypt feeds Rome.
7.  **Ideology:** **"Delenda est Carthago"** -> **Total War** concept. **Just War (Bellum Iustum)** theology develops (Fetiales). **Mos Maiorum** stressed as antidote to luxury/wealth.

### 11.3 Legacy & Historiography
*   **Sources:** 
    *   **Polybius (c. 200-118 BC):** Hostage in Rome, friend of Scipio Aemilianus. **Eyewitness/Participant.** "Pragmatic History". **Primary Source.** Survives in fragments (Books 1-5 complete, rest excerpts).
    *   **Livy (59 BC - 17 AD):** **Books 21-30 (2nd War), 31-45.** Uses Polybius, Coelius Antipater, Valerius Antias. **Literary/Annales.** Moralizing.
    *   **Appian (2nd C AD):** *Libyca* (Punic Wars). Uses lost sources.
    *   **Diodorus Siculus:** Fragments.
    *   **Plutarch:** *Lives* (Fabius, Marcellus, Flamininus, Cato, Aemilius Paullus).
    *   **Archaeology:** **Cannae** (mass graves), **Aegates** (Rams/Helmets/Amphorae), **Cothon** (Harbor excavation), **Tophet** (Stelae/Urn), **New Carthage** (Wall/Punic Quarter), **Hannibal's Route** (Traversette micro-morphology).
*   **Modern Reception:** 
    *   **Napoleon:** "Hannibal is the greatest captain."
    *   **Schlieffen:** Cannae = Envelopment doctrine.
    *   **Liddell Hart:** *Strategy* (Indirect approach - Fabian/Scipio).
    *   **Pop Culture:** *Cabiria* (1914), *Hannibal* (1959), *Carthage* (2006), *Rome: Total War*, *Civilization* series. **Elephants + Alps** = Iconic imagery.

## 12. RESEARCH GAPS & DEBATES (For Documentary Nuance)
1.  **Alps Route:** **Col de la Traversette** (Mahaney et al. 2016 - microbial soil evidence) vs **Col de Clapier / Montgenèvre** (Traditional). Visuals differ vastly (Glacial vs Forested).
2.  **Cannae Numbers:** Polybius (70k dead) vs Livy (50k). **Archaeology** (mass graves found 1900s/2000s) supports high numbers but not 70k single pit.
3.  **Tophet Sacrifice:** **Child Sacrifice** (Classical sources, Urn contents - perinates + animals) vs **Natural Infant Burial** (High mortality, distinct cemetery). **Consensus leaning:** Ritual sacrifice occurred, scale debated.
4.  **Roman Manpower:** How did Rome absorb Cannae losses? **P. Cornelius Scipio (Asina)** raised 2 legions *privately*? **Slave legions (Volones)**? **Lowering property qualification**?
5.  **Carthaginian Agency:** Were they "passive victims"? **Hannibal's strategy** was offensive. **Barcid Spain** was imperial project. **Massinissa's aggression** provoked 3rd War.
6.  **"Delenda Est":** Cato's obsession vs. **Scipio Nasica's opposition** ("Carthage keeps Rome vigilant"). **Scipio Aemilianus** weeping at destruction (Polybius) - **Tragedy of Empire**.
