# The Protestant Reformation: Comprehensive Historical Research Dossier

---

## I. BACKGROUND: THE PRE-REFORMATION WORLD (c. 1300–1517)

### A. Historical Context: The Crisis of the Late Middle Ages
*   **The Papal Schism (1378–1417):** Two (later three) rival popes claimed authority (Rome vs. Avignon), severely damaging papal prestige and the concept of a unified Christendom.
*   **Conciliarism:** The theory that a General Council of the Church held supreme authority over the Pope. Councils of Constance (1414–1418) and Basel (1431–1449) attempted reform but ultimately failed to institutionalize conciliar authority; Pope Pius II condemned appeals to councils in *Execrabilis* (1460).
*   **The Renaissance & Humanism:** Revival of classical learning (*ad fontes* – "to the sources"). Emphasis on textual criticism, philology, and return to original Hebrew/Greek scriptures and Church Fathers (Patristics) rather than medieval Scholastic commentaries.
    *   **Key Figure:** **Erasmus of Rotterdam** (1466–1536). Published *Novum Instrumentum omne* (1516), a critical Greek New Testament with Latin translation and annotations exposing Vulgate errors. Laid the intellectual groundwork for "Evangelical" reform.
*   **Printing Press (c. 1440):** Johannes Gutenberg’s movable type. By 1500, ~20 million books printed. Enabled rapid, cheap dissemination of ideas (pamphlets, vernacular Bibles, polemics) beyond clerical control. First "viral" media event in history.

### B. Political Situation: Fragmented Sovereignty
*   **Holy Roman Empire (HRE):** Elective monarchy; Emperor elected by 7 Prince-Electors. Real power lay with ~300 territorial princes, imperial knights, and ~50 Free Imperial Cities. No centralized bureaucracy or standing army. Emperor **Charles V** (r. 1519–1556) ruled vast Habsburg lands (Spain, Netherlands, Austria, Naples, New World) but faced France, Ottomans, and internal German particularism.
*   **Rise of Nation-States:** France, England, Spain, Sweden, Denmark centralizing power. Monarchs sought control over national churches (Gallicanism in England/France) to access wealth and patronage.
*   **Italian Wars (1494–1559):** Habsburg-Valois conflict fought largely on Italian soil. Distracted the Papacy and Emperor Charles V from early German reform movement.

### C. Economic Situation
*   **Commercial Revolution:** Rise of capitalism, banking (Fuggers, Welsers), joint-stock companies. Shift from feudal dues to money rents.
*   **Papal Taxation:** Annates (first year’s income of benefices), Peter’s Pence, dispensations, indulgences. Heavy flow of German silver/gold to Rome ("German money feeds the Roman curia").
*   **Fugger Banking House:** Financed Charles V’s election (1519); managed indulgence sales for Albrecht of Brandenburg (half to Rome, half to Fuggers to repay loan for pallium).
*   **Peasant Economic Grievances:** Rising rents, enclosure of commons, loss of customary rights, inflation (Price Revolution from New World silver), seigneurial dues.

### D. Social Situation
*   **Estates Society:** *Oratores* (clergy), *Bellatores* (nobility), *Laboratores* (peasants/urban poor). Increasing tension between estates.
*   **Urbanization:** Imperial Cities (Nuremberg, Augsburg, Strasbourg, Ulm) centers of literacy, trade, and reform. Guilds vs. Patricians.
*   **Clerical Abuses (Anti-clericalism):**
    *   *Simony:* Buying/selling offices.
    *   *Pluralism/Absenteeism:* Holding multiple benefices, never visiting.
    *   *Concubinage/Immoral lives:* Violating celibacy.
    *   *Ignorance:* Poorly educated parish priests unable to explain Latin liturgy.
*   **Popular Piety:** *Devotio Moderna* (imitation of Christ, interior piety), Rosary, pilgrimages, cult of saints/relics (Frederick the Wise had 19,000 relics), fear of Purgatory.

### E. Religious Situation: The "Long Reformation" Precursors
*   **John Wycliffe (England, d. 1384):** Denied transubstantiation, papal authority, advocated vernacular Bible (Lollards).
*   **Jan Hus (Bohemia, burned 1415):** Preached at Bethlehem Chapel, Prague. Influenced by Wycliffe. Condemned at Council of Constance despite safe conduct. Hussite Wars (1419–1434) established Utraquist Church (communion in both kinds) – first sustained break from Rome.
*   **Girolamo Savonarola (Florence, burned 1498):** Moral reformer, predicted divine scourge.
*   **Brethren of the Common Life:** Schools (Erasmus, Luther attended) emphasizing personal piety, scripture reading.
*   **State of Theology:** Late Medieval Nominalism (Via Moderna – Gabriel Biel, William of Ockham). God’s absolute power vs. ordained power. *Facere quod in se est* ("do what is in you") – humans can merit grace by natural effort. Luther’s "Anfechtungen" (spiritual trials) stemmed from inability to meet this standard.

## III. IMPORTANT PEOPLE

### A. The Magisterial Reformers (State-Supported)

#### **Martin Luther (1483–1546)**
*   **Role:** Augustinian friar, Professor of Theology (Wittenberg), Catalyst of Reformation.
*   **Goals:** Reform the Church based on Scripture alone (*Sola Scriptura*); Justification by Faith Alone (*Sola Fide*); Priesthood of All Believers; Vernacular worship/Bible.
*   **Actions:** 95 Theses (1517); Three Treatises of 1520 (*Address to Christian Nobility*, *Babylonian Captivity*, *Freedom of a Christian*); Diet of Worms (1521); Wartburg Translation (1522); German Mass (1526); Large/Small Catechisms (1529); Smalcald Articles (1537).
*   **Relationships:** Protected by **Frederick the Wise**; Collaborated with **Melanchthon** (systematizer); Broke with **Karlstadt** (radicalism) & **Zwingli** (Eucharist); Condemned **Peasants** & **Anabaptists**; Allied with **Princes** (John Frederick, Maurice).
*   **Influence:** Founder of Lutheranism; German language standardization; Theology of Cross; Two Kingdoms doctrine (Church/State separation of spheres).

#### **Philipp Melanchthon (1497–1560)**
*   **Role:** "Preceptor Germaniae"; Professor of Greek; Systematic theologian & diplomat.
*   **Goals:** Intellectual coherence for Reformation; Unity with Humanists & moderate Catholics; Educational reform.
*   **Actions:** *Loci Communes* (1521 – first Protestant dogmatics); **Augsburg Confession** (1530) & *Apology* (1531); Negotiator at Hagenau/Worms/Regensburg; *Saxon Confession* (1551).
*   **Relationships:** Luther’s closest colleague (tension over free will/law/gospel later); Friend of Erasmus (early); Leader of *Philippists* after Luther’s death; Opposed by *Gnesio-Lutherans* (Flacius).
*   **Influence:** Defined Lutheran orthodoxy; Architect of German public education system; Bridge to Reformed theology.

#### **Huldrych Zwingli (1484–1531)**
*   **Role:** People’s Priest at Grossmünster, Zurich; Founder of Swiss Reformed Tradition.
*   **Goals:** Restoration of primitive apostolic church; Removal of all non-biblical ceremony (images, music, organs, vestments, Lenten fast); Civic theocracy (City Council = Church governance).
*   **Actions:** Affair of the Sausages (1522); 67 Articles (1523); Disputations in Zurich/Baden/Berne; *De Vera et Falsa Religione* (1525); Marburg Colloquy (1529); Kappel Wars (died at Kappel am Albis).
*   **Relationships:** Independent of Luther (read Erasmus first); Debated Luther at Marburg (impasse on *est* vs. *significat*); Influenced **Bucer**, **Calvin**, **Bullinger**.
*   **Influence:** Reformed "Regulative Principle" of worship; Covenant theology; Church-State integration (Erastianism); Iconoclasm.

#### **John Calvin (1509–1564)**
*   **Role:** French humanist lawyer turned theologian; Organizer of Geneva; Architect of International Calvinism.
*   **Goals:** A well-ordered church glorifying God’s sovereignty; Discipline of the faithful; Missionary expansion.
*   **Actions:** *Institutes* (1536–1559 editions); Geneva Ordinances (1541) – 4 offices (Pastor, Doctor, Elder, Deacon); Consistory (moral police); Academy (1559); Missionary training (France, Netherlands, Scotland, Poland, Brazil).
*   **Relationships:** Mentored by **Bucer** (Strasbourg); Succeeded by **Beza**; Corresponded with **Cranmer**, **Knox**, **Melanchthon**; Burned **Servetus** (1553) for anti-Trinitarianism.
*   **Influence:** Systematic theology (TULIP later); Presbyterian/Reformed polity; Protestant work ethic (Weber thesis); International network (Huguenots, Puritans, Dutch, Hungarian, Polish Brethren).

#### **Martin Bucer (1491–1551)**
*   **Role:** Strasbourg Reformer; Ecumenist; Mentor to Calvin.
*   **Goals:** Unity of all Protestants (Lutherans + Reformed); Pastoral care/Confirmation; Church discipline.
*   **Actions:** *Ziegenhain Order* (1539); Colloquies of Regensburg; *De Regno Christi* (for Edward VI); Died in Cambridge (buried in Great St Mary's, later exhumed/burned by Mary I, reburied 1560).
*   **Influence:** Bridge figure; Shaped Anglican ordinal (via Cranmer); Pioneer of Confirmation rite.

### B. Radical Reformers (Anabaptists / Spiritualists) – *Believers' Baptism, Separation of Church/State, Discipleship*

#### **Thomas Müntzer (c. 1489–1525)**
*   **Role:** Mystic, Apocalyptic preacher; Leader in Peasants' War.
*   **Theology:** *Inner Word* (Spirit) > Outer Word (Scripture); Imminent Kingdom of God via violence of the Elect.
*   **End:** Captured at Frankenhausen, tortured, beheaded.

#### **Menno Simons (1496–1561)**
*   **Role:** Former Catholic priest; Organizer of peaceful Anabaptists (Mennonites).
*   **Theology:** Non-resistance (pacifism); Separation from world; Believer's baptism; Community of goods (optional); Ban/Shunning (*Meidung*).
*   **Legacy:** Mennonites, Amish, Hutterites.

#### **Michael Servetus (1511–1553)**
*   **Role:** Spanish physician/theologian; Anti-Trinitarian.
*   **Actions:** *Christianismi Restitutio* (1553); Discovered pulmonary circulation.
*   **End:** Burned at stake in Geneva (Calvin's consent) – catalyst for debates on toleration (Castellio, *De Haereticis*).

### C. Catholic Reformers & Opponents

#### **Desiderius Erasmus (1466–1536)**
*   **Role:** Prince of Humanists; *Novum Instrumentum* (1516).
*   **Stance:** Criticized abuses (Julius Exclusus, *Praise of Folly*); Wanted internal reform, not schism; *De Libero Arbitrio* (1524) vs Luther *De Servo Arbitrio* (1525). "I am a citizen of the world."

#### **Cardinal Cajetan (Thomas de Vio, 1469–1534)**
*   **Role:** Papal Legate; Thomist theologian.
*   **Action:** Interrogated Luther at Augsburg (1518); Failed to secure recantation.

#### **Johann Eck (1486–1543)**
*   **Role:** Professor Ingolstadt; Luther’s academic nemesis.
*   **Action:** Leipzig Debate (1519); Drafted *Exsurge Domine*; Leading Catholic polemicist at Diets.

#### **Pope Leo X (r. 1513–1521, Giovanni de' Medici)**
*   **Role:** Renaissance Pope; "God has given us the papacy, let us enjoy it."
*   **Action:** Indulgence for St. Peter's; *Exsurge Domine*; Excommunication.

#### **Pope Paul III (r. 1534–1549, Alessandro Farnese)**
*   **Role:** Counter-Reformation Pope.
*   **Actions:** Convened **Council of Trent** (1545); Approved **Jesuits** (1540); *Index Librorum Prohibitorum* (1559); Roman Inquisition (1542).

#### **Ignatius of Loyola (1491–1556)**
*   **Role:** Founder **Society of Jesus (Jesuits)**.
*   **Spirituality:** *Spiritual Exercises*; Obedience to Pope; Education (schools); Missions (Xavier: India, Japan; Ricci: China).

#### **Charles V (1500–1558)**
*   **Role:** Holy Roman Emperor (1519–1556); King of Spain (Charles I).
*   **Goals:** Universal Monarchy; Defense of Catholic Unity; Containment of France/Ottomans.
*   **Actions:** Diet of Worms; Wars vs Francis I; Sack of Rome (1527); Diet of Augsburg (1530); Schmalkaldic War (1546–47); Abdication (1555–56).
*   **Constraint:** Needed German princes' taxes/troops vs Turks → could not enforce Edict of Worms fully.

### D. Political Rulers (The "Magisterial" Protectorate)

*   **Frederick the Wise (Elector of Saxony, d. 1525):** Protected Luther; Founder University of Wittenberg; Relic collector; Secured Luther's hearing at Worms.
*   **John the Steadfast (Elector, 1525–1532):** Signed Protest at Speyer (1529); Leader Schmalkaldic League.
*   **John Frederick I (Elector, 1532–1547/54):** Captured at Mühlberg; Lost Electorate to Maurice; "Confessor of the Faith."
*   **Maurice of Saxony (1521–1553):** Albertine line; Initially allied Charles V (gained Ernestine Electorate 1547); Switched sides 1552 (Treaty of Chambord with Henry II France) → Peace of Passau/Augsburg. *Archetype of Realpolitik.*
*   **Philip of Hesse (1504–1567):** Political leader of League; Bigamist (Luther/Melanchthon advised secretly); Captured 1547.
*   **Henry VIII (England, r. 1509–1547):** *Defender of the Faith* (vs Luther); Break with Rome (1534) for Annulment/Succession; Dissolution of Monasteries (wealth); Six Articles (1539) – Catholic theology, Papal supremacy denied.
*   **Edward VI (r. 1547–1553):** Protestant regency (Somerset, Northumberland); *Book of Common Prayer* (1549, 1552); 42 Articles.
*   **Mary I (r. 1553–1558):** Catholic restoration; Marian Exiles; ~280 burned (Foxe's *Book of Martyrs*).
*   **Elizabeth I (r. 1558–1603):** *Via Media*; Act of Supremacy/Uniformity (1559); 39 Articles (1563); *Settlement*.
*   **Francis I (France, r. 1515–1547):** Allied with Ottomans/Protestants vs Charles V; Persecuted Huguenots (Affair of Placards 1534).
*   **Henry II (France, r. 1547–1559):** Continued Habsburg encirclement; Treaty of Chambord (1552) with Maurice.
*   **Gustav Vasa (Sweden, r. 1523–1560):** Broke with Rome (Västerås 1527); State Lutheranism; Confiscated church wealth.
*   **Christian III (Denmark/Norway, r. 1534–1559):** Civil War (Count's Feast); Imposed Reformation (1536); University of Copenhagen.

## IV. LOCATIONS

### A. Holy Roman Empire (The Epicenter)
*   **Wittenberg (Electoral Saxony):** University (Leucorea), Castle Church (Schlosskirche - Theses door), Stadtkirche (Luther's pulpit), Lutherhaus (Black Cloister), Melanchthonhaus. *Visual: Cranach altarpieces, printing presses (Lotther, Rhau), Elbe river.*
*   **Worms:** Bishop's Palace (Diet 1521), Luther Monument (largest Reformation monument), Rhine River.
*   **Wartburg Castle (Eisenach):** Luther's "Patmos" (1521–22); Lutherstube (study); Eisenach landscape (Thuringian Forest).
*   **Augsburg:** Town Hall (Goldener Saal - Diet 1530/1555), Fuggerei (social housing), St. Anne's Church (Luther stayed with Staupitz), Perlach Tower.
*   **Speyer:** Imperial Cathedral (burial of Emperors), Diet Hall (Protestation 1529), Memorial Church (Gedächtniskirche - built 1900s).
*   **Mühlberg (Elbe):** Battlefield 1547 (Charles V vs Schmalkaldic League).
*   **Nuremberg:** Imperial City; Early reform (Osiander); *Nuremberg Religious Peace* (1532); Albrecht Dürer house; Imperial Castle.
*   **Strasbourg (Free Imperial City):** Strasbourg Cathedral (astronomical clock), Bucer/Calvin ministry; *First Protestant hymnbook*; Printing hub (Grüninger, Knobloch).

*   **Magdeburg:** "Chancery of God" (Fortress city, resisted Interim 1550–51); Cathedral.
*   **Heidelberg:** University; Heidelberg Disputation (1518); Heidelberg Catechism (1563 - Calvinist); Castle ruins.

*   **Zurich:** Grossmünster (Zwingli's church - stripped interior), Rathaus (City Council), Zwinglihaus, Lake Zurich.
*   **Geneva:** St. Pierre Cathedral (Calvin's chair), Auditoire (Calvin's academy), Reformation Wall (Parc des Bastions - statues of Calvin, Farel, Beza, Knox), Rue de Calvin, International Museum of Reformation.
*   **Bern:** Münster (Cathedral), Bern Disputation (1528) – decisive for Swiss Reformed.
*   **Basel:** Erasmus' deathbed; Printing (Froben - Erasmus, Luther); Council of Basel (1431) site; University.

### C. Other Key Locations
*   **Rome:** St. Peter's Basilica (rebuilding funded by indulgences), Vatican (Sistine Chapel, Raphael Rooms), Castel Sant'Angelo (Sack of Rome 1527), Lateran Palace.
*   **Trent (Trento), Italy:** Cathedral of San Vigilio (Council sessions 1545-47, 1551-52, 1562-63), Buonconsiglio Castle.
*   **London:** Westminster Abbey (Parliament), Tower of London (More, Fisher, Cranmer), St. Paul's Cross (Paul's Churchyard - preaching), Lambeth Palace (Archbishop), Hampton Court.
*   **Canterbury:** Cathedral (Archiepiscopal see), Becket's shrine (destroyed 1538).
*   **Edinburgh:** St. Giles' Cathedral (Knox's pulpit), Parliament Hall, John Knox House (Royal Mile).
*   **Paris:** Sorbonne (Theology faculty), Parlement de Paris (burned heretics), Louvre (Royal palace), St. Bartholomew's Day Massacre sites.
*   **Antwerp / Ghent / Brussels:** Commercial centers; Printing (Plantin-Moretus Museum Antwerp); Margaret of Austria's court (Mechelen); *Iconoclasm (Beeldenstorm) 1566 sites*.
*   **Västerås, Sweden:** Diet of Västerås (1527) – Swedish Reformation.
*   **Copenhagen:** Church of Our Lady (Danish Reformation).

## V. MAJOR EVENTS: DETAILED ANALYSIS

### 1. The Indulgence Controversy & 95 Theses (1517)
*   **What:** Johann Tetzel (Dominican) preaching plenary indulgence for St. Peter's Basilica in Brandenburg (Jüterbog). Slogan: *"As soon as the coin in the coffer rings, the soul from purgatory springs."* Albrecht of Brandenburg (Archbishop Mainz/Magdeburg) split proceeds with Rome (Fuggers repaid).
*   **Why:** Luther's pastoral concern: Indulgences gave false security, neglected true repentance (*poenitentia*), exploited poor. Theological objection: Treasury of Merit vs. Merit of Christ.
*   **Who:** Luther, Tetzel, Albrecht, Leo X, Cajetan, Eck.
*   **Immediate:** Theses spread via print (Latin/German) in weeks. Tetzel counter-theses. Rome opens heresy trial.
*   **Long-term:** Irreparable breach. Shift from academic dispute to public movement.

### 2. The Leipzig Debate (June–July 1519)
*   **What:** Eck vs. Karlstadt/Luther. Topic: Papal Primacy & Infallibility.
*   **Why:** Eck wanted to brand Luther a Hussite (heretic).
*   **Outcome:** Luther forced to deny authority of Council of Constance (which burned Hus) and Papal *de jure divino* primacy. "A simple layman armed with Scripture is to be believed above a pope or council without it."
*   **Consequence:** Luther excommunicated in spirit; defined *Sola Scriptura* over *Sacerdotal Authority*.

### 3. The Three Treatises of 1520 (Manifesto of Reformation)
*   **_To the Christian Nobility of the German Nation_:** Laity appeal to Emperor/Princes to reform Church (externally). Attack *Three Walls*: 1. Spiritual > Temporal power (Priesthood of all believers). 2. Pope sole interpreter of Scripture. 3. Only Pope can call Council. **Secular magistrate duty to reform.**
*   **_The Babylonian Captivity of the Church_:** Sacramental theology. Only 3 Sacraments (Baptism, Eucharist, Penance) have Word + Sign + Promise. Rejects: Transubstantiation (prefers Real Presence/Sacramental Union), Mass as Sacrifice, Withholding Cup, Private Masses, Vows, Extreme Unction, Holy Orders (as sacrament), Marriage (civil).
*   **_The Freedom of a Christian_:** Spiritual inner man. "A Christian is a perfectly free lord of all, subject to none. A Christian is a perfectly dutiful servant of all, subject to all." Justification by Faith -> Love serves neighbor.

### 4. Diet of Worms (April 1521)
*   **What:** Imperial trial. Charles V pressures Luther to recant.
*   **Luther's Statement:** "Unless I am convinced by Scripture and plain reason... my conscience is captive to the Word of God. I cannot and will not recant... Here I stand, I can do no other. God help me. Amen." (The "Here I stand" likely apocryphal but captures essence).
*   **Consequence:** Edict of Worms (Outlaw). Frederick the Wise stages "kidnapping" to Wartburg.

### 5. The Wartburg Translation (1521–1522)
*   **What:** Luther translates Greek NT (Erasmus 2nd ed.) into German in 11 weeks.
*   **Significance:** *Septbibel* (Sept 1522). 3,000 copies sold instantly. Standardized High German (Chancery Saxon). "Luther German" becomes *Schriftdeutsch*. Vernacular Scripture = *Norma Normans*.

### 6. The German Peasants' War (1524–1525)
*   **Causes:** Socio-economic oppression (serfdom, tithes, fees, enclosure) + Religious legitimation ("Christian freedom," "Godly Law" vs. Roman Law). *Twelve Articles* (Memmingen) – biblical justification for demands.
*   **Luther's Role:** *Admonition to Peace* (blames lords); *Against the Murderous, Thieving Hordes of Peasants* (May 1525) – demands princes "smite, strangle, stab" rebels. Two Kingdoms: Spiritual freedom ≠ Political license.
*   **Battles:** Leipheim, Böblingen, **Frankenhausen** (Müntzer defeated, 6,000+ killed), Königshofen.
*   **Casualties:** ~70,000–100,000 peasants killed.
*   **Consequence:** Princes consolidated power; Reformation tied to magistral authority; Radicals (Anabaptists) discredited/purged; Peasants lost trust in Luther.

### 7. The Marburg Colloquy (Oct 1–4, 1529)
*   **Who:** Luther, Melanchthon, Jonas, Brenz vs. Zwingli, Oecolampadius, Bucer, Hedio.
*   **Agreement:** 14/15 Articles (Trinity, Christ, Justification, Baptism, State, etc.).
*   **Dispute:** **Eucharist**.
    *   *Luther:* *Hoc est corpus meum* (Literal *est*). Ubiquity of Christ's body (Communication of Attributes). Manducatio indignorum (unworthy eat body).
    *   *Zwingli:* *Est = Significat* (Memorial/Symbolic). Christ's body in heaven (Ascension). "Finitum non capax infiniti." Spiritual presence via Faith/Holy Spirit.
*   **Result:** No fellowship. *Schism within Reformation.* Political: Landgrave Philip's defensive league failed.

### 8. The Augsburg Confession & Confutatio (June 1530)
*   **What:** Melanchthon presents *Confessio Augustana* (28 Articles: 21 Doctrine, 7 Abuses corrected) to Charles V. Irénic tone.
*   **Catholic Response:** *Confutatio* (refutation) drafted by Eck, Faber, Cochlaeus.
*   **Protestant Reply:** *Apology of the Augsburg Confession* (Melanchthon) – major theological treatise.
*   **Significance:** Primary Lutheran Confession. Defines "Evangelical" identity.

### 9. The Schmalkaldic League & War (1531–1547)
*   **Formation:** Defensive alliance (Elector John, Philip Hesse, cities). *Not* initially theological offensive.
*   **War (1546–47):** Charles V (victorious over France at Crepy) turns on League. Maurice of Saxony invades Ernestine lands.
*   **Battle of Mühlberg (Apr 24, 1547):** Imperial cavalry (Alba) routs League. John Frederick captured (sentenced death, commuted). Philip Hesse surrenders.
*   **Aftermath:** *Augsburg Interim* (1548) – Catholic rites restored, clergy marriage/cup allowed temporarily. Rejected by hardliners (Magdeburg "God's Chancery").

### 10. The Council of Trent (1545–1563) – Catholic Counter-Reformation
*   **Sessions:** I (1545–47, Trent), II (1551–52, Trent), III (1562–63, Trent). Moved to Bologna briefly (plague).
*   **Dogmatic Decrees:**
    *   Scripture + Tradition (Vulgate authentic).
    *   Original Sin / Justification (Faith + Works; *sine fide* impossible, *sine operibus* not justified). *Anathema on Sola Fide.*
    *   7 Sacraments (all instituted by Christ). Transubstantiation defined. Mass = Propitiatory Sacrifice.
    *   Purgatory, Saints, Relics, Images, Indulgences affirmed (abuses corrected).
*   **Disciplinary Decrees:**
    *   Seminary system (training priests).
    *   Episcopal Residence obligation.
    *   Breviary/Missal standardization (Tridentine Mass).
    *   Index of Forbidden Books (1559).
    *   Catechism (1566).

### 11. The Peace of Augsburg (Sep 25, 1555)
*   **Negotiators:** Ferdinand I (Charles's brother) & Imperial Estates.
*   **Terms:**
    1.  *Cuius regio, eius religio*: Prince determines religion (Lutheran or Catholic) of territory.
    2.  Subjects must conform or emigrate (*jus emigrandi* - sell property, leave).
    3.  *Reservatum Ecclesiasticum*: If an ecclesiastical prince (bishop/abbot) converts, he loses office/benefice (prevents secularization of church lands).
    4.  *Declaratio Ferdinandea*: Secret exemption for some imperial knights/cities.
*   **Excluded:** Calvinists (Zwinglians), Anabaptists, Anti-Trinitarians.
*   **Significance:** Legalized religious division in HRE. Ended *confessional* wars in Germany for 60 years. Framework for Westphalia.

### 12. The English Reformation (Political/Dynastic Driver)
*   **1527–33:** "King's Great Matter" (Annulment Catherine of Aragon). Wolsey fails. Henry asserts *Praemunire* vs Clergy.
*   **1534:** Act of Supremacy (King = Supreme Head). Treasons Act. Dissolution of Monasteries (1536–40) – massive wealth transfer to gentry.
*   **1549/1552:** Book of Common Prayer (Cranmer) – Vernacular liturgy, Protestant theology (esp. 1552).
*   **1553–58:** Marian Reaction (Pole, Reconciliation with Rome, Burnings).
*   **1559:** Elizabethan Settlement. *Act of Supremacy* (Governor), *Act of Uniformity* (1552 BCP modified), *39 Articles* (1563). *Via Media*: Catholic structure (Episcopacy, Vestments, Calendar) + Reformed Theology (Predestination, Spiritual Presence).

### 13. The French Wars of Religion (1562–1598)
*   **Factions:** Catholics (Guise, Crown) vs Huguenots (Bourbon, Coligny, Navarre).
*   **Trigger:** Massacre of Vassy (1562).
*   **Key Events:** Colloquy of Poissy (1561 - failed); St. Bartholomew's Day (Aug 24, 1572) – ~3,000 Paris, ~10,000+ provinces; War of Three Henries (1585–89); Henry IV (Navarre) converts ("Paris is worth a mass") 1593.
*   **Edict of Nantes (1598):** Limited toleration (worship in designated towns, garrisons). Revoked 1685 (Louis XIV).

### 14. The Dutch Revolt (1566–1648)
*   **Cause:** Spanish centralization (Philip II), Inquisition, Taxation (Tenth Penny), *Beeldenstorm* (Iconoclastic Fury Aug 1566).
*   **Leaders:** William of Orange (William the Silent), Sea Beggars (Geuzen).
*   **Course:** Alba's Council of Blood (1567); Leiden Siege (1574); Union of Utrecht (1579); Act of Abjuration (1581 - deposing Philip); Assassination of William (1584); Maurice of Nassau reforms army; Twelve Years' Truce (1609); Final recognition 1648.

## VI. MILITARY ASPECTS

### A. Major Conflicts
1.  **Knights' Revolt (1522–23):** Franz von Sickingen (Imperial Knight) vs. Archbishop of Trier/Elector Palatine. Artillery destroys castles (Nanstein). End of knights as political force.
2.  **Peasants' War (1524–25):** Peasant bands (*Haufen*) – wagon forts (*Wagenburg*), artillery captured, poor coordination. Princes: Professional Landsknechte, heavy cavalry, artillery. **Frankenhausen** decisive.
3.  **Kappel Wars (1529, 1531):** Zurich vs. Catholic Forest Cantons. 1529: Bloodless (Milk Soup). 1531: Zwingli killed as chaplain.
4.  **Schmalkaldic War (1546–47):** Charles V (Spanish tercios, Italian condottieri, German landsknechte) vs. League (Saxon/Hessian levies). **Mühlberg**: Surprise crossing Elbe; Spanish cavalry charge.
5.  **Princes' Revolt (1552):** Maurice surprises Charles at Innsbruck (Charles flees gout-ridden on litter).
6.  **French Wars of Religion:** Cavalry (Gendarmes) vs. Huguenot pistoliers (Reiters); Siege warfare (La Rochelle, Paris).
7.  **Dutch Revolt:** Naval (Sea Beggars), Siege (Leiden, Antwerp - Farnese), Maurice's "New Model" infantry (countermarch volley fire), Trace Italienne fortresses.

### B. Military Technology & Organization
*   **Landsknechte:** Pike squares (Swiss model), Doppelsöldner (Zweihänder/halberds), Arquebusiers. Colorful slashed clothing (visual icon).
*   **Spanish Tercios:** Pike + Shot integration (mixed formation). Superior discipline.
*   **Artillery:** Bronze culverins, falconets. Siege warfare dominant (Trace Italienne - low, thick, bastioned forts).
*   **Reiters (German Cavalry):** Pistols (wheel-lock), caracole tactic (advance, fire, wheel away). Used by both sides.
*   **Naval:** Galleons (Atlantic), Galleys (Med). Dutch *fluyt* (merchant efficiency). Sea Beggars = privateers/calvinist irregulars.

## VII. POLITICS & DIPLOMACY

### A. Imperial Constitution & Law
*   **Golden Bull (1356):** Electoral procedure.
*   **Reichskammergericht (1495):** Imperial Supreme Court (religious cases major docket).
*   **Reichsregiment (1500/1521):** Failed attempt at central government.
*   **Kreis (Circles):** 10 Imperial Circles (administrative/military/tax districts) – used for executing Edict of Worms / Interim / Peace enforcement.

### B. Key Treaties & Alliances
*   **Treaty of Cambrai (1529):** "Ladies' Peace" (Margaret of Austria / Louise of Savoy) – France/Habsburg truce; Charles free for Germany.
*   **Treaty of Nuremberg (1532):** Charles grants religious peace until Council; Lutherans aid vs Turks.
*   **Schmalkaldic League (1531):** Military alliance. Constitution: Council, War Chest, Troops.
*   **Treaty of Chambord (1552):** Maurice (Protestant) allies Henry II (Catholic France) vs Charles V. Metz, Toul, Verdun to France.
*   **Peace of Passau (1552):** Precursor to Augsburg; Maurice/Charles truce.
*   **Peace of Cateau-Cambrésis (1559):** Ends Habsburg-Valois wars; France keeps Metz/Toul/Verdun; Spain dominant in Italy. Frees resources for religious war.

### C. Papal Diplomacy
*   **Legates:** Cajetan (1518), Aleander (Worms), Contarini (Regensburg 1541 - nearly agreed on Justification *duplex iustitia*, rejected by both Rome & Luther).
*   **Concordat of Bologna (1516):** Leo X / Francis I. King nominates bishops/abbots -> Gallican Church autonomy. Explains French lack of Reformation prince initially.

## VIII. SOCIETY, CULTURE & DAILY LIFE

### A. Education & Literacy
*   **Luther:** *To the Councilmen of All Cities in Germany* (1524) – Establish schools for boys/girls. *Deutsche Messe* (1526) – Catechism preaching.
*   **Melanchthon:** Saxon School Plan (1528); *Gymnasium* curriculum (Latin, Greek, Hebrew, Dialectic, Rhetoric).
*   **Geneva:** Academy (1559) – Model for Reformed colleges (Herborn, Heidelberg, Leiden, Oxford/Puritan, Harvard).
*   **Jesuits:** *Ratio Studiorum* (1599) – Global standardized curriculum (Humanities + Scholasticism).
*   **Literacy Rates:** Higher in Protestant areas (need to read Bible). ~5-10% men (urban) early 16th C; rising.

### B. Printing & Propaganda (The First Media War)
*   **Volume:** 1518–1525: ~2,100 Protestant pamphlets (Luther ~500). Millions of copies.
*   **Form:** *Flugschriften* (pamphlets, 8-16 pages), Woodcuts (Cranach: *Papst-Esel*, *Mönch-Kalb*, *Passional Christi und Antichristi*), Cartoons, Satire (Hans Sachs), Hymns (Luther: *Ein feste Burg* – "Marseillaise of Reformation").
*   **Language:** German (vernacular) vs Latin (clerical). Luther's NT: 200,000+ copies before death.
*   **Censorship:** Imperial (ineffective), Episcopal, *Index Librorum Prohibitorum* (1559).

### C. Religious Practice & Ritual Change
*   **Lutheran:** *Deutsche Messe* (1526) – Sermon central, Congregational singing (Chorales), Communion in both kinds, Private Confession retained (voluntary), Images allowed (adiaphora), Vestments simplified (chasuble optional).
*   **Reformed (Zwingli/Calvin):** *Regulative Principle* – Only what Scripture commands. No images (Iconoclasm), No organs/music (initially - Psalters only: Goudimel/Bourgeois), No vestments (Genevan gown), No church calendar (only Sunday), Lord's Supper quarterly (seated at tables), Discipline (Consistory).
*   **Anglican:** *Book of Common Prayer* – Uniform liturgy in English. Matins/Evensong, Litany, Communion. Vestments (Surplice/Cope), Kneeling for Communion (Black Rubric debate), Ornaments Rubric (retain 1549 ornaments).
*   **Catholic (Tridentine):** Standardized Missal (1570), Breviary, Catechism. Latin retained. Seminary training. Baroque art/architecture (emotional engagement). Jesuits: Preaching, Missions, Education.

### D. Marriage, Family & Gender
*   **Clerical Marriage:** Luther marries **Katharina von Bora** (1525) – former nun. Model parsonage (hospitality, boarding students, brewery, farm). Pastors = household heads.
*   **Divorce:** Permitted by Luther (adultery, desertion, impotence) – civil matter. Calvin/Reformed stricter.
*   **Women:** Argula von Grumbach (pamphleteer), Katharina Schütz Zell (Strasbourg - "Mother of the Church"), Olympia Morata (scholar), Jeanne d'Albret (Queen of Navarre - Huguenot leader). *But:* Patriarchal structure reinforced; Convents closed (option removed for women).

### E. Poor Relief & Social Welfare
*   **Protestant:** *Common Chest* (Gemeine Kasten) – Secularized church assets fund poor, schools, loans. Begging banned. YMCA precursors (Calvin's *Hôpital Général* Geneva).
*   **Catholic:** Confraternities, Hospitals (Brothers of St. John of God), Vincent de Paul (later).

## X. INTERESTING VISUAL DETAILS (For Documentary Imagery Generation)

### A. Clothing & Appearance
*   **Luther:** Augustinian habit (black, leather belt) -> Doctor's gown (fur-trimmed, biretta). Later: Plain black scholar's robe. **Katharina von Bora:** Wimple, modest matron dress, keys at waist.
*   **Landsknechte:** *Puff-and-slash* (Pluderhosen), wide-brimmed hats with ostrich feathers, *Zweihänder* swords, codpieces. **Visual Icon of era.**
*   **Reiters:** Black leather armor (buff coat), wheel-lock pistols in saddle holsters, "caracole" formation.
*   **Spanish Tercios:** Steel morion helmets (comb), white ruffs emerging, pikes (18ft), arquebuses.
*   **Clergy (Catholic):** Cassock, rochet, mozzetta, biretta (Cardinals: Red; Bishops: Purple). **Tridentine:** Roman Collar standardized later.
*   **Reformed Ministers:** **Genevan Gown** (black academic robe, preaching tabs/bands) – *Origin of modern Protestant ministerial dress.*
*   **Nuns/Monks:** Habits (White/Black/Brown) – Dissolution scenes: shedding habits for secular clothes.
*   **Peasants:** Rough tunics, wooden clogs, *Bundschuh* (tied shoe) flag symbol.
*   **Urban Elite:** Ruffs (growing), doublets, slashed sleeves, dark velvets/silks (sumptuary laws).

### B. Architecture & Buildings
*   **Wittenberg Schlosskirche:** Bronze Theses Doors (1858 replacement - original wooden burned 1760), Cranach Altar (Law & Gospel), Luther's Pulpit (Stadtkirche).
*   **Wartburg Castle:** Luther's Room (Sparrowhawk ink stain legend), Medieval Great Hall, Landgrave's chambers.
*   **Geneva St. Pierre:** Stripped interior (columns remain), Calvin's Chair (triangular), Auditoire (lecture hall).
*   **Trent Cathedral:** Renaissance facade, Council Chamber (frescoes of sessions).

*   **Protestant Churches:** *Predigtkirche* (Preaching Church) – Pulpit central, Altar/Table secondary, Galleries for seating, Clear glass (light = Word). E.g., St. Mary's Lübeck, St. Sebaldus Nuremberg.
*   **Fuggerei (Augsburg):** World's oldest social housing (1521). Gates locked at night. Rent: 1 Rhenish Gulden/year + 3 prayers.

### C. Art & Iconography
*   **Lucas Cranach the Elder (Wittenberg):** *Law and Gospel* (diptych), *Passional Christi und Antichristi* (woodcut series: Pope vs Christ), Portraits of Luther/Katie/Princes. **Workshop production.**

## XI. CONSEQUENCES & LEGACY

### A. Immediate Consequences (16th Century)
1.  **Permanent Schism:** Western Christendom fractured into Catholic, Lutheran, Reformed, Anglican, Anabaptist, Anti-Trinitarian branches.
2.  **Confessionalization:** *Confessional States* – Rulers impose uniform doctrine (Cuius regio). Social discipline (visitations, consistories, confession certificates). "Confessionalization Thesis" (Schilling, Reinhard).
3.  **Religious Wars:** 150 years of conflict (Schmalkaldic, French, Dutch, Thirty Years' War). Militarization of society.
4.  **Redistribution of Wealth:** Massive transfer of Church land/wealth to laity (Monasteries, Chapels, Chantries). Foundation of modern European aristocracy/gentry wealth.
5.  **Education Boom:** Vernacular schools, Universities (Marburg, Königsberg, Jena, Helmstedt, Geneva, Leiden, Edinburgh, Harvard). Literacy rise.
6.  **Print Culture & Public Sphere:** Pamphlets, Newspapers (early *Relationen*), Censorship mechanisms. Birth of modern media.
7.  **Catholic Renewal (Counter-Reformation):** Trent, Jesuits, Seminaries, Baroque, Missions (Global Catholicism), Inquisition/Index. *Not just reaction; internal reform.*

### B. Long-Term Historical Impact (17th Century – Modern)
1.  **Modern Nation-State:** *Cuius regio* -> Sovereignty (Westphalia 1648). State controls religion -> State controls law/education/welfare. Secularization of politics.
2.  **Individualism & Conscience:** *Priesthood of All Believers* -> Right/ Duty to interpret Scripture -> Freedom of Conscience (eventually) -> Liberal Democracy/Human Rights trajectory. *Anabaptist separation of Church/State* -> US First Amendment model.
3.  **Capitalism & Work Ethic:** **Weber Thesis** (Protestant Ethic): Calvinist *Beruf* (Calling), Worldly Asceticism, Predestination anxiety -> Rational capital accumulation. *Debated but influential.*
4.  **Science & Epistemology:** *Sola Scriptura* -> Literalism/Historicity -> Conflict with Science (Galileo) BUT also *Ad Fontes* -> Empirical observation, challenge to Aristotelian authority. Puritan/Royal Society links (Merton Thesis).
5.  **Global Missions & Colonialism:** Catholic (Jesuits/Franciscans - Americas, Asia) vs Protestant (Calvinist/Dutch/British - North America, Africa, Asia). Translation of Bible into vernaculars globally (Linguistics).
6.  **Women's Roles:** Closure of Convents (loss of female religious authority/autonomy) vs. Pastors' Wives (new public role: Katharina von Bora, Katharina Schütz Zell, Argula von Grumbach). Long term: Literacy for girls (catechism), companionate marriage ideal.
7.  **Ecumenism (20th-21st C):** *Joint Declaration on the Doctrine of Justification* (1999 - Lutheran/Catholic). World Council of Churches. Ongoing dialogues (Eucharist, Ministry, Papacy).

### C. Legacy Summary
*   **Theological:** Justification by Faith; Authority of Scripture; Sacramental Theology (Real Presence vs Memorial); Predestination vs Free Will; Church Polity (Episcopal, Presbyterian, Congregational).
*   **Cultural:** Hymnody (Chorales, Psalters, Wesley); Vernacular Literature (Luther Bible, KJV, Statenvertaling); Education systems; Work ethic.
*   **Political:** Religious Toleration (eventually); Separation of Church/State; Resistance Theory (Vindiciae Contra Tyrannos, Knox, Buchanan -> Locke); National Churches.
*   **Demographic:** Migration (Huguenots, Salzburgers, Puritans, Mennonites) shaping Americas, Prussia, South Africa, Russia.
