## I. BACKGROUND: ROOTS AND CAUSES (1919–1939)

### Historical Context: The Post-WWI Order
- **Treaty of Versailles (1919):** Imposed war guilt (Article 231), reparations (132 billion gold marks), territorial losses (Alsace-Lorraine, colonies, Polish Corridor), military restrictions (100k army, no air force, limited navy) on Germany. Created deep resentment ("Stab-in-the-back myth").
- **League of Nations:** Established for collective security; weakened by US non-membership, lack of enforcement mechanism, and requirement for unanimity.
- **Washington Naval Treaties (1921–22):** Limited capital ship ratios (5:5:3:1.75:1.75 - UK:US:Japan:France:Italy). Temporary stabilization but fueled Japanese naval resentment.

### Political Situation
- **Weimar Republic (Germany):** Unstable democracy; hyperinflation (1923), Great Depression (1929) destroyed middle-class savings; political polarization (Communists KPD vs. Nazis NSDAP vs. Social Democrats SPD). Rule by decree (Article 48) under Hindenburg after 1930.
- **Rise of Fascism/Totalitarianism:**
    - **Italy:** Mussolini appointed PM 1922; corporate state, suppression of opposition, Lateran Treaty (1929) with Vatican.
    - **Soviet Union:** Stalin consolidates power late 1920s; Five-Year Plans (forced industrialization), Collectivization (Holodomor 1932–33, ~3.5–5M Ukrainian deaths), Great Purge (1936–38).
    - **Japan:** Militarism ascendant; Kwantung Army autonomy; assassinations of moderates (1930s); "Gekokujō" (loyalty from below).
- **Appeasement Policy (UK/France):** Fear of bomber fleets ("The bomber will always get through" - Baldwin), guilt over Versailles harshness, anti-communism (view Hitler as bulwark vs USSR), economic weakness, US isolationism (Neutrality Acts 1935–37).

### Economic Situation
- **Great Depression (1929–1939):** Global GDP fell ~15%; trade collapsed (Smoot-Hawley Tariff). Unemployment: US 25%, Germany 30% (6M), UK 20%.
- **Autarky Policies:** Germany (Four Year Plan 1936 - Göring), Italy (Battle for Grain/Lira), Japan (Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere concept). Drive for self-sufficiency required territorial expansion for resources (oil, rubber, iron, food).

### Social Situation
- **Demographic Pressures:** Japan population ~73M (1940) on resource-poor islands; Germany ~69M (1939) seeking *Lebensraum*.
- **Ideological Indoctrination:** Hitler Youth (8M members 1939), Komsomol, Italian Balilla, Japanese *Seinendan*. State control of education/media.
- **Antisemitism/Racism:** Nuremberg Laws (1935) strip German Jews of citizenship; *Kristallnacht* (Nov 1938); Japanese racial superiority doctrine (*Yamato-damashii*); Italian Manifesto of Race (1938).

### Religious Situation
- **Germany:** *Kirchenkampf* (Church Struggle). Concordat with Vatican (1933) violated. Confessing Church (Niemöller, Bonhoeffer) vs. German Christians. Jehovah's Witnesses persecuted (refusal to swear oath).
- **USSR:** State atheism; Orthodox Church persecuted then partially rehabilitated (1943) for patriotism.
- **Japan:** State Shinto mandatory; Emperor as *Arahitogami* (living god). Christianity suppressed.

## III. IMPORTANT PEOPLE

### ALLIED LEADERS (THE "BIG THREE" + CHINA)

#### **Joseph Stalin (USSR)**
- **Role:** General Secretary CPSU, Chairman Council of Ministers, Supreme Commander-in-Chief.
- **Goals:** Survival of USSR, territorial security (buffer zone), spread communism, reclaim Tsarist losses (Poland, Baltics, Bessarabia), avoid two-front war until ready.
- **Actions:** Purges (weakened Red Army 1937); Molotov-Ribbentrop Pact (bought time); Order No. 227 "Not a Step Back"; Stavka micromanagement; Katyn Massacre (1940); Tehran/Yalta/Potsdam negotiations; Manchurian Offensive (1945).
- **Relationships:** Distrusted Churchill (imperialist), tolerated Roosevelt (needed Lend-Lease), feared Hitler. Molotov (loyal FM), Zhukov (favored Marshal), Beria (NKVD terror).
- **Influence:** Architect of Eastern Front victory; shaped post-war map of Europe; initiated Cold War tensions.

#### **Franklin D. Roosevelt (USA)**
- **Role:** President (1933–1945). Commander-in-Chief.
- **Goals:** Defeat Axis, establish UN/Woodrow Wilson's vision, "Four Freedoms," decolonization (anti-imperial), "Germany First," keep USSR in war, manage US public opinion/isolationism.
- **Actions:** Lend-Lease Act (Mar 1941); Atlantic Charter; Pearl Harbor leadership; Grand Strategy (Torch → Overlord); Manhattan Project authorization; Yalta concessions (UN veto, Far East); "Unconditional Surrender" (Casablanca).
- **Relationships:** Close personal rapport with Churchill ("Former Naval Person"); pragmatic partnership with Stalin; managed Marshall, King, Hopkins, Eisenhower.
- **Influence:** Arsenal of Democracy; global US hegemony architect; died before war's end/atomic use.

#### **Winston Churchill (UK)**
- **Role:** PM (1940–45, 1951–55). Minister of Defence.
- **Goals:** Survival of British Empire/Commonwealth, defeat Germany, maintain Mediterranean position, limit Soviet expansion, involve US fully.
- **Actions:** "Blood, toil, tears, sweat" speeches; Fight on alone (1940); Mediterranean strategy (soft underbelly); Bomber Command area offensive; Special Operations Executive (SOE); Tehran/Yalta/Potsdam; "Iron Curtain" speech (1946).
- **Relationships:** Deep friendship with Roosevelt; intense suspicion of Stalin; clashes with US Chiefs of Staff (Brooke vs Marshall/King); reliance on Alanbrooke (CIGS).
- **Influence:** Moral leadership 1940; strategic brake on premature Cross-Channel attack; post-war geopolitical visionary.

#### **Chiang Kai-shek (China)**
- **Role:** Chairman Nationalist Government, Supreme Commander National Revolutionary Army.
- **Goals:** Survive Japanese onslaught, unify China (defeat Communists), gain Great Power status, US support.
- **Actions:** Xian Incident (1936) forced United Front; Yellow River flood (1938, 500k+ dead); Chongqing capital; Stilwell/Burma conflicts; Cairo Conference; Corruption/inflation weakened regime.
- **Relationships:** Uneasy alliance with Mao (CCP); dependent on US (Stilwell, Chennault, Wedemeyer); Stalin signed treaty with him (1945) but backed Mao.
- **Influence:** Tied down ~1M Japanese troops; post-war collapse led to Communist victory (1949).

### AXIS LEADERS

#### **Adolf Hitler (Germany)**
- **Role:** Führer und Reichskanzler, Supreme Commander Wehrmacht (from 1938).
- **Goals:** *Lebensraum* in East (Ukr/Russia), destruction of "Judeo-Bolshevism", racial purity (Final Solution), reversal of Versailles, Germanic hegemony Europe.
- **Actions:** Rearmament; Rhineland, Anschluss, Munich, Prague; Blitzkrieg doctrine approval; Halt Order (Dunkirk); Directive 21 (Barbarossa); "No Retreat" orders; declared war on US; Valkyrie survival; Nero Decree (scorched earth).
- **Relationships:** Subservient: Göring (Luftwaffe, Four Year Plan), Goebbels (Propaganda), Himmler (SS/Police), Ribbentrop (Foreign Office), Keitel/Jodl (OKW "yes men"). Professional friction: Brauchitsch, Halder, Guderian, Manstein, Rommel.
- **Influence:** Central driver of war/genocide; strategic gambler; micromanagement caused disasters (Stalingrad, Normandy, Bulge).

#### **Benito Mussolini (Italy)**
- **Role:** Duce, Prime Minister, Minister of Foreign Affairs/War/Navy/Air.
- **Goals:** New Roman Empire (*Mare Nostrum*), Mediterranean dominance, Balkans/North Africa, prestige, population settlement.
- **Actions:** Corfu (1923), Abyssinia (1935), Spain (1936), Albania (1939), Pact of Steel, Greece (1940 disaster), USSR (CSIR/ARMIR), declaration on US.
- **Relationships:** Junior partner to Hitler (resented); Ciano (son-in-law, FM, executed 1944); Badoglio (successor); King Victor Emmanuel III (dismissed him).
- **Influence:** Strategic liability for Germany (Balkans delay Barbarossa, North Africa drain); Fascist template.

#### **Emperor Hirohito (Japan)**
- **Role:** Emperor (Shōwa), Supreme Commander (theoretical), Head of State Shinto.
- **Goals:** Preservation of *Kokutai* (National Polity/Imperial Institution), Asian hegemony, resources (oil), revision of unequal treaties.
- **Actions:** Sanctioned expansion (Manchuria, China, Southeast Asia); Imperial Conferences (Gozen Kaigi) ratified decisions; "Sacred Decision" to surrender (Aug 1945).
- **Relationships:** Tojo (PM/War Min), Kido (Lord Keeper Privy Seal), Konoe (moderate PM), Suzuki (surrender PM). Military factionalism (Control Faction vs Imperial Way).
- **Influence:** Symbol of unity; degree of responsibility debated (active vs passive); surrender authority essential.

#### **Hideki Tojo (Japan)**
- **Role:** Prime Minister (1941–44), War Minister, Chief of Staff Kwantung Army.
- **Goals:** Total victory in China, Southern Expansion (resources), military dominance over state.
- **Actions:** Pearl Harbor decision; "Total War" mobilization; resignation after Saipan fall (Jul 1944); tried/executed Class A war criminal.
- **Relationships:** Rivalry with Navy (Nagano, Yamamoto); Emperor's confidence (lost 1944).

## V. MAJOR EVENTS: DETAILED ANALYSIS

### 1. Invasion of Poland (Fall Weiss / Plan Zachód) – Sep–Oct 1939
- **What:** 1.5M German (Army Groups North/South) + 0.5M Soviet troops invade. Blitzkrieg: Panzer thrusts, Luftwaffe air supremacy (Stuka), encirclement (Kesselschlacht).
- **Why:** Hitler's *Lebensraum* start; test Western resolve; Secret Protocol with Stalin.
- **Who:** Germany (Brauchitsch, Rundstedt, Bock), USSR (Timoshenko, Zhukov), Poland (Rydz-Śmigły).
- **Immediate:** Poland partitioned. UK/France declare war but no offensive (Saar Offensive fails). Phony War begins.
- **Long-term:** *Einsatzgruppen* begin intelligentsia killings (Intelligenzaktion). Soviet deportations (1.5M Poles to Gulag). Katyn decision (1940).

### 2. Battle of France (Fall Gelb / Fall Rot) – May–Jun 1940
- **What:** Manstein Plan (Sichelschnitt): Army Group A (Rundstedt/Guderian) through Ardennes → Sedan → Channel Coast. Army Group B (Bock) feint Netherlands/Belgium. Encirclement of Allied armies in Belgium.
- **Why:** Knockout France before UK/US rearm; secure Western flank for Eastern war.
- **Who:** Germany 135 divs (2.5M), 2,500 tanks. Allies 144 divs (3M), 3,000+ tanks (better armor but dispersed).
- **Immediate:** Dunkirk evacuation (338k saved). Fall of Paris (Jun 14). Armistice Jun 22. Vichy France established. Italy gains little.
- **Long-term:** Europe dominated by Hitler. Britain alone. US accelerates rearmament (Two-Ocean Navy Act). Free French (de Gaulle) formed.

### 3. Battle of Britain – Jul–Oct 1940
- **What:** Luftwaffe (Göring) attempts air superiority for Operation Sea Lion (invasion). Phases: Channel convoys → Radar/airfields (Eagle Attack) → London Blitz (Sep 7) → Night bombing.
- **Why:** Precondition for invasion; force UK peace deal.
- **Who:** RAF Fighter Command (Dowding, Park, Leigh-Mallory) ~700 Spitfires/Hurricanes. Luftwaffe ~2,500 aircraft (Bf 109, 110, He 111, Ju 88, Stuka).
- **Tech:** **Chain Home Radar** (Dowding System - integrated C2), IFF, Ultra decrypts (Enigma).
- **Immediate:** Luftwaffe fails. Sea Lion postponed indefinitely. Shift to night Blitz (London, Coventry, Liverpool) until May 1941.
- **Long-term:** UK survives as base. First German strategic defeat. Proved air power alone cannot conquer industrial nation.

### 4. Operation Barbarossa – Jun–Dec 1941
- **What:** 3 Army Groups (North/Leeb, Centre/Bock, South/Rundstedt) + Finnish/Romanian allies. 3.8M men, 3,500 tanks, 7,000 guns, 2,800 aircraft. Goal: A-A Line (Arkhangelsk-Astrakhan) in 3 months.
- **Why:** Ideological war (Judeo-Bolshevism), resources (Ukraine grain, Caucasus oil, Donets coal), destroy Red Army before US entry.
- **Who:** Wehrmacht vs RKKA (Stalin, Shaposhnikov, Zhukov, Timoshenko, Budyonny).
- **Events:** Massive encirclements (Minsk, Smolensk, Kiev - 665k POWs, Vyazma/Bryansk - 673k POWs). 3.3M Soviet POWs captured (2M die 1941–42).
- **Failure Causes:** Logistics (gauges, roads, distance), underestimation of Soviet reserves (300→600+ divs), weather (Rasputitsa, General Winter), no strategic bomber, diversion to Kiev.
- **Immediate:** Moscow counter-offensive saves capital. Germany commits to war of attrition it cannot win.
- **Long-term:** Eastern Front becomes main effort (75–80% German losses). Holocaust escalates (Einsatzgruppen shootings → Wannsee → Death Camps).

### 5. Pearl Harbor & Japanese Southern Expansion – Dec 1941 – May 1942
- **What:** Kido Butai (6 carriers, 414 planes) strikes US Pacific Fleet. Simultaneous invasions: Malaya, Philippines, Hong Kong, Wake, Guam, Dutch East Indies, Burma, Thailand.
- **Why:** US oil embargo (90% of Japanese oil) threatened navy/industry. "Strike South" (Nanshin-ron) vs "Strike North" (Hokushin-ron) decided 1941.
- **Who:** Yamamoto/Nagumo (Japan); Kimmel/Short (US); Percival (Malaya); MacArthur (Philippines).
- **Immediate:** US battleships sunk/damaged (8), 2,403 dead. Carriers absent (Enterprise, Lexington, Saratoga). US united. Japan achieves all initial objectives (resource zone secured) by Mar 1942.
- **Long-term:** Strategic failure: US carrier force intact; submarine bases intact; fuel tanks intact; US industrial might mobilized; "Sleeping Giant" awakened.

### 6. Battle of Midway – Jun 4–7, 1942
- **What:** Yamamoto attempts to lure US carriers into decisive battle by invading Midway. US codebreakers (Hypo/Station H, Rochefort) know plan (AF=Midway).
- **Why:** Destroy US carrier fleet; extend defensive perimeter; force negotiation.
- **Who:** Nagumo (4 CV: *Akagi, Kaga, Soryu, Hiryu*) vs Spruance/Fletcher (3 CV: *Enterprise, Hornet, Yorktown*).
- **Events:** Jun 4 AM: Japanese strike Midway. US land attacks fail (torpedo squadrons decimated - VT-8). Jun 4 10:20: McClusky (VB-6) & Best (VB-3) dive bombers catch *Kaga, Akagi, Soryu* with decks full (refueling/rearming). *Hiryu* damages *Yorktown*. Jun 4 PM: *Hiryu* sunk. Jun 6: *Yorktown* sunk by sub *I-168*.
- **Immediate:** Japan loses 4 fleet carriers, 332 aircraft, 3,000 experienced aircrew/maintainers. Strategic initiative passes to US.
- **Long-term:** Irreplaceable loss of *Kido Butai* core. Japan shifts to defensive.

### 7. Battle of Stalingrad – Aug 1942 – Feb 1943
- **What:** 6th Army (Paulus) + 4th Panzer Army (Hoth) fight for city on Volga. Urban warfare (Rattenkrieg). Soviet 62nd Army (Chuikov) holds "tennis court" strips. Operation Uranus (Nov 19) encircles 250k+ Axis. Winter Storm fails. Surrender Feb 2.
- **Why:** Hitler: City bearing Stalin's name; flank cover for Caucasus oil drive (Case Blue). Stalin: "Not a step back" (Order 227); symbol of resistance.
- **Who:** Paulus (promoted Field Marshal to deter surrender), Chuikov, Zhukov (planning), Vasilevsky, Vatutin, Rokossovsky (Don Front).
- **Numbers:** Axis ~800k–1M committed; ~300k encircled; ~91k captured (5k survived). Soviet ~1.1M casualties.
- **Immediate:** Psychological turning point. Germany loses strategic offensive capability. Turkey/Japan/Allies reassess.
- **Long-term:** Myth of German invincibility broken. Soviet confidence soared. "Total War" speech (Goebbels Feb 18).

### 8. Battle of the Atlantic – Sep 1939 – May 1945
- **What:** Tonnage war. U-boats (Dönitz) vs Allied convoys/escorts/air cover. Wolfpacks (Rudeltaktik).
- **Phases:**
    - 1939–40: Surface raiders + U-boats (Happy Time 1: Fall of France gives bases).
    - 1941: US entry → "Second Happy Time" (US East Coast unescorted).
    - 1942–Mar 1943: Climax. Mid-Atlantic Gap (Black Pit). March 1943: 108 ships sunk (627k tons).
    - **Turning Point (May 1943):** "Black May" - 41 U-boats sunk (25% of fleet). Dönitz withdraws from North Atlantic.
- **Tech/Intel:** **Ultra (Enigma M4/Shark broken Dec 1942)**, Huff-Duff (HF/DF), Centimetric Radar (ASV Mk III - undetectable by Metox), Leigh Light, Escort Carriers (CVE), Long-range Liberators (VLR), Hedgehog/Squid ASW mortars.
- **Numbers:** 3,500 merchant ships sunk (14.5M tons), 175 warships. 783 U-boats lost, 30,000 U-boat crew dead (75% casualty rate).
- **Significance:** Lifeline for UK/USSR (Lend-Lease via Arctic/Med/Persian Gulf). Precondition for Overlord.

### 9. D-Day: Operation Overlord / Neptune – Jun 6, 1944
- **What:** Largest amphibious invasion. 5 Beaches (US: Utah, Omaha; UK: Gold, Sword; CA: Juno). 3 Airborne Divisions (US 82/101, UK 6) inland. 7,000 vessels, 11,000 aircraft, 156k troops D-Day.
- **Why:** Open Second Front (Stalin pressure); destroy German forces in West; advance to Germany.
- **Deception:** **Operation Fortitude** (Fake FUSAG under Patton at Dover/Pas-de-Calais; Double Cross agents - Garbo, Brutus; Fake radio traffic, inflatable tanks).
- **Tech:** Mulberry Harbours (Arromanches - UK, Omaha - US destroyed by storm), PLUTO (Pipeline Under The Ocean), Hobart's Funnies (DD Tanks, Crab Flails, Crocodile Flamethrowers, AVRE).
- **Omaha:** Near disaster. High bluffs, veteran 352nd Div, DD tanks sunk, fire support insufficient. "Bloody Omaha" (2,400 casualties).
- **Immediate:** Beachheads secured. Cherbourg captured Jun 26 (port destroyed). Caen (D-Day objective) falls Jul 20.
- **Breakout:** Operation Cobra (Jul 25) → Falaise Pocket (Aug) → Paris (Aug 25) → German border (Sep).

### 10. Operation Bagration – Jun–Aug 1944
- **What:** Soviet Belorussian Offensive. 1.7M men, 4,000 tanks, 24,000 guns. 4 Fronts (Baltic, 1/2/3 Belorussian). Deep Battle doctrine (Maskirovka deception).
- **Why:** Destroy Army Group Centre (Busch/Model). Coordinate with Overlord.
- **Result:** 28 German divisions destroyed (350k–450k casualties). Minsk liberated Jul 3. Advance 600km to Vistula/Warsaw in 5 weeks. German Eastern Front collapses.
- **Significance:** Largest German defeat. Proved Red Army operational mastery. Warsaw Uprising (Aug–Oct) occurs as Soviets halt on Vistula (controversy: exhaustion vs politics).

### 11. Battle of Leyte Gulf – Oct 23–26, 1944
- **What:** Largest naval battle (tonnage/area). 4 engagements: Sibuyan Sea, Surigao Strait, Cape Engaño, Samar.
- **Why:** Japan defends Philippines (oil lifeline). *Sho-Go* Plan: Decoy Northern Force (Ozawa - carriers no planes) lures Halsey (3rd Fleet) north. Center Force (Kurita - *Yamato, Musashi*) + Southern Force (Nishimura/Shima) attack landing beaches via San Bernardino/Surigao.
- **Events:** *Musashi* sunk (Sibuyan). Nishimura destroyed (Surigao - last battleship line action). **Battle off Samar:** Taffy 3 (Escort Carriers + Destroyers) vs Kurita's Center Force. Desperate defense (Johnston, Hoel, Heermann, Samuel B. Roberts). Kurita withdraws fearing fleet carriers.
- **Result:** IJN finished as fighting force. Kamikaze debut (*Shikishima* unit).

### 12. Battle of the Bulge (Ardennes Offensive / Wacht am Rhein) – Dec 1944 – Jan 1945
- **What:** Hitler's last gamble. 6th SS Panzer Army (Dietrich), 5th Panzer Army (Manteuffel), 7th Army (Brandenberger). 200k men, 600 tanks. Goal: Antwerp, split US/UK, force negotiated peace.
- **Why:** Desperation. Poor weather grounded Allied air.
- **Events:** Initial surprise (Losheim Gap). Malmedy Massacre (Peiper/Kampfgruppe). Bastogne surrounded (101st Airborne "Nuts!"). Weather clears Dec 23 → Allied airpower decimates supply columns. Patton 3rd Army turns 90° north (logistical miracle) relieves Bastogne Dec 26.
- **Result:** German offensive spent. Irreplaceable losses (1,600 AFVs, 20k KIA, 80k total). Western Front defense collapsed.

### 13. Strategic Bombing of Japan & Atomic Bombs – 1944–45
- **Shift:** Precision (Nordheim) failed (jet stream). LeMay (XXI Bomber Command, Mariana bases) → **Night Low-Level Incendiary Raids** (M-69 napalm).
- **Operation Meetinghouse (Mar 9/10, 1945):** 334 B-29s. Tokyo firestorm. 16 sq mi destroyed. ~100k dead, 1M homeless. 67 cities firebombed.
- **Manhattan Project:** Trinity Test (Jul 16, Alamogordo). *Little Boy* (Hiroshima, Aug 6), *Fat Man* (Nagasaki, Aug 9).
- **Decision Factors:** Avoid invasion (Downfall/Olympic/Coronet - est. 500k–1M US casualties, millions Japanese), impress USSR, justify cost ($2B), end war before Soviet deep penetration Manchuria/Korea.
- **Japanese Surrender:** "Gyokuon-hōsō" (Aug 15). Condition: *Kokutai* preserved (Hirohito stays). Accepted by Allies (Byrnes Note).

## VII. POLITICS & DIPLOMACY

### Alliances
- **Axis:** Rome-Berlin Axis (1936), Anti-Comintern Pact (1936/37), Pact of Steel (1939), Tripartite Pact (1940). Hungary, Romania, Bulgaria, Slovakia, Croatia, Thailand, Manchukuo co-belligerents. **No true combined staff/planning.**
- **Allies:** "United Nations" Declaration (Jan 1, 1942, 26 nations). **Grand Alliance (UK, US, USSR)** - Marriage of convenience. Combined Chiefs of Staff (CCS - US/UK). No Soviet seat at CCS. Tehran/Yalta/Potsdam summits.

### Key Conferences
1.  **Atlantic Conference (Aug 1941):** Roosevelt/Churchill → Atlantic Charter (8 points: no territorial aggrandizement, self-determination, free trade, freedom of seas, disarmament).
2.  **Arcadia (Dec 41–Jan 42):** Germany First; CCS created; ABDA Command (failed).
3.  **Casablanca (Jan 1943):** Unconditional Surrender; Sicily invasion; Bomber Offensive (Pointblank); Free French unification (Giraud/de Gaulle).
4.  **Tehran (Nov–Dec 1943):** Overlord (May 1944) + Anvil (S. France); USSR enters vs Japan 3 months post-Germany; Poland borders (Curzon/ Oder-Neisse); UN concept.
5.  **Yalta (Feb 1945):** Germany zones (4+ Berlin); Reparations ($20B, 50% USSR); UN Veto (P5); Poland govt (Lublin + London Poles); Far East (Sakhalin, Kuriles, Port Arthur, Mongolia independence).
6.  **Potsdam (Jul–Aug 1945):** Truman/Stalin/Attlee. Oder-Neisse line confirmed; Denazification, Demilitarization, Democratization, Decentralization; Reparations from zones; Potsdam Declaration (Japan surrender terms).

### Occupation Policies
- **Germany:** 4 Zones (US, UK, FR, USSR) + Berlin 4 sectors. Allied Control Council (ACJ). Nuremberg Trials (IMT). Denazification (fragebogen). Bizonia (1947) → Trizonia → FRG (1949) / GDR (1949).
- **Japan:** Sole US Occupation (SCAP - MacArthur). Emperor retained. Constitution (1947 - Article 9 pacifism). Zaibatsu dissolution. Land reform. Tokyo Trials (IMTFE).
- **Eastern Europe:** Soviet "People's Democracies" installed (Poland, Hungary, Czechoslovakia, Romania, Bulgaria, East Germany, Albania). Yugoslavia (Tito) independent communist. Finland: Neutrality (Paasikivi-Kekkonen line).

## VIII. SOCIETY: DAILY LIFE, CULTURE, HOME FRONTS

### The Holocaust (Shoah) & Nazi Crimes
- **Systematic Murder:** ~6 Million Jews (2/3 of European Jewry).
- **Phases:** Persecution (Nuremberg Laws 1935, Kristallnacht 1938) → Ghettoization (Warsaw, Lodz, 1940–41) → Mobile Killing (Einsatzgruppen 1941–42, 1.3M shot) → **Final Solution (Wannsee Jan 1942)** → Extermination Camps (Operation Reinhard: Belzec, Sobibor, Treblinka - 1.7M; Auschwitz-Birkenau - 1.1M; Majdanek, Chelmno, Maly Trostenets).
- **Other Victims:** Roma/Sinti (250k–500k), Disabled (Aktion T4, 275k), Soviet POWs (3.3M died), Polish intelligentsia, Political opponents, Homosexuals, Jehovah's Witnesses. **Total ~11–17M non-combatants murdered by Nazi regime.**

### Soviet Repression & War Effort
- **Gulag:** Prison labor (Kolyma, Vorkuta). Mortality spikes 1942–43.
- **Deportations:** Volga Germans (1941), Chechens/Ingush/Karachays/Balkans/Kalmyks/Crimean Tatars (1943–44) - "Collective Punishment" (accused of collaboration). High mortality.
- **Home Front:** "Everything for the Front, Everything for Victory." Women 800k in military (snipers, pilots - Night Witches 588th Regt, medics). Factory evacuation (Ural/Siberia) - 1,500 enterprises moved 1941. Rationing severe (Leningrad starvation).

### United States Home Front
- **Mobilization:** 16M served (10M drafted). Women workforce +6M ("Rosie the Riveter"). Rationing (gas, tires, sugar, meat, shoes). War Bonds (85M buyers). Internment: **Executive Order 9066** → 120k Japanese-Americans (2/3 citizens) in camps (Manzanar, etc.). *Korematsu v US* upheld. Double V Campaign (Black press: Victory abroad, Victory at home vs segregation). Tuskegee Airmen, 761st Tank Battalion, 442nd RCT (Nisei - most decorated).
- **Economy:** War Production Board (WPB). Office of Price Administration (OPA). GDP doubles. Full employment.

### British Home Front
- **Total War:** Conscription (men 18–51, women 20–30 - Auxiliary Services). Rationing (1940–1954). Blitz (London, Coventry, Liverpool, Clydeside). Evacuation (Operation Pied Piper - 1.5M children). Home Guard (LDV - 1.5M). Ultra secrecy. BBC World Service. SOE/OSS resistance support.

### Japanese Home Front
- **Total War:** "One Hundred Million Hearts Beating as One." Neighborhood Associations (*Tonarigumi*). Rationing severe (1944: 1,800 cal → 1,400 cal). Student mobilization (factory, kamikaze prep). Firebombing devastation (67 cities). Civilian defense (bamboo spears, *Ketsu-Go* plan for invasion). Propaganda: "Glorious death" vs "Devil Americans."

### German Home Front
- **Early "Guns & Butter":** Speer rationalization (1942) → "Total War" (Goebbels Sportpalast Feb 1943). Foreign/Slave Labor (Ostarbeiter, 7.5M forced laborers 1944). Bombing effects (morale, dispersal, oil/transport collapse 1944–45). Volkssturm (Oct 1944 - old men/boys). Werewolf (guerrilla - negligible).

### China
- **Nationalist (KMT) Areas:** Corruption, inflation (currency collapse), conscription brutality, famine (Henan 1942–43, 2–3M dead), US aid (Stilwell/Chennault).
- **Communist (CCP) Areas (Yan'an):** Rectification Movement (Mao Thought), land reform, guerrilla warfare, self-reliance (Nanniwan), United Front diplomacy.
- **Occupied Areas:** Collaborationist regimes (Wang Jingwei Nanjing). Brutal "Three Alls" (Kill All, Burn All, Loot All) campaigns. Comfort Women system (200k+ Korean/Chinese/SE Asian women).

## XI. CONSEQUENCES & LEGACY

### Immediate Consequences (1945–1947)
- **Geopolitical:** Bipolar World (US vs USSR). Iron Curtain descends (Churchill Fulton 1946). Europe divided. Germany/Japan occupied. Colonial Empires fatally weakened (India 1947, Indonesia 1949, Vietnam War starts 1946).
- **Humanitarian:** UNRRA / IRO manage 10M+ DPs. Nuremberg Trials (Nov 1945–Oct 1946) establish "Crimes Against Humanity," "Crimes Against Peace." Tokyo Trials. Denazification (mixed success). Expulsion of Germans (12–14M from East Europe, 500k–2M deaths).
- **Economic:** US produces 50% world GDP. Bretton Woods (1944) → IMF/World Bank, Gold-Dollar Standard. Marshall Plan (1948) rebuilds West Europe. USSR rejects, creates Comecon. Japan reformed (Land reform, Unions, Constitution).
- **Territorial:** USSR annexes Baltics, E. Poland, Bessarabia, N. Bukovina, Carpatho-Ukraine, S. Sakhalin, Kuriles. Poland shifted West (Oder-Neisse). Korea divided 38th Parallel. China Civil War resumes (Communist victory 1949).

### Long-Term Historical Impact
- **International Order:** United Nations (Security Council P5 Veto). Universal Declaration Human Rights (1948). Genocide Convention (1948). Geneva Conventions (1949). Decolonization wave (1945–1975).
- **Technology & Science:** Nuclear Age (Hiroshima → Cold War arms race). Jet Aviation (Comet 1952, 707 1958). Rocketry (V-2 → Sputnik 1957, Apollo). Radar → Air Traffic Control, Microwave. Computing (Colossus, ENIAC → Digital Age). Penicillin/Medical mass production. Synthetic materials (Nylon, Rubber).
- **Social:** Women in workforce (permanent shift). Civil Rights momentum (US: Executive Order 9981 1948 desegregation; UK: Windrush generation). Welfare State expansion (Beveridge Report UK 1942 → NHS 1948). European Integration (ECSC 1951 → EEC → EU) - "Never Again."
- **Memory & Historiography:** "Good War" narrative (Allied) vs Revisionism (Strategic Bombing morality, Soviet crimes, Colonial contributions). Holocaust memory central to Western identity (1960s Eichmann trial, 1978 *Holocaust* series, 1993 *Schindler's List*). "Great Patriotic War" core to Russian identity (Victory Day May 9). Japan: Pacifism (Article 9) vs Revisionism (Yasukuni, textbooks). China: "Century of Humiliation" narrative.

### Legacy Statistics
- **Nuclear Weapons:** ~12,500 warheads globally (2024) - direct lineage Manhattan Project.
- **Cold War Conflicts:** Korea, Vietnam, Afghanistan, Proxy Wars - ~20M dead.
- **Decolonization:** 80+ nations gained independence 1945–1990.
