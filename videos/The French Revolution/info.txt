# The French Revolution: Comprehensive Historical Research Document

---

## 1. BACKGROUND

### 1.1 Historical Context: The Ancien Régime
*   **Structure:** France was an absolute monarchy under the **Bourbon dynasty** (Louis XVI ruled 1774–1792). Society was legally divided into **Three Estates**:
    *   **First Estate (Clergy):** ~130,000 people. Owned ~10% of land. Exempt from most taxes (paid *don gratuit* voluntary gift). Higher clergy (bishops/abbots) were nobles; lower clergy (parish priests) were often poor and sympathetic to Third Estate.
    *   **Second Estate (Nobility):** ~350,000–400,000 people. Owned ~25–30% of land. Exempt from *taille* (main land tax) and *corvée* (forced labor). Held monopolies on high military/ecclesiastical/government offices. Collected feudal dues (*seigneurial rights*) from peasants.
    *   **Third Estate (Everyone Else):** ~27–28 million people (98% of population). Included bourgeoisie (merchants, lawyers, bankers, ~8% of pop), urban workers (*sans-culottes*), and peasantry (~80% of pop). Bore the full tax burden.
*   **Political Theory:** "L'État, c'est moi" (The State is me). King ruled by Divine Right. No national representative body (Estates-General last met 1614). *Parlements* (regional high courts, mostly nobility) had right of *remonstrance* (registering royal edicts), often blocking reform to protect privilege.
*   **Legal Patchwork:** No unified legal code. *Pays d'état* (provinces with estates) vs. *pays d'élection* (provinces directly administered). Different weights, measures, customs barriers (*traites*) internally.

### 1.2 Causes

#### **Long-Term Structural Causes**
*   **Fiscal Crisis:** Chronic deficit spending. Revenue system inefficient (tax farming *Ferme Générale*), nobility/clergy exempt. Debt service consumed ~50% of annual revenue by 1788.
*   **War Debts:**
    *   Seven Years' War (1756–1763): Loss of colonies (Canada, India), massive debt.
    *   American War of Independence (1778–1783): ~1.3 billion *livres* spent. No territorial gain. Bankrupted treasury.
*   **Enlightenment Ideas:** Challenge to Divine Right, privilege, tradition.
    *   *Montesquieu:* Separation of powers (*Spirit of Laws*, 1748).
    *   *Voltaire:* Religious tolerance, criticism of Church/clergy.
    *   *Rousseau:* Popular sovereignty, General Will, Social Contract (1762).
    *   *Physiocrats (Quesnay/Turgot):* Free trade in grain, single land tax (*impôt unique*), anti-mercantilism.
*   **Rise of the Bourgeoisie:** Wealthy, educated, excluded from political power. Resented noble monopoly on office. Demanded "Careers open to talent."

#### **Short-Term Triggers (1787–1789)**
*   **Bad Harvests (1787, 1788):** Hailstorm July 1788 destroyed crops. Grain prices skyrocketed (bread = 50–88% of worker wages).
*   **Hard Winter 1788–89:** Rivers froze, mills stopped, transport halted. Unemployment in textile industry.
*   **Financial Ministers' Failures:**
    *   *Turgot (1774–76):* Tried free grain trade, abolished corvée/guilds. Dismissed by nobility/Parlements.
    *   *Necker (1777–81, 1788–89):* Published *Compte Rendu* (1781) – misleadingly showed surplus. Borrowed heavily at high interest. Recalled 1788 as savior.
    *   *Calonne (1783–87):* Spent lavishly, borrowed. Proposed *Subvention Territoriale* (universal land tax) + Assembly of Notables (1787). Notables rejected; Calonne fired.
    *   *Brienne (1787–88):* Modified Calonne's plans. *Parlement of Paris* refused registration, demanded Estates-General. King exiled Parlement (August 1787). "Day of Tiles" (Grenoble, June 1788) – popular revolt against royal troops. Brienne collapsed; Necker recalled August 1788.
*   **Calling of Estates-General:** August 8, 1788 – Louis XVI agreed to convene May 1, 1789 (later May 5).

### 1.3 Political Situation (1789)
*   **Weak Monarchy:** Louis XVI indecisive, influenced by Marie Antoinette ("Madame Deficit") and hardliners (Artois, Polignac).
*   **"Pre-Revolution" (1787–89):** Conflict between Crown and *Parlements* (nobility defending privilege framed as defending "liberty").
*   **Cahiers de Doléances:** Grievance notebooks drafted by all three estates for Estates-General. Revealed consensus on: constitutional monarchy, regular Estates-General, tax equality, individual liberty, end to *lettres de cachet*. Divergence on: voting by head vs. order, noble privileges.

### 1.4 Economic Situation
*   **Currency:** *Livre tournois* (£). 1 Livre = 20 *sols* = 240 *deniers*. *Louis d'or* (gold) = 24 livres. *Écu* (silver) = 6 livres.
*   **Debt:** ~4 billion *livres* (1789). Annual revenue ~500M; expenditure ~600M+. Interest rates 8–12%.
*   **Tax System:**
    *   *Taille:* Land tax (peasants only).
    *   *Gabelle:* Salt tax (monopoly, hated).
    *   *Aides:* Excise on wine/liquor.
    *   *Traites:* Customs duties (internal/external).
    *   *Capitation/Vingtièmes:* War taxes (nobles/clergy theoretically paid *vingtièmes* but often evaded).
*   **Grain Trade:** *Police du grain* (regulation) vs. *Liberté du grain* (free trade). Fluctuating policies caused instability.

### 1.5 Social Situation
*   **Peasantry (80%):** Serfdom largely gone in North/Central (personal freedom), persisted in East (Franche-Comté). *Seigneurial dues* (cens, champart, banalités - mill/oven/winepress monopoly) heavy. *Corvée royale* (road repair) burdensome. Hunger constant threat.
*   **Urban Poor/Sans-Culottes:** Journeymen, shopkeepers, laborers in Paris (~600k pop). Highly volatile. Mobilized by bread prices, rumors, radical journalists (Marat, Hébert).
*   **Bourgeoisie:** Lawyers (*avocats*), merchants, financiers, doctors. *Robe* nobility (office holders) vs. non-noble. Cultural capital (Enlightenment salons).
*   **Nobility:** *Noblesse d'épée* (old military) vs. *Noblesse de robe* (judicial/admin). *Hobereaux* (poor provincial nobles) vs. Court nobility. Rift between liberal nobles (Lafayette, Noailles) and reactionaries.

### 1.6 Religious Situation
*   **Gallican Church:** Catholic Church in France had autonomy from Pope (Pragmatic Sanction of Bourges, Concordat of Bologna). King appointed bishops.
*   **Wealth:** Tithe (*dîme*) ~6-10% of harvest. Vast landholdings. Exempt from *taille*.
*   **Tensions:** Jansenist vs. Jesuit conflicts. Enlightenment deism/atheism spreading among elites. Lower clergy often aligned with Third Estate grievances.

## 2. TIMELINE: KEY DATES & CHRONOLOGICAL EVENTS

### **Phase 1: The Collapse of the Ancien Régime (1789)**

*   **Jan 24, 1789:** Royal edict convokes Estates-General for May 5. Doubling of Third Estate deputies granted (600 vs 300 each for 1st/2nd), but voting method unspecified.
*   **May 5, 1789:** Estates-General opens at Versailles. Necker's 3-hour speech focuses on finances, not political reform. Voting by *Order* (Estate) implied.
*   **June 17, 1789:** Third Estate declares itself **National Assembly** (Sieyès motion). "The nation exists prior to all; it is the source of all authority."
*   **June 20, 1789:** **Tennis Court Oath** (*Jeu de Paume*). Deputies locked out of hall; swear "not to separate until a constitution is established." Clergy/deputies join.
*   **June 23, 1789:** *Séance Royale*. Louis XVI rejects National Assembly, orders voting by order, promises reforms. Deputies refuse to leave (Mirabeau: "We are here by the will of the people... only bayonets can drive us out").
*   **June 27, 1789:** Louis XVI capitulates, orders First/Second Estates to join National Assembly. **National Constituent Assembly** formed.
*   **July 11, 1789:** Necker dismissed (conservative victory). Troop concentrations around Paris/Versailles (Swiss, German regiments).
*   **July 12–14, 1789:** **Paris Insurrection**. Camille Desmoulins calls to arms (Palais-Royal). Electors form Permanent Committee. Search for arms.
*   **July 14, 1789:** **Storming of the Bastille**. Governor de Launay surrenders after 4-hour siege. ~98 attackers killed, 1 defender. De Launay & Prévôt des Marchands (Flesselles) lynched. Symbolic fall of royal tyranny.
*   **July 15, 1789:** Louis XVI visits Paris, wears tricolor cockade. Recalls Necker. Bailly = Mayor of Paris. Lafayette = Commander National Guard.
*   **July–Aug 1789:** **Great Fear** (*Grande Peur*). Rural panic rumors of aristocratic brigands burning crops. Peasants attack châteaux, burn feudal records (*terriers*).
*   **August 4, 1789:** **August Decrees** (Night of Abolition of Feudalism). Noble/clerical deputies renounce privileges (tithes, seigneurial dues, venality of office, tax exemptions, provincial privileges). *Legally* ended feudalism, but "redemption" clauses required peasants to buy out dues.
*   **August 26, 1789:** **Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen** (17 articles). Natural rights, liberty, property, security, resistance to oppression, popular sovereignty, law as expression of general will, freedom of speech/press/religion.
*   **October 5–6, 1789:** **Women's March on Versailles** (March on Versailles). 7,000+ Parisian women (market women) + National Guard march to Versailles. Demand bread, King's return to Paris. Invasion of Palace. Royal family forced to Tuileries Palace, Paris. Assembly follows.

### **Phase 2: The Constitutional Monarchy (1789–1791)**

*   **Nov 2, 1789:** Church lands nationalized (*biens nationaux*) to back new currency *assignats*.
*   **Dec 1789:** Administrative reorganization: 83 **Departments** (uniform size/population), Districts, Cantons, Communes. Elected officials.
*   **Feb 13, 1790:** Monastic vows suppressed; religious orders dissolved (except teaching/nursing).
*   **July 12, 1790:** **Civil Constitution of the Clergy**. Church subordinated to State. Bishops/priests elected by citizens (including non-Catholics). State pays salaries. Oath required.
*   **July 14, 1790:** **Fête de la Fédération** (Champ de Mars). Festival of unity. King swears oath to Constitution. High point of consensus.
*   **Nov 1790:** Clerical Oath deadline. ~54% parish priests refuse (*refractory* clergy). Schism begins. Pope Pius VI condemns (April 1791).
*   **June 1791:** **Le Chapelier Law**. Bans guilds, workers' associations (coalitions), strikes. Economic liberalism (laissez-faire).
*   **June 20–21, 1791:** **Flight to Varennes**. Royal family attempts escape to Montmédy (loyal troops under Bouillé). Recognized at Varennes, returned to Paris. Monarchy discredited. Petition for Republic (Cordeliers/Jacobins).
*   **July 17, 1791:** **Champ de Mars Massacre**. Crowd (~50k) petitions for Republic/King's abdication. Lafayette/National Guard fires. ~50 dead. Split: Constitutional Monarchists (Feuillants) vs. Republicans (Jacobins/Cordeliers).
*   **Sept 3, 1791:** **Constitution of 1791** ratified by King. Constitutional Monarchy. **Legislative Assembly** (745 deputies, no sitting Constituent members). King has suspensive veto. Active/Passive citizen distinction (tax franchise).
*   **Oct 1, 1791:** Legislative Assembly convenes.

### **Phase 3: War & The Fall of the Monarchy (1792)**

*   **Apr 20, 1792:** France declares war on **Austria** (King/Feuillants hope war discredits Revolution or restores prestige; Girondins want to export Revolution; Royalists hope for foreign victory).
*   **July 11, 1792:** **"La Patrie en Danger"** declared. Volunteers (*fédérés*) march to Paris.
*   **July 25, 1792:** **Brunswick Manifesto** (Duke of Brunswick, Austro-Prussian commander). Threatens "exemplary vengeance" on Paris if Royal family harmed. Radicalizes Paris.
*   **August 10, 1792:** **Storming of the Tuileries** (Second Revolution). *Fédérés*, *Sans-culottes*, Paris National Guard (mandated by Pétion) attack Palace. Swiss Guard massacred (~600 killed). King suspended. **Insurrectionary Commune** takes power. Assembly votes Convention election by universal male suffrage.
*   **Aug–Sept 1792:** **September Massacres**. Fear of advancing Prussians (Verdun falls Sept 2) + internal enemies. Mobs storm Paris prisons, kill ~1,100–1,400 prisoners (half common criminals, many refractory priests). Condoned by Commune (Marat, Danton).
*   **Sept 20, 1792:** **Battle of Valmy**. Kellermann/Dumouriez halt Prussian advance. Cannonade. Goethe: "From this place and from this day forth begins a new era in the history of the world."
*   **Sept 21, 1792:** **National Convention** meets. Abolishes Monarchy. **Proclaims Republic (Year I).**
*   **Nov 1792:** **Decree of Fraternity**: Offers aid to peoples wishing to overthrow tyrants. Annexation of Savoy/Nice.
*   **Dec 1792:** **Trial of Louis XVI** begins ("Louis Capet").

### **Phase 4: The Reign of Terror & Radical Republic (1793–1794)**

*   **Jan 21, 1793:** **Execution of Louis XVI** (Place de la Révolution). War declared on Britain, Dutch Republic, Spain (First Coalition).
*   **Feb 1793:** **Levée en masse** (300,000 men). **War in Vendée** begins (Royalist/Catholic peasant uprising against conscription/clergy oath).
*   **Mar 1793:** **Revolutionary Tribunal** created. **Committee of Public Safety** created (executive power). **Committee of General Security** (police). **Representatives on Mission** sent to departments/armies.
*   **Apr 1793:** Dumouriez defects to Austrians. Girondins blamed.
*   **May 31 – June 2, 1793:** **Insurrection of 31 May – 2 June**. *Sans-culottes* / National Guard (Hanriot) surround Convention. **Arrest of 29 Girondin deputies**. Montagnard (Jacobin) dominance secured.
*   **June 24, 1793:** **Constitution of 1793** (Year I) ratified by plebiscite (universal male suffrage). Never implemented ("Revolutionary Government until Peace").
*   **July 13, 1793:** **Assassination of Marat** by Charlotte Corday (Girondin sympathizer).
*   **July 27, 1793:** **Robespierre joins Committee of Public Safety**.
*   **Aug 23, 1793:** **Levée en masse** (Total War decree). All Frenchmen in permanent requisition. ~750,000–800,000 under arms by 1794.
*   **Sept 5, 1793:** **Terror made "Order of the Day"** (Paris sections demand). **Law of Suspects** (vague definition of enemies).
*   **Oct 16, 1793:** **Execution of Marie Antoinette**.
*   **Oct 24, 1793:** **Republican Calendar** adopted (Year II starts Sept 22, 1793).
*   **Oct–Dec 1793:** **Federalist Revolts** crushed (Lyon, Toulon, Marseille, Bordeaux). **Siege of Lyon** (Collot d'Herbois/Fouché – mass executions/grapeshot). **Toulon** retaken (Napoleon's artillery plan).
*   **Oct 31, 1793:** **Execution of 21 Girondins** (Brissot, Vergniaud).
*   **Nov 10, 1793 (20 Brumaire Year II):** **Festival of Reason** (Notre Dame -> Temple of Reason). Dechristianization peaks (closing churches, forced priest marriages).
*   **Dec 4, 1793 (14 Frimaire):** **Law of 14 Frimaire**: Centralizes power in Committee of Public Safety. Revolutionary Army disbanded.
*   **Dec 1793:** **Vendée crushed** at Battle of Savenay. "Infernal Columns" (Turreau) devastate region (Jan–May 1794). ~170k–250k dead (Vendée total).
*   **Feb 1794:** **Ventôse Decrees** (Saint-Just): Confiscate property of suspects for distribution to poor (largely unimplemented).
*   **Mar 1794:** **Hébertists (Ultra-radicals)** arrested/executed (Mar 24). **Dantonists (Indulgents)** arrested/executed (Apr 5). Robespierre isolates himself.
*   **May 7, 1794 (18 Floréal):** **Cult of the Supreme Being** decreed (State Deism).
*   **June 8, 1794 (20 Prairial):** **Festival of Supreme Being** (Champ de Mars). Robespierre as high priest.
*   **June 10, 1794 (22 Prairial):** **Law of 22 Prairial** (Couthon/Robespierre). "Great Terror". Tribunal streamlined: no defense counsel, no witnesses, only death or acquittal. Executions skyrocket (Paris: ~1,300 in 6 weeks).
*   **June 26, 1794:** **Battle of Fleurus** (Jourdan). Austrian army defeated. Belgium conquered. Military crisis ends.
*   **July 27, 1794 (9 Thermidor Year II):** **Fall of Robespierre**. Convention denounces him. Outlawed. Hanriot/Commune attempt insurrection fails.
*   **July 28, 1794 (10 Thermidor):** **Execution of Robespierre, Saint-Just, Couthon, Hanriot** + 80+ followers.

### **Phase 5: Thermidorian Reaction & The Directory (1794–1799)**

*   **Aug 1794:** Revolutionary Tribunal reformed. Jacobin Club closed (Nov 1794). "White Terror" in South (royalists kill Jacobins).
*   **Dec 1794:** Price controls (Maximum) lifted. Hyperinflation of *assignats* accelerates.
*   **Apr 1795:** Treaty of Basel (Prussia/Spain leave Coalition).
*   **May 20–23, 1795 (Prairial Year III):** **Last Sans-Culotte Uprising** (Bread/Constitution 1793). Crushed. Disarmament of *sans-culottes*.
*   **Aug 22, 1795:** **Constitution of Year III** ratified. **Directory** (5 Directors) + Bicameral Legislature (Council of 500 / Council of Ancients). Property franchise.
*   **Oct 5, 1795 (13 Vendémiaire):** **Royalist Insurrection** in Paris. Napoleon Bonaparte ("Whiff of Grapeshot") saves Convention.
*   **Oct 26, 1795:** Convention dissolves. Directory takes power.
*   **1796:** **Conspiracy of Equals** (Babeuf) – early communism. Crushed.
*   **1796–97:** **Italian Campaign** (Napoleon). Military glory, loot funds Directory. Treaty of Campo Formio (Oct 1797) – Austria out.
*   **Sept 4, 1797 (18 Fructidor Year V):** **Coup of 18 Fructidor**. Directors (Barras, Rewbell, La Révellière) + Army (Augereau) purge royalist gains in elections. 2 Directors removed, 53 deputies deported. Directory becomes military dictatorship.
*   **1798:** **Egyptian Campaign** (Napoleon). **War of Second Coalition** (Austria, Britain, Russia, Ottomans). French invade Switzerland, Rome, Naples (Parthenopean Republic).
*   **1799:** French defeats in Germany/Italy (Suvorov). Directory crisis.
*   **June 18, 1799 (30 Prairial VII):** **Coup of 30 Prairial**. Directors replaced (Sieyès enters).
*   **Nov 9–10, 1799 (18–19 Brumaire VIII):** **Coup of 18 Brumaire**. Napoleon + Sieyès + Ducos overthrow Directory. **Consulate** established. **Revolution effectively ends.**

## 4. LOCATIONS

### **Countries/Regions**
*   **France:** 83 Departments (1790).
*   **Austrian Netherlands (Belgium):** Annexed 1795.
*   **Left Bank of Rhine:** Annexed 1797 (Campo Formio).
*   **Savoy & Nice:** Annexed 1792.
*   **Vendée (West):** Counter-revolution heartland (Bocage terrain).
*   **Lyon, Marseille, Toulon, Bordeaux:** Federalist Revolt centers.
*   **German States / Italy / Switzerland / Egypt:** Theaters of Revolutionary Wars.

### **Cities & Buildings**
*   **Paris:**
    *   **Versailles:** *Château*, *Salle des Menus-Plaisirs* (Estates-General), *Jeu de Paume* (Tennis Court).
    *   **Tuileries Palace:** Royal residence (Oct 1789–Aug 1792). *Salle du Manège* (Assembly hall).
    *   **Bastille:** Fortress/Prison (Stormed July 14). Demolished immediately (Pierre-François Palloy sold souvenirs).
    *   **Hôtel de Ville:** Paris Commune HQ.
    *   **Palais-Royal:** Orléanist center, Camille Desmoulins speech (July 12), cafés, prostitution, gambling.
    *   **Place de la Révolution (now Concorde):** Guillotine site (Louis XVI, Marie Antoinette, Robespierre, Danton, etc.).
    *   **Champ de Mars:** Fête de la Fédération (1790), Massacre (1791), Festival of Supreme Being (1794).
    *   **Conciergerie:** Prison (Marie Antoinette, Robespierre held).
    *   **Pantheon (Ste-Geneviève):** Secular mausoleum (Mirabeau, Marat, Voltaire, Rousseau).
    *   **Jacobin Club:** Rue Saint-Honoré (former Dominican convent).
    *   **Cordeliers Club:** Rue de l'École de Médecine (Danton, Marat, Hébert).
*   **Vendée:** Cholet, Nantes (Carrier's *noyades* - mass drownings in Loire), Savenay.
*   **Lyon:** *Place Bellecour* (guillotine), *Brodeaux* (grapeshot executions).
*   **Toulon:** Port, Fort Mulgrave (Napoleon's siege).
*   **Valmy:** Windmill (Kellermann's command post).

### **Battlefields (Revolutionary Wars)**
*   **Valmy (1792):** Artillery duel, rain, mud.
*   **Jemappes (1792):** Dumouriez beats Austrians, Belgium conquered.
*   **Neerwinden (1793):** Dumouriez defeated, defects.
*   **Hondschoote / Wattignies (1793):** Relieved Dunkirk/Maubeuge.
*   **Fleurus (1794):** Jourdan beats Coburg; first military balloon (*l'Entreprenant*) observation.
*   **Italian Campaign (1796–97):** Montenotte, Lodi, Arcole, Rivoli.
*   **Pyramids (1798):** Napoleon vs Mamluks, Cairo.
*   **Nile/Aboukir (1798):** Nelson destroys French fleet.

## 5. MAJOR EVENTS: DETAILED ANALYSIS

### **5.1 Estates-General & National Assembly (May–July 1789)**
*   **What:** Transition from estates-based consultation to national sovereignty.
*   **Why:** Fiscal collapse forced King to call Estates-General; Third Estate refused to verify powers separately; deadlock on voting method.
*   **Who:** 1,139 deputies (291 Clergy, 270 Nobility, 578 Third). Key: Mirabeau, Sieyès, Bailly, Mounier.
*   **Immediate:** King recognizes National Assembly (June 27). Royal troops surround Paris.
*   **Long-term:** End of Absolutism; Constitution written; sovereignty resides in Nation.

### **5.2 Storming of the Bastille (July 14, 1789)**
*   **What:** Parisian crowd + *Gardes Françaises* deserters seize fortress for gunpowder.
*   **Why:** Fear of royal coup (troops, Necker dismissal); need arms; symbol of despotism.
*   **Who:** ~800–1,000 attackers (artisans, workers, soldiers); Governor Marquis de Launay (82 invalides, 32 Swiss).
*   **Immediate:** Launay killed; King recalls Necker; Lafayette/Bailly power in Paris; *Grande Peur* spreads; *émigrés* flee.
*   **Long-term:** **Symbol of Revolution**; popular violence legitimized as political tool; collapse of royal authority in provinces.

### **5.3 August Decrees & Declaration of Rights (Aug 1789)**
*   **What:** Legislative abolition of feudalism; statement of universal principles.
*   **Why:** Panic from *Grande Peur*; need to pacify countryside; ideological imperative.
*   **Who:** Noble/clerical deputies (Noailles, Aiguillon, Le Chapelier); Third Estate.
*   **Immediate:** Feudal dues legally abolished but "redeemable" (peasants must pay compensation); Tithes abolished unconditionally; Venality of office abolished.
*   **Long-term:** **Legal equality**; property rights enshrined; model for 19th C liberalism; "Redeemable" dues caused rural litigation until 1793 (finally abolished free).

### **5.4 Women's March / October Days (Oct 5–6, 1789)**
*   **What:** Women march to Versailles, invade Assembly/King's apartments, force Royal Family to Paris.
*   **Why:** Bread shortage (famine prices); fear King vetoing August Decrees/Declaration; rumors of "Orgy" at Versailles trampling tricolor.
*   **Who:** Market women (poissardes), Stanislas Maillard; National Guard (Lafayette) followed reluctantly.
*   **Immediate:** King accepts Decrees/Declaration; moves to Tuileries; Assembly moves to Paris (Manège); King becomes "Prisoner of the People".
*   **Long-term:** Revolution centered in Paris; King under surveillance; power shifts to Paris Commune/Sections.

### **5.5 Civil Constitution of the Clergy (July 1790)**
*   **What:** Church restructured as state department; bishops/priests elected; oath required.
*   **Why:** Financial need (sell church lands); Regalist/Gallican tradition; desire for civic religion.
*   **Who:** Assembly (Treilhard, Boisgelin); King (signed reluctantly); Pope Pius VI (condemned).
*   **Immediate:** Schism: *Jurors* (54% priests) vs *Refractory/Non-jurors* (46%); regional variance (West/South refractory); King's conscience crisis.
*   **Long-term:** **Religious Civil War** (Vendée, Chouannerie); alienated peasantry; drove King to Flight to Varennes; "Dechristianization" Year II.

### **5.6 Flight to Varennes (June 20–21, 1791)**
*   **What:** Royal family flees Tuileries disguised; caught at Varennes (postmaster Drouet recognizes Louis from assignat).
*   **Why:** King rejected Civil Constitution; felt prisoner; hoped to join Bouillé's army at Montmédy; negotiate Constitution from strength.
*   **Who:** Louis, Marie Antoinette, children, Madame de Tourzel; Bouillé (General); Drouet (postmaster); Barnave/Pétion/Latour-Maubourg (escort back).
*   **Immediate:** King suspended; *Padua Circular* (émigrés/Austria); Champ de Mars Petition (Republic); Le Chapelier Law (ban clubs).
*   **Long-term:** **Monarchy fatally wounded**; Republicanism becomes mainstream; Constitution 1791 based on lie (King accepted it under duress).

### **5.7 War Declaration & August 10 (April–Aug 1792)**
*   **What:** France declares war on Austria; Brunswick Manifesto; Storming of Tuileries; Monarchy falls.
*   **Why:** Girondins want to expose King/traitorous generals; King/Feuillants hope defeat restores power; Austria/Prussia threaten intervention.
*   **Who:** Legislative Assembly (Girondins: Brissot, Vergniaud); King (vetoes decrees on priests/émigrés); *Fédérés* (Marseillais song); Paris Sections/Commune (Pétion, Roederer); Swiss Guard.
*   **Immediate:** King suspended; Assembly calls Convention (Universal Male Suffrage); Lafayette flees; Prussians invade; September Massacres.
*   **Long-term:** **Republic proclaimed**; War becomes total/national; Terror machinery created; European Coalition expands.

### **5.8 September Massacres (Sept 2–7, 1792)**
*   **What:** Mobs kill prisoners in Paris jails (Abbaye, Carmes, Force, etc.).
*   **Why:** Fear of advancing enemy (Verdun fell Sept 2); fear of internal "fifth column" (refractory priests, Swiss guards); lack of judicial process; Marat incitement.
*   **Who:** *Sans-culottes*, *Marseillais* fédérés; Condoned by Commune (Danton, Marat, Manuel); Assembly/Ministers paralyzed.
*   **Immediate:** ~1,100–1,400 dead (many common criminals); Prussia halted at Valmy (Sept 20); Convention meets Sept 21.
*   **Long-term:** **Stain on Revolution**; showed power of Paris mob; precedent for extra-legal terror; foreign propaganda weapon.

### **5.9 King's Trial & Execution (Dec 1792 – Jan 1793)**
*   **What:** Convention tries "Louis Capet" for treason; votes Death (361/387/721); Executed Jan 21.
*   **Why:** Proof of Republic; eliminate symbol for counter-revolution; unite Montagnards/Girondins (temporarily); challenge Europe.
*   **Who:** Convention (President Vergniaud); Defense (Malesherbes, Tronchet, Desèze); Montagnards (Saint-Just, Robespierre - "Louis must die so the nation may live"); Girondins (appeal to people/maillet - failed).
*   **Immediate:** War with Britain/Spain/Dutch; Vendée uprising; Coalition solidifies.
*   **Long-term:** **Point of no return**; Regicide isolates France diplomatically; sanctifies Revolution for radicals.

### **5.10 Fall of the Girondins (May 31 – June 2, 1793)**
*   **What:** *Sans-culottes* + National Guard (Hanriot) surround Convention; 29 Girondin deputies arrested.
*   **Why:** Military disasters (Dumouriez defection); Girondin opposition to Paris dominance/Maximum; Federalist revolts brewing; Marat acquitted by Tribunal (April).
*   **Who:** Commune (Peyroux, Chaumette), Sections (Varlet, Roux), Hanriot (Guard); Convention (Barère, Couthon); Girondins (Brissot, Vergniaud, Buzot - escaped).
*   **Immediate:** Montagnard dictatorship; Constitution 1793 drafted; Federalist revolts erupt (Lyon, Marseille, Bordeaux, Caen, Normandy).
*   **Long-term:** **Centralized Terror**; elimination of moderate republican voice; "One and Indivisible Republic" enforced by bayonet.

### **5.11 Reign of Terror (Sept 1793 – July 1794) - Institutional Structure**
*   **Law of Suspects (Sept 17, 1793):** Defined suspects vaguely (enemies by conduct, relations, words, writings). Local Surveillance Committees arrest.
*   **Revolutionary Tribunal (Paris):** Fouquier-Tinville (Prosecutor); Herman (President). No appeal. Verdicts: Acquittal or Death.
*   **Representatives on Mission:** Proconsuls with unlimited power (Collot d'Herbois/Fouché Lyon; Carrier Nantes; Barras/Fréron Toulon/Marseille; Saint-Just/Le Bas Rhine Army).
*   **Committee of Public Safety (CPS):** 12 members (Robespierre, Saint-Just, Couthon, Carnot, Lindet, Barère, Billaud-Varenne, Collot d'Herbois...). Executive directory. Carnot = War (Organizer of Victory).
*   **Law of 14 Frimaire (Dec 1793):** Centralized CPS control; suppressed *armées révolutionnaires* (Hébertist); regulated Representatives.
*   **Law of 22 Prairial (June 1794):** "Great Terror". Removed defense counsel/witnesses; moral proof sufficient; CPS controls Tribunal lists. Executions/day in Paris: ~30 -> ~50+.

### **5.12 Dechristianization & Cults (Winter Year II)**
*   **Spontaneous/Forced:** Closing churches -> Temples of Reason; Priests forced to resign/marry; Street names changed (Saint -> Reason); Calendar decimalized (10-day week *décadi* kills Sunday).
*   **Cult of Reason (Hébert/Chaumette/Anacharsis Clootz):** Atheist, materialist. Festival Nov 10, 1793 (Notre Dame -> Temple of Reason).
*   **Cult of Supreme Being (Robespierre):** Deist, moralistic. "Nature's God". Festival June 8, 1794. Robespierre as pontiff.
*   **Conflict:** Robespierre saw atheism as aristocratic/immoral; needed religious sanction for Republic.

### **5.13 Fall of Robespierre (9–10 Thermidor / July 27–28, 1794)**
*   **What:** Convention votes to arrest Robespierre, Saint-Just, Couthon, Lebas, Hanriot; Commune attempts insurrection; Convention declares them outlaws; executed next day.
*   **Why:** Fear for own lives (Law of 22 Prairial); Robespierre isolated (attacked "factions" but named none); Military success (Fleurus) removed need for Terror; Personal conflicts (Fouché, Tallien, Barras, Vadier threatened).
*   **Who:** **The "Plain" (Marais)** + **Thermidorians** (Tallien, Bourdon de l'Oise, Fréron, Barras, Merlin, Thuriot, Lecointre). Hanriot (Commune/Guard) failed to act decisively.
*   **Immediate:** Jacobins purged; Tribunal reformed; Maximum lifted; *assignats* hyperinflation; White Terror (South).
*   **Long-term:** **End of radical democracy**; "Thermidorian Reaction" = bourgeois republicanism; Constitution Year III (Property franchise); Directory.

### **5.14 Coup of 18 Brumaire (Nov 9–10, 1799)**
*   **What:** Napoleon + Sieyès + Ducos overthrow Directory; Council of 500 routed by grenadiers; Consulate proclaimed.
*   **Why:** Directory corruption/weakness; Military reverses (1799); Sieyès wanted strong executive; Napoleon wanted power.
*   **Who:** Napoleon (General), Lucien Bonaparte (President of 500), Sieyès (Director), Murat (Grenadiers), Talleyrand (Foreign Min).
*   **Immediate:** "The Revolution is established on the principles with which it began." (Napoleon).
*   **Long-term:** **End of Revolutionary period**; Start of Consulate/Empire; Consolidation of Revolutionary gains (Code, Concordat, Bank, Prefecture).

## 6. MILITARY

### **6.1 Army Transformation**
*   **Old Royal Army:** Officer corps noble (4/4 quarters nobility required 1781). Soldiers: volunteers, substitutes, militia (lottery), foreigners (Swiss, German regiments). Harsh discipline (lash).
*   **Revolutionary Changes:**
    *   **1790:** Officer corps opened to all citizens (merit). Mass emigration of noble officers (~6,000 by 1792).
    *   **1791:** National Guard (bourgeois) distinct from Line Army.
    *   **1792:** *Fédérés* volunteers (short term, patriotic). "Amalgam" (1793): 1 old battalion + 2 volunteer battalions = Demi-brigade. Mixes experience/enthusiasm.
    *   **1793:** *Levée en masse* (Aug 23): All unmarried men 18–25 liable. ~750k–800k under arms (paper strength). Officer promotion rapid (Merit + Election early on).
    *   **Tactics:** *Ordre mixte* (Column + Skirmishers *tirailleurs* + Line). Bayonet charge (*attaque à la baïonnette*). Artillery excellence (Gribeauval system, Napoleon's concentration). Mobility (living off land, no supply trains).

### **6.3 Navies**
*   **French Navy:** Neglected by Revolution (officers emigrated/guillotined). Brest/Toulon fleets decayed.
*   **British Navy:** Dominant (Channel Fleet, Mediterranean). Blockade strangled French trade/colonies.
*   **Key Naval Events:** Glorious First of June (1794 - tactical Fr defeat, strategic grain convoy saved); Nile/Aboukir (1798 - Nelson annihilates Brueys); Bantry Bay (1796 - failed Irish invasion); Ireland 1798 (Humbert landed, surrendered).

### **6.4 Weapons & Equipment**
*   **Musket:** *Charleville Model 1777* (corrected An IX). .69 cal, smoothbore, flintlock. Rate: 2–3 rpm. Bayonet (socket) socket bayonet.
*   **Artillery:** Gribeauval System (4, 8, 12-pdr guns; 6-inch howitzer). Standardized, mobile, interchangeable parts. **Napoleon** = Master of artillery concentration.
*   **Cavalry:** Heavy (Cuirassiers, Carabiniers), Light (Hussars, Chasseurs-à-cheval, Dragoons). Poor horses early on (requisitioned).
*   **Uniforms:** **White** (Line infantry, Royal legacy) -> **Blue** (National Guard, then Line 1793+). *Sans-culottes*: Red cap (bonnet rouge), carmagnole jacket, striped trousers, pike.
*   **Balloon:** *L'Entreprenant* (Compagnie d'Aérostiers, Coutelle). Used at Fleurus, Mainz. First military aviation.

## 7. POLITICS: GOVERNMENTS, LAWS, TREATIES

### **7.1 Constitutional Timeline**
1.  **Absolute Monarchy** (pre-1789)
2.  **Constitutional Monarchy** (Sept 1791 – Aug 1792): Constitution 1791. Legislative Assembly (745). King + Suspensive Veto. Active Citizens (tax 3 days labor) vote for Electors (tax 10 days) -> Electors choose Deputies. Passive Citizens (poor) no vote.
3.  **National Convention / Republic** (Sept 1792 – Oct 1795): Universal Male Suffrage (21+, 1yr residence). **Girondin Phase** -> **Montagnard Phase (Terror)** -> **Thermidorian Phase**. Constitution 1793 (Democratic, never applied).
4.  **The Directory** (Nov 1795 – Nov 1799): Constitution Year III. **Bicameral**: Council of 500 (initiate laws), Council of Ancients (250, approve/reject). **5 Directors** (Executive, chosen by Ancients from 500's list). Property franchise (Direct tax = Elector). Annual rotation (1/5).
5.  **The Consulate** (Dec 1799): Constitution Year VIII. **Three Consuls** (Napoleon First Consul 10 yrs, then Life). Senate, Tribunate, Corps Législatif (rubber stamps). **End of popular sovereignty experiment.**

### **7.2 Key Laws & Decrees**
*   **August Decrees (1789):** Feudalism abolished.
*   **Declaration of Rights (1789):** Constitutional preamble.
*   **Civil Constitution of Clergy (1790):** Church subordination.
*   **Le Chapelier Law (June 1791):** Ban guilds/coalitions/strikes. *Laissez-faire*.
*   **Law of Suspects (Sept 1793):** Legal basis for Terror arrests.
*   **Law of 14 Frimaire (Dec 1793):** Centralization of CPS power.
*   **Ventôse Decrees (Feb–Mar 1794):** Confiscation of suspect property for poor (Saint-Just).
*   **Law of 22 Prairial (June 1794):** Great Terror procedure.
*   **Law of 3 Brumaire Year IV (Oct 1795):** Amnesty for "opinions" (White Terror moderation).
*   **Law of Hostages / Law of 22 Floréal Year VI (1798):** Directory repression of Jacobins/Royalists.
*   **Law of 19 Fructidor Year VII (1799):** Forced loan (impôt forcé) on rich.

### **7.3 Treaties & Diplomacy**
*   **Treaty of Basel (Apr 1795):** Prussia leaves Coalition; cedes Left Bank Rhine (secret); Spain cedes Santo Domingo, recognizes Republic.
*   **Treaty of The Hague (May 1795):** Batavian Republic (NL) ally; cedes Maastricht, Flanders; pays 100M florins.
*   **Treaty of Campo Formio (Oct 1797):** Austria recognizes French annexation of Belgium/Left Bank Rhine; Venice to Austria; Cisalpine/Ligurian Republics created.
*   **Treaty of Lunéville (Feb 1801):** Confirms Campo Formio (Second Coalition defeat).
*   **Concordat of 1801 (Napoleon/Pope Pius VII):** Ends schism; Catholicism "religion of majority"; State pays clergy; Pope accepts land loss. (Post-Rev but consequence).

### **7.4 Political Clubs (The "Machine")**
*   **Jacobins (Society of Friends of Constitution):** Nationwide network (~7,000 clubs). Radicalized 1790–93. Robespierre's base. Closed Nov 1794.
*   **Cordeliers (Society of Rights of Man):** Radical, populist, *sans-culotte*. Danton, Marat, Hébert. Dissolved 1794.
*   **Feuillants (1791):** Constitutional Monarchists split from Jacobins (Lafayette, Barnave, Lameth). Dissolved Aug 1792.
*   **Girondins:** Not a club, but loose grouping (Brissot, Roland, Vergniaud) meeting at *Salon de Madame Roland*. Federalist, war-hawks.
*   **Société des Républicaines Révolutionnaires (1793):** Women's club (Léon, Lacombe). Banned Oct 1793.

## 8. SOCIETY: DAILY LIFE, CULTURE, TECHNOLOGY, ECONOMY, RELIGION

### **8.1 Daily Life & Material Culture**
*   **Clothing:**
    *   *Aristocrats (pre-1789):* Silk, velvet, lace, powdered wigs, high heels, *habit à la française*.
    *   *Bourgeois:* Dark wool, simple cravats, own hair or simple wig.
    *   *Sans-culottes:* **Red Phrygian Cap (Bonnet Rouge)**, *Carmagnole* (short jacket), *Pantalon* (long striped trousers - vs aristocrat knee-breeches/culottes), *Sabots* (clogs) or rough shoes. Tricolor cockade mandatory (1792).
    *   *Women:* **Chemise à la reine** (muslin, simple) -> Revolutionary: Tricolor ribbons, red caps, simpler dresses. *Incroyables/Merveilleuses* (Directory): Extreme fashion (huge cravats, sheer gowns, caricatures).
*   **Food:** Bread (80% diet). Price fixed by *Maximum* (Year II). Meat rare for poor. Coffee/Chocolate/Sugar (colonial) expensive/blockaded. *Assignats* hyperinflation -> barter/common.
*   **Housing:** Paris: Overcrowded, tall buildings (5-6 floors), no sanitation, chimneys. Sections = administrative/political units (48).
*   **Time:** **Republican Calendar (Oct 1793)**. 12 Months (30 days) + 5/6 *Sansculottides*. 10-day week (*Décadi* = rest). Decimal time (10 hrs/day, 100 min/hr, 100 sec/min) - failed. Months: Vendémiaire, Brumaire, Frimaire, Nivôse, Pluviôse, Ventôse, Germinal, Floréal, Prairial, Messidor, Thermidor, Fructidor.

### **8.2 Culture & Propaganda**
*   **Print Culture Explosion:** ~1,500 newspapers (1789–93). *L'Ami du Peuple* (Marat), *Révolutions de France* (Desmoulins), *Le Père Duchesne* (Hébert - vulgar/slang), *Le Moniteur* (Official).
*   **Pamphlets:** *What is the Third Estate?* (Sieyès), *Rights of Man* (Paine - French trans), *Declaration of Rights of Woman* (Gouges).
*   **Theater/Opera:** Political allegories. *La Marseillaise* (Rouget de Lisle, 1792 - Army of Rhine). *Ça Ira*, *Carmagnole* (songs).
*   **Festivals:** Choreographed by David / Gossec / Voltaire. **Fête de la Fédération (1790)**, **Fête de la Raison (1793)**, **Fête de l'Être Suprême (1794)**. Secular liturgy. Altars of Fatherland.
*   **Art:** **Jacques-Louis David** (Official artist). *Oath of Tennis Court* (unfinished), *Death of Marat* (1793 - Pietà pose), *Oath of the Horatii* (pre-rev, republican virtue), *Sabine Women* (reconciliation). **Guillotine** = "National Razor" (equality in death). Dr. Guillotin / Antoine Louis / Schmidt (builder). Sanson family (executioners).

### **8.3 Technology & Science**
*   **Metric System:** Decreed 1791, defined 1799 (Meter = 1/10,000,000 quadrant of meridian; Kilogram = 1dm³ water). Scientists: Laplace, Lagrange, Lavoisier (guillotined 1794), Monge, Condorcet.
*   **Chemistry:** Lavoisier (Oxygen, conservation of mass). *Ferme Générale* tax farmer -> Victim.
*   **Ballooning:** Montgolfier (1783). Military use (Coutelle) 1794.
*   **Telegraph:** **Chappe Semaphore** (1793–94). Paris-Lille line (1794). Towers with movable arms. Speed: ~1 symbol/min. First telecommunications network.
*   **Manufacturing:** Interchangeable parts (Gribeauval/Blanc - muskets). Le Chapelier Law abolished guilds -> factory growth hindered by war/inflation.

### **8.4 Economy & Finance**
*   **Assignats (Paper Money):**
    *   Dec 1789: 400M issue (backed by *biens nationaux* - Church land).
    *   1790: Legal tender. Interest bearing -> non-interest.
    *   1791–92: Issues explode (war/deficit). Counterfeiting (British/Pass Through).
    *   1793: *Maximum* (Price/Wage controls). *Law of General Maximum* (Sept 29, 1793). Grain requisitioning.
    *   1795: Controls lifted (Thermidor). Hyperinflation: 1790: 100% value. 1795: 1% value. 1796: *Assignats* burned publicly (Place Vendôme). *Mandats territoriaux* fail.
    *   1797: Bankruptcy (Law of 30 Sept - "Two-thirds" repudiated). Metallic currency restored.
*   **Taxation:** *Contribution foncière* (land), *mobilière* (movables), *patente* (business), *portes et fenêtres* (windows). Direct taxes only (Year III). Efficient collection via Prefects (later).
*   **Trade:** British blockade -> Continental System (later). Loss of colonies (Haiti revolution 1791, Britain occupies Caribbean/Indian Ocean).

### **8.5 Religion: The Schism & Dechristianization**
*   **Constitutional Church:** Elected bishops (Talleyrand -> Gobel), married priests. State paid. Weak popular support.
*   **Refractory Church:** Underground. Mass in barns/woods. "Non-juring" priests. Pilgrimages. Center of Counter-Revolution (Vendée).
*   **Dechristianization (Year II):** Spontaneous (Hébertists) + Official (CPS).
    *   Closure of churches -> Temples of Reason.
    *   Forced abdications (Gobel Nov 1793).
    *   Civic cults (Reason, Supreme Being).
    *   Republican Calendar (remove Saints/Sunday).
    *   **Robespierre reverses:** "Atheism is aristocratic." Cult of Supreme Being mandatory. Freedom of worship restored (Dec 1794). Separation Church/State (1795).

## 9. NUMBERS & STATISTICS

### **Population**
*   **1789:** ~28 Million (France proper).
*   **1793:** ~27 Million (war losses, emigration).
*   **Paris:** ~600,000 – 650,000.
*   **Lyon:** ~150,000. **Marseille:** ~120,000. **Bordeaux:** ~100,000.

### **Army Sizes**
*   **1789 (Royal):** ~180,000 (incl. foreigners).
*   **1792 (Legislative):** ~300,000 (Line + Volunteers).
*   **1793 (Levée en masse):** Paper strength **~750,000 – 800,000**. Effective field armies ~450,000.
*   **1794 (Peak):** ~730,000 effectives (Carnot).
*   **1799 (Directory):** ~300,000–400,000.

### **Casualties (Revolutionary Wars 1792–1802)**
*   **French Military Dead:** ~200,000–250,000 (battle + disease).
*   **Allied Military Dead:** ~150,000–200,000.
*   **Vendée Civil War:** **170,000 – 250,000** total (Combatants + Civilians: Republicans ~30k, Royalists ~200k+). Genocide debate (Reynald Secher vs mainstream historians).
*   **September Massacres:** ~1,200–1,400 (Paris).
*   **Revolutionary Tribunal (Paris):** ~2,600 executed (official records).
*   **Total Terror Executions (France):** **~16,000 – 17,000** (official death sentences). **~10,000–12,000** died in prison/summary executions (Nantes drownings, Lyon grapeshot, Marseille). **Total ~25,000–40,000** violent deaths attributed to Terror justice.
*   **Émigrés:** ~150,000–200,000 fled (1789–1814). 60% Nobles, 25% Clergy, rest others.

### **Economic Data**
*   **National Debt 1789:** ~4 Billion *livres*.
*   **Annual Revenue 1789:** ~500 Million *livres*.
*   **Debt Service:** ~50% Revenue.
*   **Assignats Issued:** ~45 Billion *livres* (face value) by 1796.
*   **Church Land Seized:** ~6.5 Million hectares (~10% France). Sold in large lots (benefited bourgeois/peasants with capital).
*   **Grain Prices (Paris):** 1789: ~30-40 *sols*/setier. 1793: ~60-80 (controls). 1795: ~500+ (free market).

### **Distances**
*   Paris to Versailles: **21 km** (March of Women).
*   Paris to Varennes: **~200 km** (Flight).
*   Paris to Lyon: **~470 km** (Federalist repression).
*   Paris to Nantes: **~385 km** (Carrier's drownings).
*   Valmy to Paris: **~200 km**.
*   Toulon to Paris: **~830 km**.

## 10. INTERESTING VISUAL DETAILS (FOR DOCUMENTARY IMAGERY)

### **Architecture & Settings**
*   **Versailles:** Hall of Mirrors, Royal Opera (Estates-General), Petit Trianon (Queen's Hamlet - rustic fantasy), King's Bedchamber (lever/coucher ceremonies).
*   **Paris Streets:** Narrow, muddy, cobblestones, open sewers (kens), *réverbères* (oil lamps), barricades (paving stones, carts, furniture - 1789, 1792, 1795).
*   **Bastille:** 8 Towers (30m high), moat, drawbridge, governor's lodge. Demolition site (Palloy's entrepreneurs selling model bastilles, stones for Pont de la Concorde).
*   **Tuileries Gardens:** Manège (riding school -> Assembly), Salle des Machines (Convention), King's apartments overlooking garden.
*   **Place de la Révolution (Concorde):** Guillotine (red painted), *tricolor* flag, statue of Liberty (plaster), crowds on *bancs*, *tricoteuses* (knitting women), Sanson's cart (red/black).
*   **Prisons:** Conciergerie (Gothic vaults, "Marie Antoinette's cell"), Abbaye, Saint-Lazare, Force. Dark, crowded, straw pallets.
*   **Vendée Bocage:** High hedgerows, sunken lanes, winding rivers, windmills (signal posts), châteaux burning, white cockades (Royalists) vs Tricolor.

### **Clothing & Appearance**
*   **Deputies:** 1789: Clergy (cassock), Nobility (velvet, sword, plumed hat), Third (black wool, simple). 1793: Montagnards (plain dark, *bonnet rouge* in Club), Girondins (long hair, *habit à l'anglaise*).
*   **Sans-Culottes:** Red cap (wool/felt), carmagnole (short, coarse cloth), striped trousers, pike/sabre, tricolor cockade on cap/lapel. Women: Red cap, pike, musket.
*   **National Guard:** Blue coat, white lapels/cuffs, red epaulettes (1789), tricolor plume. Later: Standard Line Infantry blue (1793+).
*   **Line Infantry (1793+):** Blue coat (habit-veste), white turnbacks, red collar/cuffs, white gaiters/piping, shako (replacing bicorne 1790s) with tricolor cockade/plume. Greatcoat (capote) rolled.
*   **Officers:** Gold epaulettes (rank), gorget (early), sash, boots, sword. *Amalgam* = mismatched uniforms (White veterans + Blue volunteers).
*   **Women:** *Chemise à la reine* (white muslin, sash), straw hat (bergère). Revolutionary: Tricolor ribbons, red cap, *pantalon* (rare). Directory: *Incroyables* (huge cravat "tour de cou", tight coat, monocle, crooked hat), *Merveilleuses* (sheer Greek tunics, bare arms, sandals, wigs).

### **Symbols & Iconography**
*   **Tricolor Flag:** Blue-White-Red (Paris militia + King). Vertical stripes (1794). Naval ensign distinct.
*   **Phrygian Cap (Bonnet Rouge):** Red, soft, peaked. Symbol of freed slave (Roman *pileus*). On pikes, flagpoles, statues.
*   **Fasces:** Bundle of rods + axe. Unity/Authority. On seals, architecture, officer's epaulettes.
*   **Liberty Tree:** Planted in village squares (Poplar/Oak). Dancing around.
*   **Guillotine:** "Louisette" / "National Razor". Two uprights, crossbeam, weighted blade (oblique 45°), bascule (tipping board), basket. Blood trough.
*   **Marianne:** Female allegory of Republic. Phrygian cap, exposed breast (nurturing liberty), fasces. Statues, seals, busts in town halls.
*   **Cockade:** Round, pleated ribbon. Tricolor mandatory 1792. White (Royalist), Green (early hope), Black (counter-rev).

### **Weapons Close-up**
*   **Charleville Musket:** Brass furniture, ramrod pipes, bayonet lug. Loading: Paper cartridge (ball + powder) -> tear -> prime pan -> pour -> ball -> ram -> shoulder.
*   **Pike:** 3-4m ash shaft, steel leaf blade. *Sans-culotte* weapon. "The weapon of the people."
*   **Officer's Sabre:** Curved, brass hilt, leather scabbard.
*   **Gribeauval Cannon:** Bronze barrel (4/8/12lb), elevating screw, trails, limber. Ammunition: Solid shot, Canister (tin cans of musket balls - "hail of death"), Spherical case (Shrapnel - later).

## 11. CONSEQUENCES & LEGACY

### **11.1 Immediate Consequences (1799–1815)**
*   **Political:** End of Bourbon Monarchy (until 1814/15). Rise of Napoleon (Consulate -> Empire). **Centralized State** (Prefects, Cadastre, Civil Code, Bank of France, University, Legion of Honor).
*   **Legal:** **Abolition of Privilege** (Feudalism, Venality, Guilds, Provinces). **Equality before Law**. Secularization of Civil Status (Birth/Marriage/Death = Mairie, not Church). Divorce legalized (1792, restricted 1803). Inheritance equality (partible).
*   **Religious:** **Concordat 1801**: Catholic Church recognized but state-controlled. Freedom of worship for Protestants/Jews. Refractory clergy rehabilitated.
*   **Territorial:** France expanded to "Natural Frontiers" (Rhine, Alps, Pyrenees). Belgium, Left Bank Rhine, Savoy, Nice annexed. Sister Republics (Batavian, Helvetic, Cisalpine, Ligurian, Parthenopean).
*   **Social:** **Nobility abolished** (1790, 1793). Titles banned. *Émigré* property sold (*biens nationaux*). **New Elite:** "Notables" (Wealthy landowners, bureaucrats, officers, bourgeois). Meritocracy (Legion of Honor 1802).

### **11.2 Long-Term Historical Impact (19th–21st Century)**
*   **Ideological Legacy:**
    *   **Liberalism:** Constitutions, Rights of Man, Rule of Law, Property Rights, Representative Government (struggle for suffrage).
    *   **Nationalism:** "La Nation" = Sovereign. *Levée en masse* = People's War. Spread by French armies -> German/Italian/Polish nationalism.
    *   **Republicanism:** "Liberté, Égalité, Fraternité". 1848, 1870 (Third Republic), 1944, 1958 (Fifth Republic).
    *   **Socialism/Communism:** Babeuf (Conspiracy of Equals) -> Marx (analyzes 1789 as Bourgeois Revolution). "Gracchus" Babeuf = First modern communist.
    *   **Secularism (Laïcité):** 1905 Law separation Church/State rooted in 1790/1794/1795 struggles.
*   **Administrative Model:** **Department/Prefect** system copied globally (Napoleonic Code + Prefects = modern bureaucracy). Spain, Italy, Germany (Rhineland), Latin America, Poland, Egypt, Japan (Meiji).
*   **Legal Code:** **Code Civil (1804)** = Codification of Revolutionary gains (Equality, Property, Secular Contract). Basis for law in ~70 countries (Quebec, Louisiana, Latin America, Europe, Middle East, Asia).
*   **Metric System:** Global standard (SI Units).
*   **Warfare:** **Total War / Nation in Arms**. Conscription (Jourdan Law 1798). Mass armies, patriotic mobilization, strategic depth. Clausewitz analyzes Revolutionary/Napoleonic war.
*   **Human Rights:** Universal Declaration (1948) descends from 1789 Declaration (via 1793, 1848).

### **11.3 Historiographical Debates (Key for Documentary Context)**
*   **Marxist/Classic (Lefebvre, Soboul, Hobsbawm):** "Bourgeois Revolution". Rise of Capitalism. Class struggle (Aristocracy vs Bourgeoisie). *Sans-culottes* = proto-proletariat. Terror = Necessity of war/defense.
*   **Revisionist (Furet, Richet, Schama):** **Political Culture** focus. Revolution as **tragedy/catastrophe**. Language of "Will of the People" -> Totalitarianism (Terror inherent in 1789 sovereignty). Reject class analysis; emphasize contingency, violence, rhetoric.
*   **Post-Revisionist (Hunt, Tackett, Andress, Jones):** Synthesis. **Culture/Politics/Emotions**. Fear/Rumor/Panic as drivers (Tackett *When the King Took Flight*, *The Coming of the Terror*). Gender (Women's role). Colonial dimension (Haiti/Slavery). Atlantic context.
*   **Colonial/Slavery:** **1794 Abolition of Slavery** (First Empire). Toussaint Louverture / Haitian Revolution (1791–1804) as *part* of French Revolution. Napoleon reinstates slavery (1802). 1848 Final Abolition.

### **11.4 Legacy Summary for Visuals**
*   **Bastille Day (July 14):** Military Parade (Champs-Élysées), Fireworks, Bals des Pompiers. National Holiday since 1880.
*   **Marseillaise:** National Anthem (1879).
*   **Marianne:** Bust in every Mairie (Town Hall). Stamps, Coins, Logos.
*   **Tricolor:** Flag of France, Italy (inspired), Ireland (inspired), Mexico, Romania, etc.
*   **Panthéon:** "Aux Grands Hommes La Patrie Reconnaissante". Voltaire, Rousseau, Hugo, Zola, Curie, Malraux, Simone Veil.
*   **Language:** "Citoyen/Citoyenne", "Terroriste", "Réactionnaire", "Gauche/Droite" (Assembly seating 1789), "Saboter" (sabot/clog -> sabotage), "Vandalisme" (Abbé Grégoire 1794).
