# Comprehensive Historical Research: The Hundred Years' War (1337–1453)

---

## 1. BACKGROUND

### 1.1 Historical Context & Dynastic Origins
*   **Feudal Complexity:** The King of England was a vassal of the King of France for the Duchy of Aquitaine (Gascony). This created a structural conflict: the English King was a sovereign ruler in England but a subordinate duke in France, owing homage and military service to the French Crown.
*   **Capetian Extinction (1328):** Death of Charles IV of France (last direct Capetian) without a male heir.
*   **Salic Law vs. English Common Law:**
    *   **French Position:** Invoked *Salic Law* (Lex Salica) – inheritance through male line only. Nearest male relative: **Philip of Valois** (cousin of Charles IV), became **Philip VI**.
    *   **English Position (Edward III):** Claimed throne through his mother, **Isabella of France** (daughter of Philip IV). Argued Salic Law applied only to Salian Frank lands, not the Crown of France. Claim: "King of France and England."
*   **Territorial Friction:** **Gascony/Aquitaine** – wealthy wine-producing region. French kings sought to consolidate royal domain; English kings resisted French encroachment (judicial appeals to Paris Parlement, military pressure).
*   **Economic Interdependence & Conflict:** **Flanders** (County of Flanders, nominally French vassal) relied on English wool for its cloth industry. English embargoes on wool exports pressured Flanders; French pressure on Flanders to remain loyal created a triangular diplomatic crisis.
*   **Scottish Alliance (Auld Alliance):** France supported Scotland against England (David II vs. Edward Balliol/Edward III). English intervention in Scotland (Halidon Hill, 1333) gave Philip VI cause to support Scottish raids and threaten Gascony.

### 1.2 Political Situation (Pre-1337)
*   **France:** Philip VI (Valois) consolidating power; strong feudal nobility; Parlement of Paris asserting judicial supremacy over Gascony.
*   **England:** Edward III young but assertive; Parliament growing in power (control of taxation); rising national identity.
*   **Holy Roman Empire:** Louis IV (Wittelsbach) allied with Edward III against Papacy/France (1337–1341), providing diplomatic cover for Edward's claim.

### 1.3 Economic Situation
*   **England:** Wool trade = primary revenue source (customs duties). War financing relied on Parliamentary grants (tenths/fifteenths), wool subsidies, and loans from Italian bankers (Bardi/Peruzzi – collapsed 1340s).
*   **France:** Larger population/tax base (taille, gabelle, aides), but collection difficult in feudal periphery. Currency manipulation (debasement) common.
*   **Flanders:** Urban revolution (Artevelde regime) seeking neutrality/English alliance.

### 1.4 Social Situation
*   **Chivalric Culture:** Shared aristocratic culture across Channel (tournaments, heraldry, romance literature). War initially fought by "brothers in arms."
*   **Rise of Commons:** English longbowmen (yeoman class); French urban militias (limited effectiveness); Jacquerie (1358) & Ciompi Revolt (1378) show social stress.
*   **Demographics:** Pre-Black Death population peak (France ~15-20M, England ~4-5M). Post-1348 catastrophic decline.

### 1.5 Religious Situation
*   **Avignon Papacy (1309–1377):** Popes resident in Avignon (French influence). Attempted mediation (Clement VI, Innocent VI).
*   **Western Schism (1378–1417):** Two (later three) Popes. France supported Avignon Pope (Clement VII/Benedict XIII); England supported Roman Pope (Urban VI/Boniface IX). War became religiously polarized.

## 2. TIMELINE: KEY DATES & PHASES

### Phase 1: Edwardian War (1337–1360)
*   **1337, May 24:** Philip VI confiscates Duchy of Aquitaine (Great Council of Paris). *Formal start.*
*   **1337, October:** Edward III formally claims title "King of France" (Ghent).
*   **1340, June 24:** **Battle of Sluys** – English naval victory; control of Channel.
*   **1346, August 26:** **Battle of Crécy** – English longbow dominance; Calais besieged.
*   **1347, August:** Fall of **Calais** (English possession until 1558).
*   **1348–1350:** **Black Death** pauses major campaigning.
*   **1356, September 19:** **Battle of Poitiers** – King John II captured; French collapse.
*   **1358:** **Jacquerie** (peasant revolt) & **Étienne Marcel** revolt in Paris.
*   **1360, May 8:** **Treaty of Brétigny** – Edward III renounces French crown for full sovereignty over enlarged Aquitaine (1/3 France), Calais, Ponthieu. Ransom for John II: 3M écus.

### Phase 2: Caroline War (1369–1389) – French Resurgence
*   **1369:** Charles V (the Wise) declares war (Edward III fails to pay homage for Aquitaine; Black Prince's rule in Aquitaine provokes appeals to Paris Parlement).
*   **1370, December:** **Sack of Limoges** (Black Prince).
*   **1372, June 22–23:** **Battle of La Rochelle** – Castilian/French fleet destroys English fleet; loss of naval supremacy.
*   **1370s:** **Bertrand du Guesclin** (Constable) uses *fabian strategy* (avoid pitch battles, harass supply lines, siege warfare). Recaptures most English gains.
*   **1376:** Death of **Edward the Black Prince**.
*   **1377:** Death of **Edward III**; Richard II (minor) succeeds.
*   **1380:** Death of **Charles V** & **Du Guesclin**; Charles VI (minor) succeeds.
*   **1389, June:** **Truce of Leulinghem** (extended to 28 years later).

### Phase 3: Lancastrian War (1415–1429) – English High Tide
*   **1407:** Assassination of **Louis of Orléans** by **John the Fearless** (Duke of Burgundy) → **Armagnac–Burgundian Civil War**.
*   **1415, August 13:** Henry V lands at **Harfleur**.
*   **1415, October 25:** **Battle of Agincourt** – Decisive English victory.
*   **1417–1419:** Henry V conquers **Normandy** (Rouen falls 1419).
*   **1419, September 10:** Assassination of **John the Fearless** at Montereau (by Armagnacs/Dauphin's men) → Burgundy allies with England.
*   **1420, May 21:** **Treaty of Troyes** – Henry V marries Catherine of Valois; recognized as Heir/Regent of France; disinherits Dauphin Charles.
*   **1422, August 31 / October 21:** Deaths of **Henry V** & **Charles VI**. **Henry VI** (infant) King of England & France (Dual Monarchy). **Charles VII** (Dauphin) claims throne in Bourges ("King of Bourges").
*   **1428, October – 1429, May:** **Siege of Orléans**.

### Phase 4: War of the Maid / French Reconquest (1429–1453)
*   **1429, May 8:** Joan of Arc lifts **Siege of Orléans**.
*   **1429, June 18:** **Battle of Patay** – French heavy cavalry destroys English longbow corps.
*   **1429, July 17:** **Coronation of Charles VII at Reims**.
*   **1430, May 23:** Capture of **Joan of Arc** at Compiègne (by Burgundians).
*   **1431, May 30:** Execution of Joan of Arc (Rouen).
*   **1435, September 21:** **Congress of Arras / Treaty of Arras** – **Burgundy defects** to Charles VII (crucial turning point). Paris falls 1436.
*   **1444:** **Truce of Tours** (Henry VI marries Margaret of Anjou).
*   **1449, March:** French breach truce; reconquest of **Normandy**.
*   **1450, April 15:** **Battle of Formigny** – French artillery/field fortifications defeat English army in Normandy.
*   **1451, June–August:** French reconquest of **Gascony** (Bordeaux, Bayonne fall).
*   **1452, October:** Bordeaux revolts; English expedition under **John Talbot** retakes city.
*   **1453, July 17:** **Battle of Castillon** – French artillery decimates Talbot's relief army. Talbot killed. Bordeaux surrenders October 19. *War ends (no formal treaty).*

## 3. IMPORTANT PEOPLE

### ENGLAND

#### **Edward III (1312–1377)**
*   **Role:** King of England (1327–1377), Claimant King of France.
*   **Goals:** Secure Gascony sovereignty; Press claim to French throne (leverage); Domestic unity via foreign war.
*   **Actions:** Declared claim 1337; Sluys, Crécy, Calais; Order of the Garter (1348); Brétigny Treaty.
*   **Relationships:** Son of Edward II/Isabella of France; Father of Black Prince, John of Gaunt, Edmund of Langley; Allies: Louis IV HRE, Flemish towns (Artevelde).
*   **Influence:** Transformed England into military power; Parliament's power grew via war taxation; Dynastic splits (Lancaster/York) stem from his sons.

#### **Edward, the Black Prince (1330–1376)**
*   **Role:** Prince of Wales, Aquitaine; Heir Apparent.
*   **Goals:** Independent rule in Aquitaine; Military glory; Support father's claim.
*   **Actions:** Victory at Poitiers (1356); Ruled Principality of Aquitaine (1362–1372); Spanish campaign (Nájera 1367); Sack of Limoges (1370).
*   **Relationships:** Son of Edward III; Husband of Joan of Kent; Father of Richard II; Brother of John of Gaunt.
*   **Influence:** Epitome of chivalry; Death before father caused minority of Richard II; Brutality in Aquitaine alienated Gascon nobility.

#### **Henry V (1386–1422)**
*   **Role:** King of England (1413–1422), Heir/Regent of France (Troyes).
*   **Goals:** Conquer France "by right"; Unite English nobility; Secure Lancastrian dynasty.
*   **Actions:** Agincourt (1415); Normandy conquest (1417–19); Treaty of Troyes (1420); Siege of Meaux (1422).
*   **Relationships:** Son of Henry IV (Bolingbroke); Married Catherine de Valois; Brothers: Bedford, Gloucester; Allies: Burgundy (Philip the Good).
*   **Influence:** Near-total victory; Dual Monarchy concept; Early death doomed English cause; Administrative/military genius.

#### **John, Duke of Bedford (1389–1435)**
*   **Role:** Regent of France for Henry VI (1422–1435).
*   **Goals:** Maintain English France; Govern justly to win loyalty.
*   **Actions:** Victory at Verneuil (1424); Administration of Paris/Normandy; Treaty of Amiens (1423) with Burgundy/Brittany; Married Anne of Burgundy.
*   **Relationships:** Brother of Henry V; Uncle of Henry VI; Husband of Anne (sister of Philip the Good), then Jacquetta of Luxembourg.
*   **Influence:** "Good Duke John"; Held English France together; Death + Burgundian defection (Arras 1435) = collapse.

#### **John Talbot, Earl of Shrewsbury (1384–1453)**
*   **Role:** English Commander in France (1420s–1453).
*   **Goals:** Defend English Gascony; Personal honor/wealth.
*   **Actions:** "English Achilles"; Victories at Verneuil, Patay (defeated), Siege of Orléans (defeated); Reconquest of Bordeaux (1452); Killed at Castillon.
*   **Influence:** Last great English captain; Death symbolizes end of English military tradition in France.

### FRANCE

#### **Philip VI (1293–1350)**
*   **Role:** First Valois King (1328–1350).
*   **Goals:** Secure Valois legitimacy; Confiscate Aquitaine; Crush Flemish rebels.
*   **Actions:** Confiscation 1337; Defeats at Sluys, Crécy; Bought off English at Calais; Dauphiné purchase (1349).
*   **Influence:** Established Valois dynasty; Financial/exchequer reforms (Chambre des Comptes).

#### **John II "The Good" (1319–1364)**
*   **Role:** King of France (1350–1364).
*   **Goals:** Restore royal authority; Ransom payment; Chivalric idealism.
*   **Actions:** Captured at Poitiers (1356); 4 years captivity in London; Treaty of Brétigny; Returned to captivity (honor) 1364, died London.
*   **Relationships:** Son of Philip VI; Father of Charles V, Louis I of Anjou, John of Berry, Philip the Bold (Burgundy).
*   **Influence:** Captivity caused constitutional crisis (Estates General, Étienne Marcel); Ransom bankrupted France.

#### **Charles V "The Wise" (1338–1380)**
*   **Role:** King of France (1364–1380); Dauphin/Regent 1356–1364.
*   **Goals:** Recover territory without pitched battles; Centralize administration/finance; Legitimize Valois.
*   **Actions:** Appointed Du Guesclin Constable; Fabian strategy; Recaptured Aquitaine/Poitou/Normandy; Reformed coinage/taxation (permanent *taille*); Built Bastille/Louvre; Library at Louvre.
*   **Relationships:** Son of John II; Brothers: Anjou, Berry, Burgundy; Enemy: Charles the Bad (Navarre); Ally: Olivier de Clisson.
*   **Influence:** Architect of French recovery; Created standing army (compagnies d'ordonnance) basis; Financial independence from Estates.

#### **Bertrand du Guesclin (c.1320–1380)**
*   **Role:** Constable of France (1370–1380).
*   **Goals:** Serve Charles V; Expel English; Personal advancement.
*   **Actions:** Breton War of Succession (Auray 1364 captive); Spanish campaign (Nájera 1367 captive); Constable 1370; Reconquest of Poitou, Saintonge, Normandy; Siege warfare expert.
*   **Relationships:** Loyal to Charles V; Rival: Olivier de Clisson; Married Tiphaine Raguenel.
*   **Influence:** Master of "small war" (guerre de course); Symbol of meritocratic rise; Body laid in state at St. Denis (royal honor).

#### **Charles VI "The Mad" (1368–1422)**
*   **Role:** King of France (1380–1422).
*   **Goals:** Rule personally; Manage factions.
*   **Actions:** Madness onset 1392; Regency of Uncles (Berry, Burgundy); Armagnac-Burgundian Civil War; Treaty of Troyes (disinherited son).
*   **Relationships:** Son of Charles V; Wife Isabeau of Bavaria; Brother Louis of Orléans; Uncle Philip the Bold/John the Fearless; Son Charles VII (disinherited).
*   **Influence:** Madness caused power vacuum → Civil War → English conquest.

#### **Louis I, Duke of Orléans (1372–1407)**
*   **Role:** Brother of Charles VI; Leader Armagnac faction.
*   **Goals:** Control King/Government; Wealth/Patronage.
*   **Actions:** Regency rivalry with Philip the Bold/John the Fearless; Taxation policies; Assassinated 1407.
*   **Relationships:** Brother of Charles VI; Father of Charles of Orléans; Enemy: John the Fearless.

#### **John the Fearless, Duke of Burgundy (1371–1419)**
*   **Role:** Duke of Burgundy (1404–1419); Leader Burgundian faction.
*   **Goals:** Control French government; Protect Burgundian autonomy/expansion (Low Countries); Destroy Armagnacs.
*   **Actions:** Assassination of Louis of Orléans (1407); Justified by Jean Petit (tyrannicide); Cabochien Revolt (1413); Alliance with Henry V (1419); Assassinated at Montereau.
*   **Relationships:** Son of Philip the Bold; Father of Philip the Good; Enemy: Armagnacs/Dauphin Charles; Ally: Henry V (briefly).

#### **Charles VII "The Victorious" (1403–1461)**
*   **Role:** Dauphin (1417), King of France (1422–1461).
*   **Goals:** Survive; Regain throne; Expel English; Centralize monarchy.
*   **Actions:** Court at Bourges/Chinon; Joan of Arc (1429); Coronation Reims; Treaty of Arras (1435); Pragmatic Sanction of Bourges (1438); Standing army (1445); Normandy/Gascony reconquest.
*   **Relationships:** Son of Charles VI/Isabeau; Disinherited by Troyes; Mother-in-law: Yolande of Aragon; Mistress: Agnès Sorel; Advisors: Georges de La Trémoille, Arthur de Richemont, Pierre Cauchon (later), Jacques Cœur.
*   **Influence:** Created modern French state; Permanent taxation; Standing army; Ended English presence (except Calais).

#### **Joan of Arc (Jeanne d'Arc) (c.1412–1431)**
*   **Role:** Peasant visionary; Military leader; Symbol.
*   **Goals:** "Drive the English out"; Crown Charles at Reims.
*   **Actions:** Vaucouleurs → Chinon (Feb 1429); Orléans relief (May 1429); Loire Campaign (Jargeau, Meung, Beaugency, Patay); Coronation Reims (July 1429); Failed Paris attack; Capture Compiègne (May 1430); Trial/Execution Rouen (May 1431).
*   **Relationships:** Voices (St. Michael, Catherine, Margaret); Charles VII (dauphin/king); Captains: La Hire, Xaintrailles, Dunois, Alençon; Enemies: Bedford, Talbot, Cauchon (Bishop of Beauvais).
*   **Influence:** Psychological turning point; Legitimized Charles VII; National saint/martyr; Rehabilitation trial 1456.

#### **Philip the Good, Duke of Burgundy (1396–1467)**
*   **Role:** Duke of Burgundy (1419–1467).
*   **Goals:** Avenge father (Montereau); Secure Burgundian state (Flanders, Artois, Picardy, Luxembourg); Autonomy from France.
*   **Actions:** Alliance with England (Treaty of Troyes/Amiens); Governed Paris/North France for Henry VI; Congress of Arras (1435) – switched sides; Praguerie revolt (1440); Wars vs. Liège/Ghent.
*   **Relationships:** Son of John the Fearless; Father of Charles the Bold; Sister Anne (married Bedford); Wife Isabella of Portugal; Allies: Bedford, Henry V/VI (until 1435), Charles VII (after 1435).
*   **Influence:** His defection (Arras) made French victory inevitable; Burgundian state became major European power.

#### **Arthur de Richemont, Constable (1393–1458)**
*   **Role:** Constable of France (1425–1458); Duke of Brittany.
*   **Goals:** Reform French army; Expel English; Breton independence.
*   **Actions:** Battle of Patay; Reform of *compagnies d'ordonnance* (1445); Siege of Meaux, Saint-Denis; Battle of Formigny; Entry into Bordeaux (1453).
*   **Relationships:** Brother of Duke of Brittany; Ally then rival of La Trémoille; Friend of Charles VII (later).
*   **Influence:** Professionalized French army; Key commander in reconquest.

### OTHER KEY FIGURES
*   **Jacob van Artevelde (c.1290–1345):** Captain of Ghent; Allied Flanders with England (1340); Killed in riot.
*   **Étienne Marcel (d.1358):** Provost of Merchants Paris; Led bourgeois reform movement 1357–58; Allied with Charles the Bad; Killed defending Paris gates.
*   **Charles the Bad, King of Navarre (1332–1387):** Claimant to French throne; Held lands in Normandy/Paris; Chronic troublemaker; Died horribly (burned in brandy-soaked sheets).
*   **Owain Glyndŵr (c.1359–c.1415):** Welsh rebel (1400–1415); Allied with France; Diverted English resources.
*   **Jean de Bueil (1406–1477):** "Le Fléau des Anglais"; Author *Le Jouvencel* (treatise on warfare).
*   **Jacques Cœur (c.1395–1456):** Financier; Master of Mint; Funded Charles VII's reconquest; Arrested 1451.
*   **Jean Bureau (c.1390–1463) & Gaspard Bureau:** Masters of Artillery; Designed French artillery park; Victories at Formigny, Castillon.
*   **Pierre Cauchon (1371–1442):** Bishop of Beauvais; Pro-English; Presided over Joan of Arc trial.

## 4. LOCATIONS

### COUNTRIES / REGIONS
*   **Kingdom of France:** Core theatre. North (Île-de-France, Picardy, Artois), West (Normandy, Maine, Anjou, Brittany), Southwest (Aquitaine, Gascony, Poitou), East (Burgundy, Champagne, Dauphiné).
*   **Kingdom of England:** Base of operations; Source of men/money/wool. South coast ports (Southampton, Portsmouth, Sandwich, Winchelsea) vital for embarkation.
*   **Duchy of Burgundy:** Two parts: Duchy (Dijon, east) + County (Flanders, Artois, north). Pivotal pivot point.
*   **County of Flanders:** Ghent, Bruges, Ypres. Industrial heart; English wool dependency.
*   **Duchy of Brittany:** Strategic peninsula; Civil War (Montfort vs. Blois) proxy for Anglo-French conflict.
*   **Kingdom of Navarre:** Pyrenees + Normandy lands (Cherbourg, Évreux); Charles the Bad's base.
*   **Crown of Castile:** Naval ally of France (Battle of La Rochelle); Civil war (Peter the Cruel vs. Henry of Trastámara) involved Black Prince.
*   **Holy Roman Empire:** Diplomatic arena (Louis IV, Charles IV); Luxembourg, Brabant, Hainault involved.
*   **Scotland:** "Auld Alliance"; Northern front (Berwick, Roxburgh, Edinburgh).

### KEY CITIES & STRATEGIC POINTS
*   **Paris:** Political capital; Parlement; Bastille (built 1370s); University; Population ~200k (largest West of Constantinople).
*   **Bordeaux:** Capital of English Gascony; Wine trade hub; Loyal to England (300 years); Fell 1451/53.
*   **Calais:** English bridgehead (1347–1558); Wool staple port; Heavily garrisoned (Pale of Calais).
*   **Rouen:** Capital of Normandy; Fell to Henry V 1419; Joan of Arc trial/execution site; Recaptured 1449.
*   **Reims:** Coronation cathedral (Clovis tradition); Charles VII crowned 1429 (symbolic legitimacy).
*   **Orléans:** Gateway to Loire Valley/South; Siege 1428–29 turning point.
*   **Harfleur:** Port entry for 1415/1417 campaigns; Henry V's base.
*   **Cherbourg:** Port in Cotentin; English held 1290s–1450.
*   **Brest:** Breton port; English naval base (held until 1397, then 1440s).
*   **Tours:** Charles VII's treasury/administration (Loire Valley).
*   **Bourges:** "Capital" of Dauphin Charles (1418–1422).
*   **Dijon:** Burgundian ducal capital; Palace of Dukes; Chartreuse de Champmol (dynastic necropolis).
*   **Ghent / Bruges / Ypres:** Flemish cloth cities; Artevelde / Van den Berghe regimes.
*   **Sluys (Sluis):** Zwin estuary; Battle 1340; Port of Bruges.
*   **La Rochelle:** Atlantic port; Battle 1372; Huguenot later.
*   **Agincourt (Azincourt):** Pas-de-Calais; Battlefield 1415.
*   **Crécy-en-Ponthieu:** Somme valley; Battlefield 1346.
*   **Poitiers / Nouaillé-Maupertuis:** Battlefield 1356.
*   **Patay:** Loiret; Battlefield 1429 (French cavalry revenge).
*   **Formigny:** Calvados; Battlefield 1450 (Artillery).
*   **Castillon-la-Bataille:** Gironde; Battlefield 1453 (Artillery/End).
*   **Mont-Saint-Michel:** Held by French throughout; Symbol of resistance.

### IMPORTANT BUILDINGS
*   **Louvre (Paris):** Charles V's royal library/residence (fortress origins).
*   **Bastille (Paris):** Built 1370–1383 (Porte Saint-Antoine); Symbol of royal authority.
*   **Palace of the Popes (Avignon):** Papal seat 1309–1377; Mediators.
*   **Chartreuse de Champmol (Dijon):** Burgundian dynastic tombs (Sluter's *Well of Moses*).
*   **Windsor Castle:** Edward III rebuilding (St. George's Chapel, Order of Garter).
*   **Westminster Hall:** Richard II hammer-beam roof (1390s); Parliament meeting place.
*   **Châteaux of Loire:** Chinon (Joan meets Charles), Loches, Saumur, Mehun-sur-Yèvre.
*   **Carcassonne:** Fortified city (restored Viollet-le-Duc); Border sentinel.
*   **Aigues-Mortes:** Mediterranean port (Crusade/Departure point).

## 5. MAJOR EVENTS (DETAILED ANALYSIS)

### 5.1 Battle of Sluys (June 24, 1340)
*   **What:** Edward III's fleet (c. 150–200 converted merchant cogs + royal ships) attacked French/Castilian/Genoese fleet (c. 200 ships) anchored in Zwin estuary.
*   **Why:** Edward needed to invade France; French fleet threatened English coast/wool trade.
*   **Who:** Edward III (aboard *Cog Thomas*); French Admirals Hugues Quiéret & Nicolas Béhuchet; Genoese crossbowmen under Barbavera.
*   **Tactics:** English archers on high castles (fore/aft) rained arrows; Grappling hooks; Boarding actions. Genoese Barbavera escaped with squadron.
*   **Immediate:** French naval power destroyed; English Channel control; Edward besieged Tournai (failed).
*   **Long-term:** England "Mistress of the Seas" (propaganda); Shift to land war in France; Finance crisis for Edward (debts to Bardi/Peruzzi).

### 5.2 Battle of Crécy (August 26, 1346)
*   **What:** Chevauchée (Edward III) from Cherbourg across Normandy to Somme; Trapped at Crécy by Philip VI.
*   **Why:** Demonstrate Valois inability to protect subjects; Economic warfare (chevauchée); Provoke battle.
*   **Who:** Edward III & Black Prince (vanguard); Philip VI, John of Bohemia, Charles of Alençon, Louis of Nevers (Flanders).
*   **Forces:** England ~10–15k (longbowmen ~7k, men-at-arms ~3k, hobelars); France ~20–30k (Genose crossbowmen ~6k, French men-at-arms ~12k, militia).
*   **Tactics:** English defensive: Dismounted men-at-arms center, longbowmen wings (wedge/harrow formation), ditches/sharpened stakes. Rain wet Genoese bowstrings. French chivalry charged piecemeal into arrowstorm. 15–16 charges repulsed. Black Prince "won his spurs."
*   **Casualties:** French nobility decimated (11 princes, 1,200+ knights, ~10k total). English minimal (~100–300).
*   **Consequences:** Myth of French invincibility broken; Calais siege begins; Chevauchée validated; Longbow dominance established.

### 5.3 Siege of Calais (Sept 1346 – Aug 1347)
*   **What:** 11-month blockade. English built "wooden city" (ville de bois) with market, houses, siege lines. French relief attempts failed.
*   **Why:** Need permanent Channel port/base.
*   **Outcome:** Surrender on terms: Six burghers (Rodin) spared; English population planted; Calais becomes English staple port for wool.
*   **Significance:** English foothold on continent for 211 years; "Brightest jewel in English crown."

### 5.4 Battle of Poitiers (September 19, 1356)
*   **What:** Black Prince's chevauchée from Bordeaux north to Loire; Cornered south of Poitiers by John II.
*   **Why:** Prince returning to Gascony; John II intercepted.
*   **Tactics:** English defensive behind hedges/vineyards. Feigned retreat by Captal de Buch (Gascon) drew French reserve. Archers moved to flanks/enfilade. French men-at-arms (dismounted) exhausted climbing obstacles under fire.
*   **Capture of King:** John II and youngest son Philip (the Bold) captured. Surrounded, yielded to Denis de Morbecque.
*   **Consequences:** French state decapitated; Dauphin Charles (regent) weak; Estates General (Paris) demand reform (Great Ordinance 1357); Jacquerie; Treaty of Brétigny.

### 5.5 Treaty of Brétigny / Calais (May/Oct 1360)
*   **Terms:** Edward III: Full sovereignty (no homage) over Aquitaine, Calais, Ponthieu, Guînes (~1/3 France). Renounces Crown of France. John II ransom: 3 million gold écus (paid in installments). Hostages (40 nobles) for payment.
*   **Why it failed:** Sovereignty clause ambiguous (Parlement of Paris appeals). Ransom unpayable (John II died in London 1364). Charles V repudiated 1369.

### 5.6 Battle of La Rochelle (June 22–23, 1372)
*   **What:** English fleet (Earl of Pembroke) bringing reinforcements/money to La Rochelle ambushed by Castilian/French fleet (Ambrosio Boccanegra).
*   **Tactics:** Castilian galleys/oared ships vs English high-sided cogs. Grappling, boarding, incendiary weapons (Greek fire/pots). Tide trapped English.
*   **Consequences:** Total English naval disaster. Pembroke captured. Loss of sea lanes = inability to supply/relieve Aquitaine. Du Guesclin begins systematic reconquest.

### 5.7 Agincourt Campaign & Battle (1415)
*   **Siege of Harfleur (Aug–Sept):** Dysentery decimates English army (~25% losses). Henry marches to Calais via Somme ( chevauchée ).
*   **Battle (Oct 25, St. Crispin's Day):** Henry ~6–9k (80% longbowmen, exhausted, sick). French ~12–25k (Constable d'Albret, Marshal Boucicaut, Dukes of Alençon, Brabant, Bar).
*   **Terrain:** Narrow plowed field between woods (Agincourt/Tramecourt). Mud (recent rain).
*   **Tactics:** English stakes; Archers advance within range; Volley provokes French charge; Men-at-arms slog through mud into stakes/arrows; Congestion = suffocation/helplessness. English archers drop bows, use hatchets/mallets/knives in melee.
*   **Massacre of Prisoners:** Henry orders killing of captured nobles (fear of rear attack). Violated chivalry.
*   **Casualties:** French ~6–10k dead (incl. Constable, 3 Dukes, 90+ nobles), 1,500 prisoners. English ~100–400.
*   **Consequences:** Normandy open; French leadership vacuum; Burgundian/Armagnac civil war escalates.

### 5.8 Treaty of Troyes (May 21, 1420)
*   **Context:** Burgundy allied with England after Montereau. Paris controlled by Anglo-Burgundians. Dauphinist south of Loire.
*   **Terms:** Charles VI: Henry V = Heir & Regent of France. Marriage to Catherine. Dauphin Charles disinherited (accused of Montereau murder). Dual Monarchy: Separate administrations, one king.
*   **Significance:** High watermark English claim; Legal basis for Henry VI's French kingship; Rejected by Dauphin/Armagnacs/Pope Martin V.

### 5.9 Siege of Orléans (Oct 1428 – May 1429)
*   **Strategic Value:** Last major Armagnac stronghold on Loire. Fall opens road to Bourges/Charles VII.
*   **English Forces:** Earl of Salisbury (killed by cannon shot), Suffolk, Talbot, Scales. ~5–7k men. Fortifications (bastilles) around city (Tourelles, Augustins, St. Loup).
*   **French Relief:** Joan of Arc arrives April 29 with convoy. Morale shift.
*   **Assaults (May 4–7):** St. Loup (May 4), Augustins (May 6), Tourelles (May 7 – Joan wounded, returns, final assault). English abandon siege May 8.
*   **Consequences:** English aura of invincibility broken; Loire Campaign follows; Path to Reims open.

### 5.10 Battle of Patay (June 18, 1429)
*   **What:** English (Talbot, Fastolf) retreating from Loire. French vanguard (La Hire, Xaintrailles, Dunois, Richemont) catches them deployed for battle (archers stakes) but unprepared.
*   **Tactics:** French heavy cavalry charges *before* English stakes fully set/archers ready. Rout. Fastolf flees (disgraced). Talbot captured.
*   **Significance:** Destruction of English field army veteran core; French cavalry proven effective vs longbows if tactics right; Reims march feasible.

### 5.11 Coronation of Charles VII at Reims (July 17, 1429)
*   **Symbolism:** Sacred oil (Sainte Ampoule); Traditional site. Legitimacy over Treaty of Troyes.
*   **Political:** Win over waverers; Burgundy isolated.

### 5.12 Congress of Arras / Treaty of Arras (Aug–Sept 1435)
*   **Delegates:** England (Beaufort, Kemp), France (Alençon, Vendôme), Burgundy (Philip the Good), Papal/Legates, Imperial.
*   **English Position:** Refused to renounce Henry VI's French crown. Offered long truce.
*   **Burgundian Shift:** Philip demanded return of Somme towns (ceded 1419), exemption from homage, apology for Montereau. Charles VII conceded (humiliating).
*   **Result:** Burgundy recognizes Charles VII. English walk out. Paris falls 1436.
*   **Why:** Bedford dead (1435); English intransigence; Burgundy's interest in Low Countries vs France; Charles VII's political concessions.

### 5.13 Battle of Formigny (April 15, 1450)
*   **Context:** French reconquest Normandy. English army (Kyriell, ~4–5k) marching to relieve Bayeux.
*   **Tactics:** French (Clermont, Richemont ~3–4k + Breton cavalry) deploy culverins/ribaults on flanks. English longbows engage. French guns disrupt English. Richemont's flank attack (arriving late) decides.
*   **Significance:** First major battle decided significantly by field artillery; Normandy lost.

### 5.14 Battle of Castillon (July 17, 1453)
*   **Context:** Talbot (~6–8k) relieving Castillon siege (Bureau brothers commanding French ~7–10k in fortified camp with 300+ guns).
*   **Tactics:** Talbot attacks prematurely (misled by dust cloud = French fleeing). English charge into enfilading artillery fire from fortified camp. Breton cavalry (Richemont) hits flank. Talbot horse killed, pinned, finished by axe.
*   **Consequences:** Gascony falls. Bordeaux surrenders Oct 19. War ends. No treaty.

## 6. MILITARY AFFAIRS

### 6.1 English Military System
*   **Indentured Retinues:** Contracts (indentures) between King and Captain (Lord/Gentleman). Captain provides specified numbers of men-at-arms & archers for set period/rate of pay. Replaced feudal levy.
*   **Longbowmen (Yeomen):** Core strength. Recruited from shires (Cheshire, Wales, South Wales, Macclesfield). High pay (6d/day). Skill mandated by law (Archery Statutes 1363). Rate of fire: 10–12 arrows/min. Range: 250+ yds. Armor-piercing bodkin points.
*   **Men-at-Arms:** Knights, esquires. Fought dismounted (mostly). Plate armor (white harness late war). Weapons: Poleaxe, sword, lance (dismounted), dagger.
*   **Hobelars / Mounted Archers:** Light cavalry (Irish/English borders). Reconnaissance, raiding, pursuit. Archers often mounted for mobility (dismount to fight).
*   **Artillery:** Used at sieges (bombards) but rarely fielded effectively until very end (Castillon). Edward III used *ribauldequins* (organ guns) at Crécy (limited effect).
*   **Logistics:** Purveyance (forced purchase), wagon trains, shipping (cogs). Chevauchée = living off land (destruction as strategy).

### 6.2 French Military System (Evolution)
*   **Feudal Ost / Arrière-ban:** Traditional summons. Slow, unpaid (40 days), undisciplined. Failed at Crécy/Poitiers/Agincourt.
*   **Compagnies d'Ordonnance (1439–1445, Charles VII):** **First standing army in Western Europe since Rome.**
    *   15 companies * 100 lances = 1,500 lances.
    *   **Lance:** 1 Man-at-arms (gendarme), 1 Coustillier (lighter cavalry), 3 Archers (mounted, fight on foot), 1 Page/Valet. Total ~6 men. ~9,000 professional cavalry/infantry core.
    *   Paid by *Taille* (permanent tax). Captains loyal to Crown.
*   **Francs-Archers (1448):** Parish-based militia (1 per 50 hearths). Tax-exempt. Infantry reserve. Variable quality.
*   **Artillery Bureau (Jean & Gaspard Bureau):** Professional corps. Master of Artillery. Standardized calibers (culverins, bombards, veuglaires). Powder production (saltpeter). Wagon trains. Fortified artillery parks (Castillon).
*   **Mercenaries / Routiers / Écorcheurs:** Free Companies (Tard-Venus, Great Company). Unemployed soldiers pillaging France (1360s). Du Guesclin hired/expelled them (Spain).

### 6.4 Weapons & Armor Evolution
*   **1337:** Mail hauberks, bascinets (klappvisors), great helms (jousting), surcoats. Longbow dominant.
*   **1360s:** Plate reinforcement (coat of plates), hourglass gauntlets, visored bascinets. Plate cuirass appears.
*   **1415:** White harness (full plate) for knights. Poleaxe standard. Longbow still penetrates at close range.
*   **1453:** Fluted/Gothic armor (German/Italian). Arming sword + rondel dagger. Handguns (handculverins) appear (French *couleuvriniers*). Artillery: Cast iron balls, corned powder (better range/velocity), wheeled carriages.

### 6.5 Naval Warfare
*   **Ships:** Cog (high-sided, clinker, 1 mast, stern/rudder) = English workhorse. Hulk (carvel). Galley (Mediterranean, oars) = French/Castilian preference.
*   **Tactics:** No broadside guns. Grappling, boarding, missiles (arrows, crossbows, lime, Greek fire), dropping weights from tops.
*   **Cinque Ports / Merchant Fleet:** English naval mobilization relied on arresting merchant ships (40 days service).

### 6.6 Fortifications & Siegecraft
*   **Trace Italienne (Early):** Low, thick walls; Bastions (early forms at castles like Chinon, Vincennes).
*   **English "Bastilles":** Timber/earth forts (Orléans siege: Tourelles, St. Loup). Prefabricated?
*   **Artillery:** Bombards (stone balls, 200–500 lbs). Slow rate of fire (hours). Transport difficult. Bureau brothers improved mobility/rate (cast iron, corned powder, trunnions).
*   **Mining/Counter-mining:** Standard.
*   **Cost:** Sieges expensive (wages, materials). Decisive factor often starvation/disease/treachery.

## 7. POLITICS & DIPLOMACY

### 7.1 Governments & Institutions
**England:**
*   **Parliament:** Commons (Knights of Shire/Burgesses) + Lords. Power of Purse. War taxation (Tenths/Fifteenths, Wool Subsidy, Poll Taxes). Petitions (Redress of Grievances) before Supply. Statutes of Laborers (1351), Provisors (1351), Praemunire (1353) – anti-papal.
*   **Council/Chancery:** Executive. Keepers/Regents during minorities (Richard II, Henry VI).
*   **Exchequer:** Tallies (debt instruments). Bankruptcies (Bardi/Peruzzi 1340s; Crown debts massive).

**France:**
*   **King's Council (Conseil du Roi):** Professional administrators (lawyers, clerks). *Chambre des Comptes* (audit), *Parlement de Paris* (supreme court, registration of edicts).
*   **Estates General (États Généraux):** Three orders (Clergy, Nobility, Third Estate). No permanent institution. Met intermittently (1355–58, 1413, 1420, 1439, 1484). Voted *taille* (often permanent after 1439).
*   **Provincial Estates (Pays d'États):** Languedoc, Brittany, Burgundy, Normandy – negotiated taxes separately.
*   **Parlements:** Regional sovereign courts (Paris, Toulouse, Grenoble, Bordeaux, Dijon). Judicial appeal from Gascony = friction point.

### 7.2 Alliances
*   **Anglo-Flemish (1337–1347, 1380s, 1410s):** Wool for cloth. Artevelde, Van den Berghe, Philip the Good (early).
*   **Anglo-Burgundian (1419–1435):** John the Fearless / Philip the Good + Henry V / Bedford. Treaty of Amiens (1423), Troyes. Collapsed at Arras.
*   **Anglo-Castilian (1360s, 1380s):** Pedro the Cruel / John of Gaunt claim. Failed at Nájera/La Rochelle.
*   **Anglo-Portuguese (1373 Treaty of London / 1386 Windsor):** Oldest alliance. John of Gaunt's daughter Philippa married João I.
*   **Anglo-Breton (Montforts):** John de Montfort / John IV / John V vs Blois/Penthièvre. English garrisons in Breton ports.
*   **Franco-Scottish (Auld Alliance 1295 renewed 1326, 1371, 1418, 1428):** Scottish troops in France (Battle of Baugé 1421 victory; Verneuil 1424 defeat). Diversion in North.
*   **Franco-Castilian (1368+):** Naval alliance. Enrique de Trastámara. La Rochelle.
*   **Franco-Burgundian (1435+):** Arras. Critical.

### 7.3 Major Treaties & Diplomatic Events
*   **Treaty of Paris (1259):** Henry III homage for Gascony. Root cause.
*   **Treaty of Northampton (1328):** Recognized Scottish independence (Edward III forced).
*   **Truce of Espléchin (1340):** First major pause.
*   **Treaty of Calais/Brétigny (1360):** Detailed above.
*   **Treaty of Bruges (1375):** Truce (1 year extended). Cherbourg, Brest, Calais, Bordeaux remain English.
*   **Treaty of Leulinghem (1389):** 3-year truce extended to 1396, then 28 years (1396 Truce of Paris – Richard II marries Isabella).
*   **Treaty of Troyes (1420):** Detailed above.
*   **Treaty of Amiens (1423):** Bedford/Burgundy/Brittany triple alliance.
*   **Congress of Arras (1435):** Failed peace conference; Burgundy defects.
*   **Truce of Tours (1444):** 2 years (extended). Henry VI marries Margaret of Anjou. Maine surrendered (secret).
*   **Treaty of Picquigny (1475):** *Post-war.* Edward IV pension. Formal end of claims (mostly).

### 7.4 Papal Diplomacy
*   **Clement VI (Avignon):** Tried to stop war (1342–43). Failed.
*   **Innocent VI:** Legates at Brétigny.
*   **Great Schism (1378):** Urban VI (Rome) vs Clement VII (Avignon). England = Urban; France = Clement. War becomes religious duty.
*   **Council of Constance (1414–18):** Ended Schism. English nation (Henry V) influential.
*   **Council of Basel / Ferrera-Florence (1431–45):** Conciliarism vs Papacy. Charles VII Pragmatic Sanction (1438) – Gallican liberties.

## 8. SOCIETY, ECONOMY, CULTURE, TECHNOLOGY, RELIGION

### 8.1 Daily Life & Demographics
*   **Population:**
    *   France: ~17–20M (1328) → ~10–12M (1450). Loss ~30–40% (Plague + War + Famine).
    *   England: ~4.5–5M (1348) → ~2.5M (1450). Loss ~40–50%.
    *   Paris: ~200k → ~100k. London: ~50k → ~40k. Bordeaux: ~25k.
*   **Social Orders (Three Estates):** *Oratores* (Clergy), *Bellatores* (Nobility), *Laboratores* (Peasants/Townsfolk).
*   **Peasantry:** Serfdom declining in West (commutation to rent), persisting East. *Taille* (tax) burden heavy. *Corvée* (labor). Chevauchée = burn crops, kill livestock, destroy tools.
*   **Urban Life:** Guilds (craft/merchant). City councils. Population decline → labor shortage → higher wages (Statute of Laborers tried to freeze). Migration to towns.

### 8.2 Economy
*   **Wool Trade:** England = raw material. Flanders/Italy = manufacture. Calais Staple (1363) = monopoly market. Customs = 50%+ Crown revenue.
*   **Wine Trade:** Gascony (Bordeaux) → England. "The Great Wine Fleet." Vital for English elite consumption & revenue (prisage/butlerage).
*   **Currency:**
    *   England: Silver penny (sterling) standard. Gold Noble (1344, 6s 8d). High purity = trusted.
    *   France: *Livre tournois* (money of account). Coins: *Écu d'or*, *Franc à cheval* (John II ransom), *Blanc*, *Gros*. **Debasement** chronic (Philip VI, John II, Charles VI) → inflation, Gresham's Law.
*   **Taxation:**
    *   England: Parliamentary grants (Direct: Tenths/Fifteenths on movables; Indirect: Wool customs). Poll taxes (1377, 1379, 1381 – triggered Revolt).
    *   France: *Taille* (hearth/land tax, nobles/clergy exempt), *Gabelle* (salt), *Aides* (sales), *Dîme* (church). *Taille* became permanent 1439.
*   **Banking:** Italian houses (Bardi, Peruzzi, Acciaiuoli) collapsed 1340s (Edward III default). Later: Medici, Jacques Cœur (France), Fuggers (later).

### 8.3 Technology
*   **Metallurgy:** Blast furnace (late 14th c. Europe) → Cast iron cannonballs (cheaper than stone), better plate armor.
*   **Gunpowder:** Corned powder (granulated, 1420s+) → Higher velocity, safer storage, standard loads. Bureau brothers pioneers.
*   **Shipbuilding:** Carvel construction (planks edge-to-edge) replacing clinker. Stern rudder standard. Multi-masts (carracks).
*   **Printing:** Gutenberg (1450s) – *just after war*. Manuscript culture (Jean Froissart, Christine de Pizan, Thomas Walsingham).
*   **Clockwork:** Mechanical clocks (public towers). Time discipline.

### 8.4 Culture & Chivalry
*   **Chronicles:** **Jean Froissart** (c.1337–1405) – *Chronicles* (chivalric view, English/French/Burgundian patrons). **Enguerrand de Monstrelet** (continuator). **Thomas Walsingham** (St Albans, English monastic). **Jean de Wavrin**. **Jean Chartier** (French royal).
*   **Literature:** **Geoffrey Chaucer** (Canterbury Tales – soldier, diplomat, customs controller). **Christine de Pizan** (*Livre des fais d'armes et de chevalerie*, *Ditié de Jehanne* – pro-Joan). **Charles d'Orléans** (poet prisoner in England 25 yrs). **Alain Chartier**.
*   **Chivalric Orders:** **Order of the Garter** (Edward III, 1348, St George, Windsor). **Order of the Star** (John II, 1351, failed). **Order of the Golden Fleece** (Philip the Good, 1430, Bruges – Burgundian statehood).
*   **Heraldry:** Quarterly arms (England/France). Cadency marks. Marshals/Heralds regulate.
*   **Tournaments:** *Pas d'armes* (passage of arms). Sporting vs Judicial. René d'Anjou *Livre des tournois*.

### 8.5 Religion
*   **Parish Life:** Mass, sacraments, saints' days, guilds, chantries. Purgatory central.
*   **Mendicant Orders:** Franciscans, Dominicans (preaching, universities, Inquisition).
*   **Heresy:** **Lollards** (England, Wycliffe – Bible in English, anti-clerical). Oldcastle Revolt (1414). **Hussites** (Bohemia, 1419+) – influenced military (wagon forts).
*   **Joan of Arc:** Mysticism, voices, virginity, cross-dressing (trial focus). Rehabilitation trial (1456) = Charles VII legitimacy.
*   **Pilgrimage:** Canterbury, Walsingham, Compostela, Le Puy, Mont-Saint-Michel.

### 9.3 Financial Data
*   **English War Cost (1337–1453):** ~£5–6 million (sterling). Annual average ~£40–50k. Peak years (Agincourt/Normandy) ~£100k+.
*   **French War Cost:** Harder to calculate. *Taille* yield 1440s: ~1.2M *livres tournois*/yr. Ransom John II: 3M *écus* (~£500k sterling). Only ~1M paid.
*   **Soldier Pay (Daily):**
    *   Man-at-arms: 2s (Eng) / 1 *livre* (Fr, later).
    *   Mounted Archer: 1s / 6d.
    *   Foot Archer: 6d / 4–5 *sols*.
    *   Master Mason: 6–8d.
    *   Laborer: 3–4d (Statute Laborers 1351 fixed at pre-plague rates).
*   **Ransoms:** King John II: 3M écus. Duke of Orléans (Agincourt captive 25 yrs): 40k écus + installments. Earl of Salisbury (Orléans): 20k écus. Ransom market = major economy.

### 9.4 Distances & Logistics
*   **Channel Crossing:** Dover-Calais ~21 miles (4–12 hrs sail). Southampton-Cherbourg ~100 miles (12–24 hrs).
*   **Chevauchée Range:** 15–20 miles/day (mounted). 10–12 miles/day (army with wagons).
*   **Paris to Bordeaux:** ~350 miles (6–8 weeks march).
*   **Supply:** Army of 10k needs ~20 tons grain/day + fodder. Wagons: 1 per 30–50 men. River transport (Seine, Loire, Garonne) critical.

## 10. INTERESTING VISUAL DETAILS (FOR DOCUMENTARY IMAGERY)

### 10.1 Clothing & Armor (Evolution)
*   **1330s–1360s:** *Early Transitional.* Mail hauberk + coat of plates (hidden). Bascinet with aventail (mail curtain) + klappvisor (hinged) or great helm (joust). Surcoat (long, flowing, heraldic) → Jupon (tight, padded, heraldic). *Longbowman:* Bascinet/kettle hat (chapel de fer), jack (quilted textile armor), hose, boots. Livery coats (lord's colors/badge).
*   **1370s–1410s:** *White Harness Emerges.* Full plate cuirass (breast/back), pauldrons, vambraces, cuisses, greaves, sabatons. Bascinet with visor (globose/houndskull). Jupon shorter. *Heraldry:* Tabard (sleeveless coat) over armor.
*   **1420s–1453:** *Late Gothic / Fluted.* Milanese (smooth, rounded) vs German (fluted, angular). Sallet (helmet) + bevor (chin/throat). Tabard standard. *French:* White cross (St. Denis) on red field (sometimes). *English:* Red cross (St. George) on white. *Burgundian:* St. Andrew's Cross (raguly) + Fire-steel/Spur sparks.
*   **Civilians:** Houppelande (full, belted, dagged sleeves). Chaperon (hat/hood combo, liripipe). Poulaines (long pointed shoes). Women: Hennin (cone hat), cotehardie, sideless surcoat.

### 10.2 Flags, Banners, Symbols
*   **England:** *Royal Arms:* Quarterly France Ancient (Azure, 3 fleurs-de-lis Or) & England (Gules, 3 lions passant guardant Or). *St George's Cross:* Argent, cross Gules. *Heraldic Badges:* Edward III (Sunburst, Falcon/Fetterlock), Richard II (White Hart), Henry IV (Swan), Henry V (Antelope), York (White Rose), Lancaster (Red Rose).
*   **France:** *Royal Arms:* Azure, 3 fleurs-de-lis Or (France Modern, Charles V 1376). *Oriflamme:* Red silk banner (St. Denis), pointed, no fleur-de-lis – "No quarter" signal. *Dauphin:* Label of 3 points. *Joan of Arc:* Standard (Jesus Maria, fleur-de-lis, angels), Pennon (Lamb of God).
*   **Burgundy:** *Ducal Arms:* Quarterly France Modern/Burgundy Ancient (Bendy Or/Azure, bordure Gules) + Brabant/Limburg. *Cross of St. Andrew:* Saltire Raguly Gules on White. *Order of Golden Fleece:* Collar (flints, sparks, fleece).
*   **Brittany:** Ermine plain (Duchess) / Chequered (Montfort).
*   **Scotland:** Lion Rampant Gules (double tressure flory-counter-flory) on Or. St Andrew's Cross (Saltire) Azure/Argent.

### 10.3 Architecture & Landscapes
*   **Castles:** *Concentric:* Dover, Caerphilly (pre-war). *Tower Houses/Donjons:* Vincennes (huge keep), Coucy (destroyed 1917), Château Gaillard. *Palatial:* Louvre (Charles V), Mehun-sur-Yèvre, Saumur, Châteaudun. *Bastides:* Fortified new towns (Gascony).
*   **Cities:** Timber-framed houses (jettying), narrow streets, open sewers. Cathedral spires (Rouen, Reims, Chartres, Salisbury). City walls (Paris: Philip Augustus / Charles V walls). Gates (Porte Saint-Denis, Porte Saint-Antoine/Bastille).
*   **Battlefields:** Open plowed fields (Agincourt, Crécy, Formigny, Castillon). Mud (chalky soil + rain). Woods framing flanks. Churches (Agincourt village). Fortified camps (Castillon – trenches, palisades, artillery redoubts).
*   **Siege Works:** *Bastilles* (Orléans – Tourelles: stone tower + bridge + earthwork). Mining tunnels. Counter-weight trebuchets (late use) vs Bombards (metal, on wooden beds/sledges). Gabions (wicker baskets filled with earth). Mantlets (mobile shields).

### 10.4 Weapons Close-ups
*   **Longbow:** Yew (Italian/Spanish/Polish import), 6ft+, draw 100–180 lbs. Arrows: Sheaf (24), Bodkin (armor), Broadhead (horse/flesh). Nocks, fletching (goose feather).
*   **Crossbow:** Steel prod (late), windlass/cranequin spanning. Bolts (quarrels). Pavise (large shield for reload).
*   **Poleaxe:** Hammer/axe/spike head. 5–6ft haft. Anti-armor.
*   **Sword:** Type XVa/XVIa (stiff thrusting), Type XVIII (cut/thrust). Rondel dagger (ice-pick).
*   **Artillery:** Bombard (wrought iron staves + hoops, breech-loading chambers). Culverin (longer, smaller bore, cast bronze/iron). Ribault (organ gun, multiple barrels). Stone balls (carved) → Cast iron balls. Gunpowder barrels (serpents). Linstock/match.

### 10.5 Ships
*   **Cog:** Single mast, square sail, stern castle, clinker hull, side rudder (quarter rudder). High freeboard = advantage in boarding. *Conversion:* Fore/aft castles heightened for archers.
*   **Galley:** Low, oars (25+ per side), lateen sail, ram/prow. Fast, maneuverable, poor seakeeping in Channel. Used by French/Castilians/Genoese.
*   **Hulk:** Carvel built, flat bottom, higher capacity.

## 11. CONSEQUENCES & LEGACY

### 11.1 Immediate Consequences (1453–1475)
*   **England:** Loss of all French territory except Calais. **Dynastic Crisis:** Henry VI's madness (1453) + defeat → **Wars of the Roses** (1455–1487). Nobility bankrupt, armed retainers, private feuds (Percy/Neville). Parliament weakened (Henry VI compliant).
*   **France:** **Charles VII "The Victorious"** supreme. Standing army (*Compagnies d'Ordonnance*). Permanent taxation (*Taille*). Pragmatic Sanction (1438) – Gallican Church. **Louis XI** (Dauphin) inherits strong, centralized base. Burgundy (Philip the Good) remains semi-independent threat.
*   **Burgundy:** Charles the Bold expands (Luxembourg, Guelders, Lorraine) → clashes with Swiss/Lorraine/France → Death at Nancy (1477) → Partition (France gets Duchy, Habsburgs get Netherlands).
*   **Scotland:** James II sieges Roxburgh (1460) – killed by cannon. Scottish independence secured.
*   **Papacy:** End of Schism (1417). Conciliarism defeated. French Gallicanism entrenched.

### 11.2 Long-Term Historical Impact
*   **Nation-States:** Accelerated transition from **Feudal Monarchy** to **Early Modern State** in both countries.
    *   *England:* Parliament's power of purse solidified; Common Law distinct; Insular identity reinforced ("This sceptred isle"). Shift from continental empire to maritime/colonial focus (Tudors).
    *   *France:* Royal absolutism foundations (Standing Army, Permanent Tax, Royal Justice). "France" as geographic/political entity solidified. Language (Langue d'oïl) imposed as administrative standard (Ordonnance de Villers-Cotterêts 1539, root in Charles V).
*   **Military Revolution (Precursor):**
    *   Infantry dominance (Longbow → Pike/Arquebus).
    *   Artillery as siege/battle winner (Bureau brothers).
    *   Standing armies (Contract/Indenture → Ordonnance).
    *   Logistics/State finance capacity = War winner.
*   **National Identities:**
    *   *England:* St. George, English language (Chaucer, Wycliffe, Parliament records), hostility to France.
    *   *France:* St. Denis, Joan of Arc (national saint 1920), "L'Autre" (the English) as foil. *Christine de Pizan* "Le Livre de la paix".
*   **Language:** English replaces French in English law/Parliament (1362 Statute of Pleading). French becomes language of diplomacy/international law.
*   **Economic Shift:** Wool trade shifts from raw export to cloth manufacture (English cloth industry rises 15th c.). Antwerp replaces Bruges → Amsterdam later. Hanseatic League rivalry.

### 11.3 Legacy & Historiography
*   **"Hundred Years' War" Term:** Coined 19th c. (French historians, e.g., Michelet). Contemporaries saw series of wars/truces.
*   **Shakespeare:** *Henry V, Henry VI (Parts 1,2,3), Richard II, King John* – shaped Anglophone perception (Agincourt, Joan as witch/whore, Talbot hero).
*   **French National Myth:** Joan of Arc (Republic/Monarchy/Catholic/Nationalist symbol). "La France éternelle".
*   **Military History:** Classic case study: Asymmetric warfare (Longbow vs Cavalry), Military adaptation (French artillery/reform), Logistics, Civil-military relations.
*   **Genealogy:** Current British monarch descends from Henry VII (Lancastrian/Tudor) via Margaret Beaufort (John of Gaunt line). French Bourbons from Charles VII → Louis XIV → Spanish Bourbons. Habsburgs from Mary of Burgundy.

1. **The war lasted 116 years, not 100** – Despite its name, the conflict spanned 116 years (1337–1453), making "The Hundred Years' War" a retrospective label coined by 19th-century historians. The fighting was intermittent, with long truces like the 28-year Treaty of Brétigny (1360) and a nine-year pause after Agincourt. The name stuck because it conveniently framed a messy series of dynastic disputes into a single narrative.

2. **A teenage girl with no military training led a French army to victory because she claimed saints spoke to her** – Joan of Arc, an illiterate 17-year-old peasant, convinced the Dauphin to give her command by correctly identifying him while he hid among courtiers—a test he devised to expose frauds. She then lifted the siege of Orléans in nine days, turning a seemingly lost war. Her trial transcripts reveal she was condemned not for heresy but for wearing men's clothing, which she resumed after guards stole her dress—a technicality that sealed her fate.

3. **The English longbow's dominance ended because of a rainstorm and stolen arrows** – At the Battle of Patay (1429), French cavalry overran English archers before they could plant their defensive stakes. The stakes had been left behind in the mud during a hasty retreat the previous day, and heavy rain had soaked the bowstrings, rendering them useless. This single engagement broke the myth of English invincibility and marked the beginning of French tactical adaptation.

4. **The war technically began over a French feudal technicality: whether the King of England owed homage for Gascony** – Edward III initially accepted Philip VI as King of France but refused to kneel and perform liege homage for his French duchy, arguing it implied total subjugation.
