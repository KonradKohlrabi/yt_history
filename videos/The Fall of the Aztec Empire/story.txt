**TITLE: THE FIFTH SUN SETS: THE FALL OF THE AZTEC EMPIRE**

**EPISODE 1: THE MEN WHO CAME FROM THE EAST**

**[COLD OPEN]**

**EXT. LAKE TEXCOCO - DAWN (NOVEMBER 8, 1519)**

MIST clings to the water. A city rises from the lake like a dream—white stucco temples gleaming, causeways stretching like needles across the water, canoes slipping silently through reeds.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Tenochtitlan. The Venice of the New World. Two hundred thousand souls on an island fed by floating gardens and twin aqueducts. The heart of an empire that demanded the blood of nations to keep the sun moving.

A long column of men emerges from the mountain pass. Steel helmets catch the pale light. Horses—creatures never seen here—snort in the thin air. Four hundred and fifty Spaniards. Sixteen horses. A few cannon. And thousands of native warriors from a republic that has hated this city for generations.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
They walk down the Causeway of Iztapalapa. Eight abreast. Not hiding. Not sneaking. Walking into the mouth of the jaguar.

**ON SCREEN: GRAPHIC - THE TRIPLE ALLIANCE MAP. TENOCHTITLAN PULSES RED.**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
The Emperor Moctezuma Xocoyotzin waits. He has seen the omens: a comet, a temple burning without fire, a woman weeping in the night. The year is One Reed. The year the Plumed Serpent, Quetzalcoatl, swore he would return from the east.

**EXT. CAUSEWAY - CONTINUOUS**

MOCTEZUMA steps from a litter. Gold nose plug. Turquoise diadem. He wears sandals—only he may wear them on palace floors. He places a necklace of gold and jade around the neck of the bearded stranger.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
The Spaniard is Hernán Cortés. A failed law student. A gambler who just burned his own ships to prevent retreat. He accepts the Emperor’s gold... and asks for more.

### ACT ONE: THE STAKES

**INT. TEMPLE MAYOR - TENOCHTITLAN - DAY (FLASHBACK - 1487)**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
To understand what happens next, you have to understand the debt.

**VISUAL: RECONSTRUCTION - THE GREAT TEMPLE DEDICATION. THOUSANDS OF PRISONERS LINED UP. PRIESTS WITH OBSIDIAN KNIVES. THE HEART RAISED TO THE SUN.**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
The Mexica—the people we call Aztecs—believed the universe was fragile. The Fifth Sun, *Nahui Ollin*, would end in earthquake and darkness unless fed. *Chalchihuatl*. Precious water. Human blood.

**EXT. FLOWERY WAR BATTLEFIELD - DAY**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
So they fought "Flowery Wars." Not for land. For captives. Their neighbors—Tlaxcala, Huejotzingo, Cholula—bled for decades, surrounded, strangled, but never conquered. Because a dead enemy pays no tribute. A live enemy pays forever.

**GRAPHIC: MAP OF THE VALLEY. TLAXCALA IS AN ISLAND OF RED INSIDE A SEA OF AZTEC BLUE.**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
By 1519, the Triple Alliance—Tlacopan, Texcoco, and dominant Tenochtitlan—extracted tribute from 38 provinces. Gold dust. Jade. Feathers. Cacao. Twenty thousand cotton cloaks a year. And always, always... warriors for the stone.

**INT. MOCTEZUMA’S PALACE - NIGHT**

Moctezuma stares at a codex. Reports from the coast. Men in "floating houses." Metal that sings. Beasts with two heads and six legs (horse and rider).

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Moctezuma ruled an empire of millions. He commanded armies that could swallow the Spanish expedition a thousand times over. But he hesitated.

**WHY?**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Was it the omens? The prophecy of Quetzalcoatl returning in Year One Reed? Modern historians debate it. But Moctezuma was a politician first. He saw a *teotl*—a divine power—not a mere man. And you don't kill a god. You negotiate. You buy time.

**THE BIG QUESTION APPEARS ON SCREEN, SILENTLY:**

**HOW DOES AN EMPIRE OF MILLIONS FALL TO A FEW HUNDRED OUTLAWS?**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
The answer isn't steel. It isn't horses. It isn't even guns.

**HEAD FAKE #1 - THE EXPECTATION OF CONQUEST**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
We think we know this story. Superior technology. Disease. Destiny. But watch what actually happens.

**EXT. VERA CRUZ - APRIL 1519**

Cortés lands. He has no royal commission. The Governor of Cuba, Velázquez, wants him arrested. Cortés creates a town council—*Villa Rica de la Vera Cruz*—resigns his commission *to himself*, and gets the council to elect him Captain General.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
A legal fiction. A parlor trick. But it gave him one thing: the authority of the King of Spain. He wasn't a rebel anymore. He was the law.

**EXT. CEMPOALA - TOTONAC TERRITORY**

The "Fat Cacique" of Cempoala watches Cortés. He sees the Aztec tax collectors—*calpixque*—arrogant, demanding.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
The Aztec Empire wasn't a nation. It was a protection racket. And the protection was failing.

**CACIQUE (SUBTITLED)**
*They take our sons for sacrifice. Our daughters for their beds. Our gold for their temples. You have metal that kills. Kill them for us.*

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Cortés didn't conquer the Aztec Empire. He *audited* it. And every subject province handed him the ledger.

**EXT. TLAXCALA - SEPTEMBER 1519**

**VISUAL: THREE BATTLES. TLAXCALAN WARRIORS IN JAGUAR AND EAGLE SUITS SWARM SPANISH PIKE SQUARES. CANNON TEAR HOLES IN LINES. CAVALRY CHARGES SHATTER FLANKS. BUT THE TLAXCALANS DON'T BREAK. THEY ADAPT.**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Tlaxcala. The republic that refused to kneel. They fought Cortés for two weeks. Killed his horses. Wounded his men. And then... they stopped.

**INT. TLAXCALAN COUNCIL HALL - NIGHT**

Four rulers sit around a fire. Xicotencatl the Younger wants war to the death. The Elders—Maxixcatzin, Xicotencatl the Elder—see the writing on the obsidian wall.

**XICOTENCATL THE ELDER (SUBTITLED)**
*They hate Moctezuma as we do. Their steel breaks our obsidian. Their horses run down our best. But they are few. And they need food. We have food. They need allies. We need the Aztecs gone. This is not surrender. This is strategy.*

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Six thousand Tlaxcalan warriors joined Cortés. Not as slaves. As partners. The Spanish brought the anvil. Tlaxcala brought the hammer.

**REHOOK**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
With an army behind him, Cortés turned toward the Valley of Mexico. But the door was guarded by a holy city.

**EXT. CHOLULA - OCTOBER 1519**

**VISUAL: THE GREAT PYRAMID. THE LARGEST BY VOLUME ON EARTH. 365 TEMPLES.**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Cholula. Sacred to Quetzalcoatl. Aztec ally. Cortés entered as a guest. Then Malinche—his interpreter, his consort, the noblewoman sold into slavery—warned him: the Cholulans plan to trap you. The Aztecs are waiting in the hills.

**HEAD FAKE #1 RESOLUTION - THE TRAP SPRINGS SHUT**

**EXT. CHOLULA COURTYARD - DAY**

Nobles and warriors packed tight. Unarmed. Dancing. Cortés gives the signal.

**VISUAL: CROSSBOWS. ARQUEBUSES. STEEL SWORDS. TLAXCALANS AT THE EXITS. THREE TO SIX THOUSAND DEAD IN HOURS. THE PYRAMID SMOKES.**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
No battle. A massacre. A message written in blood for Moctezuma: *Your allies cannot protect you. Your gods cannot save you.*

**EXT. PASO DE CORTÉS - NOVEMBER 8, 1519**

**VISUAL: THE COLUMN DESCENDS FROM 12,000 FEET. THE VALLEY OPENS BELOW. A LAKE OF BLUE. A CITY OF WHITE. SMOKE RISING FROM A THOUSAND HEARTHS.**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
They walked in peace. Moctezuma met them on the causeway. "You have come to sit on your throne," the Emperor said. "For it was foretold."

**INT. AXAYACATL’S PALACE - NIGHT**

Cortés and his captains stare at the gold. The featherwork. The zoo. The armory.

**PEDRO DE ALVARADO (WHISPERING)**
*We are inside the belly of the beast. Four hundred men. One narrow causeway out.*

**CORTÉS (CALM)**
*Then we make sure the beast doesn't swallow us. We take the head.*

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Six days later, Cortés arrested the Emperor of the Aztecs. In his own palace. With his own guards watching.

**CLIFFHANGER**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Moctezuma wore the chains for six months. He swore vassalage to a King he never met. He melted down his ancestors' gold. And the city... watched. And waited.

**FADE TO BLACK.**

**TEXT ON SCREEN: *THE GOLD WAS NEVER ENOUGH.***

### ACT TWO: THE NIGHT OF SORROWS

**EXT. TENOCHTITLAN - MAY 22, 1520 - DAY**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Cortés made a mistake. He left.

**VISUAL: MAP. CORTÉS MARCHES EAST TO THE COAST. 250 MEN. ALVARADO LEFT IN COMMAND. 140 MEN. INSIDE A CITY OF 200,000.**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Pánfilo de Narváez landed at Vera Cruz. Nine hundred men sent by Velázquez to arrest Cortés. Cortés did the impossible: he marched back, struck at night, captured Narváez, and absorbed his army. He returned to Tenochtitlan with 1,300 men.

**INT. SACRED PRECINCT - TOXCATL FESTIVAL - MAY 22, 1520 - DAY**

**VISUAL: THOUSANDS OF NOBLES DANCING. UNARMED. WEARING GOLD ORNAMENTS. DRUMS POUNDING. ALVARADO’S MEN SEAL THE EXITS.**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
But while Cortés was gone, Alvarado panicked. Or got greedy. During the Toxcatl festival—the holiest day—he sealed the temple courtyard.

**VISUAL: SLAUGHTER. NOT A BATTLE. A BUTCHERY. HUNDREDS, MAYBE THOUSANDS OF THE EMPIRE'S ELITE—GENERALS, PRIESTS, NOBLES—FALL UNDER STEEL.**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
The Spanish said they feared a revolt. The Aztecs said they wanted the gold. The result was the same: the social contract shattered.

**EXT. PALACE ROOF - JUNE 29, 1520 - DAY**

Moctezuma stands on the roof. He speaks to his people in Nahuatl. Stones fly. One strikes him.

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Moctezuma died on that roof. Spanish say stoned by his own people. Aztec codices say strangled by the Spanish when he became useless. Either way, the illusion broke.

**VISUAL: CUITLAHUAC ELECTED. YOUNG. FIERCE. THE WAR PARTY LEADER.**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Cuitlahuac took the throne. He didn't negotiate. He cut the causeways. He poisoned the water. He starved them.

**EXT. TACUBA CAUSEWAY - NIGHT (JUNE 30 - JULY 1, 1520)**

**LA NOCHE TRISTE.**

**VISUAL: RAIN. DARKNESS. A PORTABLE BRIDGE. THE COLUMN MOVES SILENTLY. A WOMAN FETCHING WATER SOUNDS THE ALARM. CANOES SWARM. THE BRIDGE JAMS IN THE GAP. MEN DROWN IN ARMOR, WEIGHTED BY GOLD. HORSES SCREAM. CANNON LOST.**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Four hundred fifty Spaniards dead. A thousand Tlaxcalans. The treasure of Montezuma—gone into the mud of Lake Texcoco. Cortés wept under a cypress tree at dawn.

**HEAD FAKE #2 - THE EXPECTATION OF ANNIHILATION**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
This is where the story ends in the movies. The conquerors destroyed. The Empire victorious.

**EXT. OTOMBA PLAINS - JULY 7, 1520 - DAY**

**VISUAL: A MASSIVE AZTEC ARMY ENVELOPES THE RAGGED COLUMN. 20 HORSEMEN LEFT. PIKES LOWERED. CORTÉS SPOTS A STANDARD. A GENERAL IN A FEATHERED BACK-DEVICE.**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
But the Aztecs fought to *capture*. The Spanish fought to *kill*.

**VISUAL: CORTÉS LEADS THE CAVALRY CHARGE. NOT AT THE MASSES. AT THE ONE MAN. THE GENERAL FALLS. THE STANDARD FALLS. THE AZTEC ARMY—SEEING THEIR COMMANDER DEAD—ROUTES.**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
At Otumba, Cortés proved the Aztec war machine had a central nervous system. Sever the head, the body collapses.

**EXT. TLAXCALA - JULY 1520**

The survivors stagger in. Tlaxcala could have finished them. The Elders opened the gates.

**XICOTENCATL THE ELDER (SUBTITLED)**
*We did not ally with winners. We allied with men who keep their word. Rest. Heal. Then we finish what we started.*

**REHOOK - THE INVISIBLE ARMY**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
But the real turning point wasn't steel. It arrived in the hold of Narváez's ships. A single slave. Francisco de Eguía. He carried a passenger.

**VISUAL: MICROSCOPIC VIEW - VARIOLA VIRUS. SPREADING. EXPONENTIAL.**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Smallpox. *Huey Cocoliztli*. The Great Rash.

**INT. TENOCHTITLAN - OCTOBER 1520**

**VISUAL: BODIES IN THE STREETS. PUSTULES. BLINDNESS. THE SMELL. CUITLAHUAC—EMPEROR FOR 80 DAYS—DIES. FORTY PERCENT OF THE VALLEY DIES WITH HIM. THE PRIESTHOOD. THE GENERALS. THE FARMERS. THE CHINAMPA WORKERS.**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
The Empire didn't just lose a battle. It lost its memory. Its leadership. Its ability to feed itself.

**VISUAL: CUAUHTÉMOC. YOUNG. POX-SCARRED. ELECTED. THE LAST EMPEROR.**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Cuauhtémoc was twenty-five. A warrior. He inherited a graveyard. And he chose to fight.

**CLIFFHANGER**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Cortés spent the winter building something the Aztecs had never seen.

**VISUAL: TLAXCALA. TIMBER BEING CUT. SHAPED. NUMBERED. 13 HULLS. 8,000 PORTERS CARRYING THEM OVER THE MOUNTAINS.**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Ships. On a mountain. For a war on a lake.

**FADE TO BLACK.**

**TEXT ON SCREEN: *THE NAVY THAT CROSSED THE MOUNTAINS.***

### ACT THREE: THE SIEGE OF GOD

**EXT. LAKE TEXCOCO - APRIL 28, 1521 - DAWN**

**VISUAL: 13 BRIGANTINES SLIDE INTO THE WATER. LATEEN SAILS. CANNON IN THE BOWS. SPANISH CROSSBOWMEN. TLAXCALAN ROWERS.**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Martín López, a shipwright from Seville, built a navy in the forest and carried it piece by piece across the volcanoes. On April 28, 1521, Lake Texcoco changed hands.

**EXT. TENOCHTITLAN - MAY 1521**

**VISUAL: FOUR CAMPS. ALVARADO (TACUBA). OLID (COYOACÁN). SANDOVAL (IZTAPALAPA). CORTÉS (BRIGANTINES). THE AQUEDUCT FROM CHAPULTEPEC—CUT. FRESH WATER: GONE.**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Seventy-five days. Ninety-three, by some counts. The longest continuous siege in the history of the Americas.

**MONTAGE - THE DAILY GRIND**

**VISUAL: CANAL FILLING. HUMAN CHAINS PASSING RUBBLE. ADOBE BRICKS MADE FROM TEMPLE STONES. BRIGANTINES RAMMING CANOES. ROOFTOP COMBAT. WOMEN THROWING BOILING WATER. AZTEC WARRIORS IN JAGUAR PELTS LEAPING INTO PIKE WALLS.**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
This wasn't European warfare. The Aztecs breached causeways behind the Spanish. Drove stakes underwater to hole the brigantines. Fought from rooftops. Used psychological warfare—drums all night, flayed Spanish faces on pikes.

**EXT. TACUBA CAUSEWAY - JUNE 30, 1521**

Alvarado charges a breach. Reckless. He falls back. The Aztecs swarm.

**VISUAL: 53 SPANIARDS CAPTURED. 4 HORSES. DRAGGED TO THE GREAT TEMPLE.**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
For days, the Spanish camps watched the smoke rise from the Great Temple. Heard the drums. Knew what was happening on those altars.

**HEAD FAKE #3 - THE EXPECTATION OF SURRENDER**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Starvation. Thirst. Smallpox. Dysentery. Eating leather. Drinking brackish water. Cannibalism reported in the codices. Any other city in history opens its gates.

**INT. TLATELOLCO MARKET RUINS - AUGUST 13, 1521 - DAY**

Cuauhtémoc stands in a canoe. Fifty nobles with him. The district is rubble.

**CUAUHTÉMOC (SUBTITLED, FROM CODEX AUBIN)**
*I have done my duty. I have defended my people. Take the dagger at your belt and kill me.*

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
He didn't surrender. He offered his life to save his people.

**EXT. CANOE - CONTINUOUS**

García Holguín’s brigantine rams the canoe. Cuauhtémoc jumps up, grabs the gunwale.

**CUAUHTÉMOC (TO CORTÉS)**
*Señor, ya hicistes vuestra parte... tomad ese puñal y matadme.* (You have done your part... take that dagger and kill me.)

**CORTÉS (TO CUauhtémoc)**
*You have defended your city like a brave warrior. A Spaniard knows how to respect valor.*

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
The fighting stopped. Not with a treaty. With a recognition between two warriors.

**VISUAL: THE CITY FALLS SILENT. THE TEMPLES BURN. THE LAKE TURNS RED.**

***

### EPILOGUE: THE PRICE OF THE FIFTH SUN

**EXT. TENOCHTITLAN RUINS - 1522**

**VISUAL: SPANISH SOLDIERS PUSHING DOWN TEMPLE STONES. BUILDING A CATHEDRAL ON THE GREAT TEMPLE'S FOUNDATION. THE LAKE BEGINNING TO RECEDE.**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Tenochtitlan was erased. Stone by stone. The lake was drained over centuries. Mexico City rose on the mud.

**GRAPHIC: POPULATION TIMELAPSE. 1.5 MILLION (1519) -> 150,000 (1600). 90% GONE.**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Smallpox. Measles. Typhus. *Cocoliztli*. The conquest wasn't the war. The conquest was the biology.

**VISUAL: TLATELOLCO CODEX PAINTING. INDIGENOUS SCRIBES WRITING IN ALPHABETIC NAHUATL. THE FLORENTINE CODEX BEING COMPILED.**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
But the people didn't vanish. The *tlacuilos*—the scribe-painters—learned the Roman alphabet. They wrote their own history. The *Florentine Codex*. The *Annals of Tlatelolco*. They sued in Spanish courts. They won land rights. They kept their language. Nahuatl is still spoken by 1.5 million people today.

**VISUAL: MODERN MEXICO CITY. THE ZÓCALO. THE CATHEDRAL. THE TEMPLE MAYOR EXCAVATION RIGHT NEXT TO IT. A STATUE OF CUAUHTÉMOC ON PASEO DE LA REFORMA.**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
Cortés died in Spain, bitter, litigious, denied the viceroyalty he craved. His bones were moved, hidden, lost. No tomb marks him in the city he founded.

Cuauhtémoc was tortured for gold that wasn't there. Hanged in Honduras on a trumped-up charge. He is the hero on the currency.

Malinche? She vanished from the record. A son, Martín, the first *mestizo* of note. She is called traitor. She is called mother. She is the translator who made the conversation possible.

**FINAL VISUAL: THE COYOLXAUHQUI STONE. THE DISMEMBERED MOON GODDESS. BURIED AT THE BASE OF THE GREAT TEMPLE. REDISCOVERED 1978.**

**NARRATOR (V.O.)**
The Fifth Sun did end in earthquake and darkness. But a Sixth Sun rose. Mixed. Broken. Resilient. Built on the bones of the greatest city the Americas ever knew.

**FADE TO BLACK.**

*In memory of the tlacuilos who wrote the other side of history.*
