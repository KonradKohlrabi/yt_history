## I. BACKGROUND: HISTORICAL CONTEXT & CAUSATION

### A. The "Irrepressible Conflict": Long-Term Causes (1787–1860)
*   **Constitutional Ambiguity:** The U.S. Constitution (1787) compromised on slavery (Three-Fifths Compromise, Fugitive Slave Clause, 1808 import ban) but failed to resolve sovereignty vs. union or the expansion of slavery into territories.
*   **Sectional Divergence (Antebellum Period):**
    *   **North:** Industrializing, urbanizing, "Free Labor" ideology, immigrant labor (Irish/German), growing railroad network, financial centers (NYC), abolitionist movement growing (Garrison, Douglass, Grimké sisters).
    *   **South:** Agrarian, plantation-based, staple-crop economy (Cotton, Tobacco, Rice, Sugar), reliant on chattel slavery (4 million enslaved by 1860), rigid planter aristocracy, limited industrial base/railroads, "King Cotton" diplomacy theory.
*   **Westward Expansion & The Balance of Power:** Every new state threatened the Senate balance between Free and Slave states.
    *   *Missouri Compromise (1820):* 36°30′ line.
    *   *Mexican Cession (1848) & Wilmot Proviso:* Failed attempt to ban slavery in new territory.
    *   *Compromise of 1850:* California free; Fugitive Slave Act strengthened; Popular Sovereignty in Utah/New Mexico.
    *   *Kansas-Nebraska Act (1854):* Stephen Douglas; Repealed Missouri Compromise; "Bleeding Kansas" guerrilla warfare.
    *   *Dred Scott v. Sandford (1857):* Taney Court rules Black people not citizens; Congress cannot ban slavery in territories; Missouri Compromise unconstitutional.
    *   *John Brown’s Raid (1859):* Harpers Ferry; Failed slave insurrection attempt; South terrified of Northern-backed insurrection; North martyrs Brown.

### B. Immediate Political Catalysts (1860–1861)
*   **Election of 1860:**
    *   **Democrats Split:** Northern (Douglas – Popular Sovereignty) vs. Southern (Breckinridge – Federal Slave Code).
    *   **Constitutional Union Party:** Bell (Union preservation only).
    *   **Republicans:** Lincoln (Non-extension of slavery, Homestead Act, Transcontinental Railroad, Protective Tariff).
    *   **Result:** Lincoln wins 180 Electoral Votes (39.8% popular), carries zero Southern states. South views this as loss of political voice.
*   **Secession Winter (Dec 1860 – Feb 1861):**
    *   South Carolina secedes Dec 20, 1860.
    *   Mississippi, Florida, Alabama, Georgia, Louisiana, Texas follow by Feb 1, 1861.
    *   **Crittenden Compromise fails:** Lincoln refuses compromise on expansion.
    *   **Montgomery Convention (Feb 1861):** Confederate States of America formed; Jefferson Davis elected Provisional President; Alexander Stephens VP ("Cornerstone Speech" – slavery/white supremacy as foundation).

## IV. LOCATIONS: STRATEGIC GEOGRAPHY

### A. Theaters of War
1.  **Eastern Theater:** Virginia, Maryland, Pennsylvania. (Focus: Richmond vs. Washington DC; 100 miles apart).
2.  **Western Theater:** Tennessee, Mississippi, Georgia, Kentucky. (Focus: Mississippi River, Rail hubs: Nashville, Chattanooga, Atlanta).
3.  **Trans-Mississippi Theater:** Missouri, Arkansas, Louisiana, Texas, Indian Territory. (Guerrilla warfare, limited conventional).
4.  **Lower Seaboard / Gulf:** Coastal forts, Blockade running, New Orleans, Mobile, Charleston, Wilmington.
5.  **Naval/Global:** Atlantic (Commerce raiders *Alabama*, *Florida*, *Shenandoah*), Pacific, Cherbourg (France).

### B. Critical Cities & Sites
*   **Washington, D.C.:** Union capital; Heavily fortified (Ring of forts); Target of Early's 1864 raid.
*   **Richmond, VA:** Confederate capital; Tredegar Iron Works (major artillery); Chimborazo Hospital; Evacuation Fire (Apr 1865).
*   **Mississippi River:** "Father of Waters"; Vicksburg (Gibraltar), Port Hudson, Island No. 10, Memphis, New Orleans. Control = Confederacy bisected.
*   **Railroad Hubs:** Chattanooga (Gateway to Deep South), Atlanta (Industrial heart), Corinth, Nashville, Petersburg (5 rail lines).
*   **Border States (Crucial Loyal Slave States):** Kentucky (Lincoln: "I hope to have God on my side, but I must have Kentucky"), Missouri (Guerrilla war), Maryland (Encircled DC), Delaware, West Virginia (created 1863).
*   **Prison Camps:** Andersonville (GA - Wirz, 13k dead), Elmira (NY - "Hellmira"), Libby Prison (Richmond), Camp Douglas (Chicago), Salisbury (NC).
*   **Industrial Sites:** Tredegar Iron Works (Richmond), Springfield Armory (MA), Dupont Powder Mills (DE), Selma Ordnance/Naval Foundry (AL).

## V. MAJOR EVENTS: DETAILED ANALYSIS

### 1. Fort Sumter (Apr 12–14, 1861)
*   **What:** Confederate bombardment of US garrison (Maj. Robert Anderson) in Charleston Harbor.
*   **Why:** Lincoln attempted resupply (non-violent); Davis ordered surrender/bombardment to solidify secession/force Upper South out.
*   **Involved:** Beauregard (CSA) vs. Anderson (USA).
*   **Immediate:** Anderson surrenders after 34 hrs; No combat deaths (1 death later in 100-gun salute). Lincoln calls 75k militia.
*   **Long-term:** Unified North; Virginia/Arkansas/North Carolina/Tennessee secede; War becomes existential.

### 2. First Bull Run / Manassas (Jul 21, 1861)
*   **What:** First major land battle. Union (McDowell) flanks Confederates (Johnston/Beauregard) at Matthews Hill; Jackson holds Henry Hill ("Stonewall"); Confederate counterattack routes green Union troops.
*   **Why:** Political pressure for "On to Richmond"; 90-day enlistments expiring.
*   **Consequences:** Confederate victory; "Young Napoleon" McClellan called to organize Army of Potomac; South overconfident ("One Southerner = 10 Yankees"); North realizes long war.

### 3. The Trent Affair (Nov–Dec 1861)
*   **What:** USS *San Jacinto* (Capt. Wilkes) stops British mail packet *Trent*, removes Confederate envoys Mason & Slidell.
*   **Why:** Desperate Confederate diplomacy; Wilkes overstepped orders.
*   **Consequences:** UK demands release/apology; 11,000 troops sent to Canada; War scare. Lincoln releases envoys ("One war at a time"). UK recognizes Confederate *belligerency* (not sovereignty).

### 4. Henry & Donelson (Feb 1862)
*   **What:** Grant/Foote combined arms. Fort Henry (flooded, falls fast) -> Fort Donelson (invested). Pillow/Floyd flee; Buckner asks terms. Grant: "No terms except unconditional and immediate surrender."
*   **Consequences:** 12-15k CSA captured; Cumberland/Tennessee Rivers open; Nashville falls (first Confederate capital); Grant national hero.

### 5. Monitor vs. Virginia (Merrimack) (Mar 8–9, 1862)
*   **What:** CSS *Virginia* (casemate ironclad on *Merrimack* hull) destroys wooden USS *Cumberland*/*Congress* Day 1. USS *Monitor* (turret ironclad, Ericsson) arrives night. Day 2: Duel ends in draw.
*   **Significance:** Instant obsolescence of wooden navies worldwide; Union blockade secured; Ironclad arms race begins.

### 6. Shiloh / Pittsburg Landing (Apr 6–7, 1862)
*   **What:** Johnston launches surprise attack on Grant (unfortified). "Hornet's Nest" (Prentiss/Hornet's Nest) holds. Johnston killed (bleeds out, tourniquet not applied). Beauregard calls off attack nightfall. Buell reinforces Grant overnight. Union counterattack Day 2 drives CSA back.
*   **Numbers:** ~110k engaged; ~23,746 casualties (more than all previous US wars combined).
*   **Consequences:** Strategic Union win; Corinth falls; West Tennessee secured; "I saw an open field... so covered with dead that it would have been possible to walk across... without touching the ground" (Grant).

### 7. Antietam / Sharpsburg (Sep 17, 1862)
*   **What:** Lee invades MD (Special Order 191 lost/wrapped around cigars found by Union). McClellan attacks piecemeal. Morning (Cornfield/Dunker Church/West Woods - carnage). Midday (Sunken Road/Bloody Lane). Afternoon (Burnside Bridge). A.P. Hill arrives from Harpers Ferry saves Lee right flank.
*   **Numbers:** ~22,717 casualties (Bloodiest Single Day).
*   **Consequences:** Tactical draw; Lee retreats. McClellan fails to pursue (fired Nov). **Lincoln issues Preliminary Emancipation Proclamation.** UK/France back off recognition.

### 8. Emancipation Proclamation (Jan 1, 1863)
*   **Legal Basis:** War Powers / Military Necessity (Confiscation Acts).
*   **Scope:** Only states "in rebellion" (exempts Border States, occupied TN/LA/VA counties). Did not free slaves in Union territory.
*   **Impact:** War aim shifts to Abolition; Authorizes USCT (180k+ serve); Prevents European intervention; Encourages escape (contrabands).

### 9. Gettysburg (Jul 1–3, 1863)
*   **Day 1:** Heth (CSA) vs. Buford (US Cav) -> Reynolds (US I Corps) killed. Union driven through town to Cemetery Hill. Ewell fails to take Culp's Hill.
*   **Day 2:** Longstreet attacks Late (Sickles moves III Corps forward to Peach Orchard/Devil's Den/Wheatfield). Chamberlain holds Little Round Top (20th Maine bayonet charge). Ewell attacks East Cemetery Hill/Culp's Hill.
*   **Day 3:** Lee orders Center attack (Pickett/Pettigrew/Trimble) preceded by massive artillery barrage (largely overshot). "High Water Mark" (Armistead breaches wall, killed). Pickett's division destroyed. Cavalry battle East (Custer vs. Stuart) inconclusive.
*   **Numbers:** ~165k engaged; ~51k casualties (23k US, 28k CSA).
*   **Consequences:** Lee retreats; Meade fails to trap at Potomac. Vicksburg falls same day (Jul 4). Strategic turning point.

### 10. Vicksburg Campaign & Siege (Mar–Jul 4, 1863)
*   **Strategy:** Grant's amphibious maneuver (cross below, Bruinsburg, Port Gibson, Raymond, Jackson, Champion Hill, Big Black River).
*   **Siege:** 47 days. Civilians in caves. Mines (Crater precursor). Surrender Jul 4 (Paroled ~29k).
*   **Consequences:** "Father of Waters flows unvexed to the sea." Confederacy split (Trans-Mississippi isolated). Grant promoted.

### 11. New York Draft Riots (Jul 13–16, 1863)
*   **Causes:** Conscription Act (1863) - $300 commutation / Substitution (Rich man's war, poor man's fight). Racial hatred (Irish immigrants vs. Black workers). Democratic opposition.
*   **Events:** Mob attacks draft office, burns Colored Orphan Asylum, lynches Black men, fights police/army (troops diverted from Gettysburg).
*   **Casualties:** ~120 dead (official), estimates up to 1,000. Massive property damage.

### 12. Overland Campaign (May–Jun 1864)
*   **Strategy:** Grant moves *with* Army of Potomac (Meade retained command). Simultaneous advances: Sherman (ATL), Sigel (Valley - failed), Butler (Bermuda Hundred - bottled up), Crook/Averell (Railroads).
*   **Battles:**
    *   **Wilderness (May 5–7):** Dense woods negate Union artillery; horrific fires burn wounded. ~29k casualties.
    *   **Spotsylvania (May 8–21):** "Bloody Angle" (May 12) - 20 hrs hand-to-hand in rain/mud. Mule Shoe salient.
    *   **North Anna (May 23–26):** Lee's inverted V trap; Grant declines assault.
    *   **Cold Harbor (Jun 1–3):** Grant's greatest regret. Jun 3 assault: 7k casualties in 30-60 mins. "I have lost my right arm" (Lee on Longstreet's wound earlier).
*   **Result:** Grant crosses James River (engineering feat) -> Petersburg. Strategic success (Lee pinned), Tactical horror.

### 13. Sherman’s March to the Sea (Nov 15 – Dec 21, 1864)
*   **Force:** 62,000 veterans (Right Wing - Howard; Left Wing - Slocum). Cut loose from supply lines.
*   **Doctrine:** "Hard War" / Total War. Forage liberally. Destroy railroads (Sherman's Neckties - heat rails, wrap on trees), industry, infrastructure. Target *capacity* to wage war, not civilians directly (though suffering immense).
*   **Psychological:** "War is cruelty... the crueler it is, the sooner it will be over."
*   **Result:** Savannah captured (Christmas gift to Lincoln). $100M+ damage (1864 $). Marches North through Carolinas (Columbia burns).

### 14. Appomattox Campaign & Surrender (Apr 1–9, 1865)
*   **Five Forks (Apr 1):** Sheridan breaks Lee's right flank. Lee evacuates Petersburg/Richmond.
*   **Retreat:** West along Appomattox River. Sailor's Creek (Apr 6) - 8k CSA captured (Ewell, Custis Lee).
*   **Appomattox Court House (Apr 9):** Lee surrounded. "Then there is nothing left me but to go and see General Grant, and I would rather die a thousand deaths."
*   **Terms:** Generous. Officers keep sidearms/horses. Men keep horses (spring planting). Paroled, not imprisoned. Rations issued to starving Confederates. "The rebels are our countrymen again" (Grant stops saluting).

## VI. MILITARY ANALYSIS

### A. Army Organization & Size
*   **Union:** ~2.1–2.2 million total served (incl. 180k USCT, 20k Native Americans, ~25k Navy). Peak strength ~1 million (1864). Corps system (Infantry, Cavalry, Artillery).
*   **Confederacy:** ~750k–1 million total served. Peak strength ~450k–500k (1863). Chronic manpower shortage (Conscription Acts 1862, 1864 - ages 17–50).
*   **Immigrants:** ~25% Union army foreign-born (Irish ~150k, Germans ~175k). "Dutch" (German) corps (XI Corps).

### B. Weapons & Technology
*   **Small Arms:** **Rifled Musket (Minie Ball)** standard (.58 cal Springfield / .577 Enfield). Range 300-500 yds (effective), lethal 1000+. *Tactics lagged:* Napoleonic close-order assaults vs. defensive firepower = slaughter.
*   **Repeaters:** Spencer (7-shot), Henry (16-shot), Sharps (breechloader) - Cavalry/USCT/elite units only (logistics/conservatism limited issue).
*   **Artillery:** **Napoleon 12-pdr Smoothbore** (workhorse, canister deadly <400 yds). **10-lb Parrott Rifle / 3-inch Ordnance Rifle** (long range accuracy). **Heavy Siege Guns** (Rodman, Parrott 200/300-lb) at Petersburg/Charleston.
*   **Ironclads:** *Monitor* (Turret), *Virginia* (Casemate), City-class "Pook Turtles" (River), *New Ironsides*, *Keokuk*, *Galena*. Torpedoes (Mines) - sank *Cairo*, *Tecumseh*, *Milwaukee*.
*   **Submarines/Balloon:** *H.L. Hunley* (first sub to sink ship - *Housatonic*, then lost). Union Balloon Corps (Lowe) - Aerial recon (First Battle of Bull Run, Peninsula).
*   **Railroads/Telegraph:** US Military Railroads (Haupt) - strategic mobility. US Military Telegraph (Eckert) - Lincoln in War Dept reading ciphered telegrams real-time.
*   **Medicine:** Amputation primary surgery (Chloroform/Ether anesthesia - 95% used). **Germ theory unknown.** "Laudable pus" = infection. Sanitation Commission (Olmsted/Bellows) - volunteer relief, hygiene, kitchens. Letterman Ambulance System (triage/evac).

### C. Strategy & Command
*   **Union (Winfield Scott -> McClellan -> Halleck -> Grant):**
    *   *Anaconda Plan:* Blockade + Mississippi control + Stranglehold.
    *   *Grant's 1864 Strategy:* **Simultaneous Pressure** (Coordinate all armies so no CSA army reinforces another). Attrition (North replaces losses, South cannot). Target *Armies*, not cities (except Atlanta/Richmond as logistical hubs).
*   **Confederacy (Davis/Lee):**
    *   *Offensive-Defensive:* Defend territory, but strike North when opportunity arises (Antietam, Gettysburg) to break morale/force election/recognition.
    *   *Interior Lines:* Attempt to shift troops between theaters (often failed due to rail gauge breaks, distances, Union naval control).

## VII. POLITICS & DIPLOMACY

### A. Union Politics
*   **Republican Party:** Radicals (Stevens, Sumner, Wade - Immediate emancipation, harsh reconstruction, Black suffrage) vs. Moderates (Lincoln, Seward - Pragmatic, Union first) vs. Conservatives (Blair - Conservative on race).
*   **Democrats:** War Democrats (Support war, oppose emancipation - McClellan) vs. Copperheads/Peace Democrats (Vallandigham - Oppose war, demand negotiated peace, states' rights, racism).
*   **Key Legislation:**
    *   *Homestead Act (1862):* 160 acres free land.
    *   *Morrill Land-Grant Act (1862):* Agricultural colleges.
    *   *Pacific Railway Act (1862):* Transcontinental railroad.
    *   *National Banking Acts (1863/64):* National currency, bond sales.
    *   *Conscription Act (1863):* First federal draft; $300 commutation.
    *   *13th Amendment (Jan 1865):* Abolishes slavery.
*   **Civil Liberties:** Habeas Corpus suspended (Merryman, Vallandigham arrest); Military tribunals for civilians (*Ex parte Milligan* 1866 limits this post-war).

### B. Confederate Politics
*   **Structure:** No parties (anti-party ideology). Governors (Brown GA, Vance NC) vs. Central Govt (Davis). States' rights hindered conscription, impressment, taxation.
*   **Economy:** **Impressment Act / Tax-in-Kind (1863)** - Seized produce; caused famine/hoarding. **Inflation:** 9,000% by 1865. Currency worthless. Salt shortage critical.
*   **Diplomacy:** **King Cotton Diplomacy** (Voluntary embargo 1861) -> Backfired (UK had stockpiles, found Egypt/India). **Trent Affair** close call. **Erlanger Loan** (Cotton bonds) - limited success. **Pope Pius IX** addressed Davis as "President" (de facto recognition only).

### C. International Diplomacy
*   **United Kingdom:** Palmerston/Russell/Gladstone. Cotton famine (Lancashire workers unemployed) -> Workers *supported* Union (Emancipation). *Alabama* Claims (Commerce Raiders* built in UK (Laird) -> *Alabama*, *Florida*, *Shenandoah* destroy 250+ US merchant ships. Post-war arbitration (Geneva Tribunal 1872) - US awarded $15.5M gold.
*   **France:** Napoleon III. Wanted cotton, Mexican empire (Maximilian). Needed UK partnership. Refused to act alone.
*   **Russia:** Friendly to US (Fleet visits NY/SF 1863 - symbolic support).

## VIII. SOCIETY & DAILY LIFE

### A. The Home Front (North)
*   **Economy:** Boom. Industrial output ↑. Greenbacks ($450M) finance war. Income tax (first federal). Women enter workforce (Treasury Dept, Arsenal, Nursing, Teaching).
*   **Sanitary Commission / Christian Commission:** Massive civilian volunteer networks (Women: Bickerdyke, Livermore, Dix). Fairs raise millions. "Sanitary Fairs" (Chicago, NYC).
*   **Culture:** Photography (Brady, Gardner, O'Sullivan - *Dead of Antietam* shocks public). Walt Whitman (Nurse, *Drum-Taps*). Emily Dickinson. Baseball spreads (soldiers play).
*   **Politics:** 1864 Election - Soldiers vote in field (78% Lincoln). "Don't change horses mid-stream."

### B. The Home Front (South)
*   **Economy:** Collapse. Blockade + Loss of territory + Impressment + Inflation = Starvation. **Richmond Bread Riot (Apr 1863)** - Women loot shops.
*   **Women:** Manage plantations/farms. "Government Girls" clerks in Richmond. Spies (Rose Greenhow, Belle Boyd). Nurses (Sallie Tompkins, Phoebe Pember - Chimborazo).
*   **Enslaved People:** **Self-Emancipation.** "Contrabands" flock to Union lines (Fort Monroe, Corinth, Sea Islands). **Port Royal Experiment** (1862) - Freedmen farming cotton for wages, schools (Laura Towne). **Juneteenth** (TX 1865).
*   **Guerrilla War:** Missouri (Quantrill, Bloody Bill Anderson - Lawrence Massacre), Kentucky, West Virginia, East Tennessee. "Bushwhackers" vs "Jayhawkers". Order No. 11 (Ewing) depopulates MO counties.

### C. Soldier Life
*   **Demographics:** Average age 25 (Range 12-60+). Farmer (48% Union, 69% CSA). Literacy high (90% Union, 80% CSA) -> Massive letter writing.
*   **Camp Life:** Drill, fatigue duty, disease (Measles, Dysentery, Typhoid - 2:1 disease:battle deaths), boredom, gambling, prayer meetings, newspapers (*Stars and Stripes*).
*   **Food:** Union: Salt pork, Hardtack, Coffee (vital), Desiccated vegetables. CSA: Cornmeal (Cornpone/Ashcake), Peanuts, Sorghum, *No Coffee* (Substitutes: chicory, acorns, sweet potato).
*   **Medicine:** "Sawbones" reputation. Amputation piles. Pavilion Hospitals (Chimborazo 8k beds, Satterlee 4.5k). Dorothea Dix (Union Nurses), Phoebe Pember (CSA Matron).

### D. United States Colored Troops (USCT)
*   **Numbers:** ~179,000 Army, ~19,000 Navy. 10% of Union manpower.
*   **Units:** 54th MA (Shaw - Fort Wagner), 1st Kansas (First engaged - Island Mound), 25th Corps (First into Richmond).
*   **Discrimination:** Lower pay ($10 vs $13 - equalized 1864 retroactive), No commissions initially (rare later), Massacre risk if captured (Fort Pillow, Poison Spring, Petersburg Crater).
*   **Medal of Honor:** 25 awarded (14 at New Market Heights/Chaffin's Farm).

## X. VISUAL DETAILS FOR DOCUMENTARY PRODUCTION

### A. Uniforms & Clothing
*   **Union:** **Dark Blue Frock Coat / Sack Coat (Fatigue)**, Sky Blue Kersey Trousers. **Kepi (Forage Cap)** or Hardee Hat (Black Hat - Iron Brigade). Greatcoat (Sky Blue). Leather Brogans (Pegged/Sewn). **Corps Badges** (Diamond I, Trefoil II, Maltese Cross III, Triangle IV, Cross V, Greek Cross VI, Crescent XI, Star XII, Acorn XIV - added 1863).
*   **Confederate:** **Gray / Butternut (Homespun/Dyed)**. Frock/Shell Jacket. Trousers often mixed (Union sky blue captured). **Slouch Hat** (Wide brim, often pinned up). **No standard shoe** (Brogans, Moccasins, Barefoot). **Rank:** Collar stars (Generals), Sleeve bars (Officers), Chevrons (NCOs).
*   **USCT:** Standard Union issue. White officers.
*   **Zouaves:** Colorful (Red baggy trousers, short jacket, fez/turban) - 5th NY, 114th PA, 146th NY, LA Tigers (CSA).
*   **Civilians:** Hoop skirts (Crinoline), Bonnets, Frock coats, Top hats (Lincoln), Bowler hats. Mourning attire (Black crepe, Jet jewelry) ubiquitous.

### B. Weapons & Equipment Visuals
*   **Springfield 1861/1863:** Long (56"), Three bands, Ramrod. **Bayonet** (Socket, triangular).
*   **Enfield 1853:** Similar, Brass furniture.
*   **Accoutrements:** Black Leather Belt (US Oval Plate / CS Oval or State Seal), Cap Box (Cone/Perccusion caps), Cartridge Box (40 rounds, Tin liners), Bayonet Scabbard, Haversack (Painted canvas - rations), Canteen (Tin/Smooth side or Wood/Confederate), Knapsack (Double bag - blanket, shelter half, extra clothes).
*   **Artillery:** **Napoleon** (Green/Blue barrel, distinct muzzle swell). **Limber & Caisson** (Ammunition chests). **Battery Wagon / Forge**. Horses (6 per gun).
*   **Ironclads:** *Monitor* (Cheesebox on raft, Revolving Turret, Low freeboard). *Virginia* (Sloped Casemate, Iron plating, Ram bow). **Torpedoes (Mines):** Barrels/Kegs anchored.

### C. Landscapes & Architecture
*   **Battlefields:** Rolling hills (Gettysburg), Dense Second-growth Woods (Wilderness - "The Wilderness"), Open Fields (Antietam Cornfield), Trenches/Peterburg (9 months - zigzag, traverses, bombproofs, "The Crater").
*   **Cities:** **Richmond:** Tredegar Iron Works (Brick, Chimneys), Capitol (Jefferson design), Libby Prison (Tobacco Warehouse - 3 stories, basement), Chimborazo Hospital (Hilltop, 150 wards). **Washington:** Capitol (Dome unfinished -> completed Dec 1863), Willard Hotel, White House, Ring Forts (Stevens, Slocum). **Charleston:** Fort Sumter (Brick ruin), Battery Mansions, Ruins of 1861 Fire.
*   **Hospitals:** Pavilion style (Long wooden wards, ventilation, central admin). Amputation tables. **Ambulances:** Tri-mule / Four-wheel (Rucker / Rosecrans).
*   **Photography:** Wet Plate Collodion (Glass negatives). Long exposures (No action shots). Stereo views (3D). **Gardner's "Photographic Sketch Book"**, Brady Studios. **Dead bodies** (Bloody Lane, Devil's Den, Petersburg trenches) - *First war photographed dead extensively*.

### D. Flags & Symbols
*   **US:** 33, 34, 35, 36 Stars (Added KS, WV, NV). **Regimental Colors:** Blue field, Eagle, Stars, Battle Honors. **Headquarters Flags:** Swallowtail (Army, Corps, Division).
*   **CSA:** **First National "Stars and Bars"** (7-13 stars, 3 bars - confused on battlefield). **Second National "Stainless Banner"** (White field, Battle Flag canton - mistaken for truce). **Third National "Blood Stained Banner"** (Red vertical bar added). **Battle Flag (Southern Cross):** Blue St. Andrew's Cross, White Stars, Red Field (Square for ANV, Rectangular for West/Naval Jack). **Regimental Flags:** Battle Flag variants, State seals.

## XI. CONSEQUENCES & LEGACY

### A. Immediate Consequences (1865–1877)
*   **Human Cost:** 620k–750k dead (2.5% of population = ~7M today). 1.5M casualties. Generation of maimed men (Pension system largest federal expense by 1890).
*   **Economic:** South destroyed (2/3 wealth gone - slaves $3B value zeroed, infrastructure, livestock). North industrial boom accelerated (Gilded Age foundation). National Debt $2.7B (1865) -> $1B by 1890.
*   **Political:** **Reconstruction Begins.**
    *   *13th Amendment (Dec 1865):* Abolition.
    *   *Freedmen's Bureau (Mar 1865):* Education, Labor contracts, Legal aid.
    *   *Civil Rights Act (1866) / 14th Amendment (1868):* Citizenship, Equal Protection, Due Process.
    *   *Reconstruction Acts (1867):* Military Districts, Black Male Suffrage.
    *   *15th Amendment (1870):* Voting Rights.
    *   *Impeachment of Andrew Johnson (1868).*
    *   *Grant Presidency (1869–77):* Enforcement Acts (KKK), 15th Amend, Panic of 1873.
    *   *Compromise of 1877:* Hayes/Tilden -> Federal Troops withdrawn -> "Redemption" / Jim Crow begins.

### B. Long-Term Historical Impact
*   **Federalism Settled:** Secession illegal (*Texas v. White* 1869). Federal Supremacy established. "United States *is*" (singular verb).
*   **Constitutional Revolution:** 13th, 14th, 15th Amendments (Second Founding). Basis for 20th Century Civil Rights (Brown v Board, Civil Rights Act 1964, Voting Rights Act 1965, Obergefell, etc.).
*   **Modern Warfare:** Trench warfare (Petersburg), Rail logistics, Telegraph command/control, Ironclads, Total War doctrine, Mass conscription, Industrial slaughter.
*   **National Identity:** "The Union" -> "The Nation." Memorial Day (Decoration Day 1868). Veterans Organizations (GAR - Grand Army of the Republic; UCV - United Confederate Veterans). Pension Bureaus.
*   **Lost Cause Mythology:** (Late 19th/Early 20th C). Jubal Early, Southern Historical Society, UDC (United Daughters of Confederacy). Narrative: States' Rights (not slavery), Lee/Saintly, Grant/Butcher, Slaves loyal/happy, Reconstruction/Tragic. **Monuments:** Vast majority erected 1890–1920 (Jim Crow era) & 1950s (Civil Rights resistance).
*   **Westward Expansion Accelerated:** Homestead Act, Transcontinental Railroad (Completed 1869), Indian Wars intensified (Sand Creek 1864, Little Bighorn 1876) - Union veterans/Generals (Sherman, Sheridan, Custer) lead campaigns.

### C. Legacy in Memory & Historiography
*   **Centennial (1961–65):** Cold War context; Segregationist commemorations; Civil Rights Movement overlap.
*   **Sesquicentennial (2011–15):** Slavery centered as cause; Inclusive narratives (USCT, Women, Native Americans, Immigrants); Removal of Lost Cause monuments (Charlottesville 2017, 2020 protests).
*   **Key Historiographical Shifts:** Dunning School (Reconstruction failure) -> Revisionists (Beard - Economic) -> Modern (Foner, McPherson, Blight, Glymph, Manning) - Slavery central, Agency of Enslaved, Guerrilla War, Environmental History, Memory Studies.
