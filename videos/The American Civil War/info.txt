# Comprehensive Historical Research: The American Civil War (1861–1865)

---

## I. BACKGROUND

### Historical Context
- **United States in mid-19th century**: Rapid territorial expansion (Louisiana Purchase 1803, Mexican–American War 1846–48, Oregon Treaty 1846), population growth (31.4 million in 1860), industrialization in North, plantation agriculture in South.
- **Sectionalism**: Divergent economic systems, labor systems, political cultures, and visions for the nation’s future.
- **Constitutional ambiguity**: Constitution did not explicitly address secession; states’ rights vs. federal authority debated since founding.

### Causes
#### Primary Cause: Slavery
- **Economic**: Southern economy dependent on enslaved labor for cotton, tobacco, rice, sugar. Cotton = 59% of U.S. exports (1860). 4 million enslaved people valued at ~$3.5 billion (largest single asset in U.S. economy).
- **Political**: Balance of power in Senate (free vs. slave states). Expansion of slavery into territories (Missouri Compromise 1820, Compromise of 1850, Kansas–Nebraska Act 1854, Dred Scott decision 1857).
- **Social/ideological**: Abolitionist movement (William Lloyd Garrison, Frederick Douglass, Harriet Beecher Stowe’s *Uncle Tom’s Cabin* 1852). Pro-slavery ideology (John C. Calhoun, George Fitzhugh) arguing slavery a “positive good.”
- **Immediate trigger**: Election of Abraham Lincoln (Republican, anti-slavery expansion) November 1860 → secession of seven Deep South states before inauguration.

#### Contributing Factors
- **States’ rights**: Southern argument that states could nullify federal laws or secede. Northern counter: Union perpetual, secession illegal.
- **Tariffs**: Southern opposition to protective tariffs (Tariff of Abominations 1828, Morrill Tariff 1861) seen as benefiting Northern industry at Southern expense.
- **Cultural divergence**: Different labor systems, education, urbanization, religion (split in Baptist, Methodist, Presbyterian denominations 1840s–50s).

### Political Situation (1860)
- **Party system collapse**: Whigs dissolved; Democrats split Northern (Stephen Douglas) / Southern (John C. Breckinridge); Constitutional Union (John Bell); Republicans (Lincoln) sectional Northern party.
- **Lincoln victory**: 39.8% popular vote, 180 electoral votes (all Northern states). Not on ballot in 10 Southern states.
- **Secession winter (Dec 1860–Mar 1861)**: South Carolina (Dec 20), Mississippi, Florida, Alabama, Georgia, Louisiana, Texas (Feb 1). Formed Confederate States of America (Feb 4–8, Montgomery, AL). Jefferson Davis provisional president (Feb 9). Upper South (VA, NC, TN, AR) initially rejected secession.

### Economic Situation
| Metric | Union (23 states) | Confederacy (11 states) |
|--------|-------------------|-------------------------|
| Population (1860) | 22.3 million | 9.1 million (3.5M enslaved) |
| Industrial output | 90% of U.S. manufacturing | 10% |
| Railroad mileage | 22,000 miles | 9,000 miles (different gauges) |
| Naval power | Established navy, shipyards | Almost none |
| Financial system | National banking acts (1863–64), greenbacks, bonds | Reliance on cotton diplomacy, printing press → hyperinflation |

### Social Situation
- **North**: Urbanization, immigration (4.2 million 1840–60, mostly Irish/German), wage labor, public education, reform movements (temperance, women’s rights).
- **South**: Rural, planter aristocracy (~8,000 families owned >50 enslaved people), yeoman farmers (75% of white families owned no slaves), poor whites, enslaved communities with distinct culture (Gullah, spirituals, resistance).
- **Border states**: Delaware, Maryland, Kentucky, Missouri – slave states that remained in Union; internal divisions, guerrilla warfare.

### Religious Situation
- **Second Great Awakening** influence: Evangelical Protestantism dominant in both sections.
- **Denominational splits**: Methodist Episcopal (1844), Baptist (1845), Presbyterian (1857) over slavery.
- **Civil religion**: Both sides claimed divine favor; “Battle Hymn of the Republic” vs. “God Save the South.”
- **African American churches**: Independent Black denominations (AME, AME Zion) centers of community and abolitionism.

## II. TIMELINE: KEY DATES & CHRONOLOGICAL EVENTS

### 1860–1861: Secession & Outbreak
- **Nov 6, 1860**: Lincoln elected.
- **Dec 20, 1860**: South Carolina secedes.
- **Feb 4–8, 1861**: Montgomery Convention → Confederate Constitution (mirrored U.S. but protected slavery explicitly, single 6-year presidential term, line-item veto).
- **Feb 9, 1861**: Jefferson Davis elected provisional CSA president; Alexander Stephens VP.
- **Mar 4, 1861**: Lincoln inaugurated. “We are not enemies, but friends…” but “the Union… will constitutionally defend and maintain itself.”
- **Apr 12–13, 1861**: Bombardment of Fort Sumter (Charleston Harbor). Major Robert Anderson surrenders after 34 hours. No casualties in battle.
- **Apr 15, 1861**: Lincoln calls for 75,000 90-day volunteers → Virginia, Arkansas, Tennessee, North Carolina secede (Confederacy = 11 states).
- **Apr 19, 1861**: Lincoln proclaims blockade of Southern ports (Anaconda Plan).
- **May 24, 1861**: Union occupies Arlington Heights, Alexandria, VA.
- **Jul 21, 1861**: First Battle of Bull Run / Manassas → Confederate victory. Thomas “Stonewall” Jackson earns nickname.

### 1862: Expansion of War & Emancipation
- **Feb 6–16**: Fort Henry & Fort Donelson (TN) → Ulysses S. Grant captures forts, “Unconditional Surrender” Grant. Opens Tennessee/Cumberland Rivers.
- **Mar 8–9**: Battle of Hampton Roads – CSS *Virginia* (ex-*Merrimack*) vs. USS *Monitor* → first ironclad duel, ends wooden navies.
- **Apr 6–7**: Battle of Shiloh (Pittsburg Landing, TN) – 23,000 casualties, bloodiest battle to date. Grant holds, Corinth captured.
- **Apr 16**: Confederate Conscription Act (first in American history) – white men 18–35 (later 17–50).
- **Apr 25–May 1**: David Farragut captures New Orleans (largest Confederate city).
- **May 20**: Homestead Act signed (160 acres free land after 5 years).
- **Jul 1**: Pacific Railway Act (transcontinental railroad).
- **Jul 2**: Morrill Land-Grant Colleges Act.
- **Aug 28–30**: Second Battle of Bull Run – Lee/Jackson defeat Pope.
- **Sep 17**: Battle of Antietam / Sharpsburg (MD) – bloodiest single day (22,700 casualties). Lee’s invasion halted.
- **Sep 22**: Preliminary Emancipation Proclamation issued.
- **Dec 13**: Battle of Fredericksburg (VA) – Burnside’s futile assaults on Marye’s Heights → 12,600 Union casualties.

### 1863: Turning Points
- **Jan 1**: Final Emancipation Proclamation takes effect (rebellious states only). Authorizes Black enlistment.
- **Mar 3**: Enrollment Act (Union conscription) – $300 commutation fee, substitute provision → “rich man’s war, poor man’s fight.”
- **Apr 30–May 6**: Battle of Chancellorsville (VA) – Lee’s “perfect battle,” Jackson mortally wounded by friendly fire.
- **May 18–Jul 4**: Siege of Vicksburg (MS) – Grant’s masterpiece. 47-day surrender. Union controls Mississippi River. “The Father of Waters again goes unvexed to the sea.”
- **Jul 1–3**: Battle of Gettysburg (PA) – 51,000 casualties. Pickett’s Charge fails. Lee retreats. Meade fails to pursue.
- **Jul 13–16**: New York City Draft Riots – 120+ dead, mostly Black victims, anti-Black violence, class/ethnic tensions.
- **Aug 21**: Lawrence Massacre (Quantrill’s Raiders, MO/KS border war).
- **Sep 19–20**: Battle of Chickamauga (GA) – Bragg defeats Rosecrans. Union trapped in Chattanooga.
- **Nov 19**: Gettysburg Address – Lincoln redefines war as “new birth of freedom.”
- **Nov 23–25**: Battles for Chattanooga (Lookout Mountain, Missionary Ridge) – Grant breaks siege.

### 1864: Total War & Election
- **Mar 9**: Grant appointed General-in-Chief (Lieutenant General, rank revived). Meade stays Army of Potomac commander.
- **May 4–Jun 12**: Overland Campaign (Wilderness, Spotsylvania, Cold Harbor) – 55,000 Union casualties in 40 days. Grant: “I propose to fight it out on this line if it takes all summer.”
- **Jun 15–Apr 2, 1865**: Siege of Petersburg (VA) – 9-month trench warfare precursor to WWI.
- **May 7–Sep 2**: Atlanta Campaign – Sherman vs. Johnston/Hood. Fall of Atlanta (Sep 2) secures Lincoln re-election.
- **Jun 8**: Republican National Convention renominates Lincoln (Andrew Johnson VP, War Democrat).
- **Aug 29–Sep 1**: Democratic Convention – platform calls war a failure, nominates McClellan (who repudiates platform).
- **Nov 8**: Lincoln wins 212–21 electoral votes, 55% popular. Soldier vote ~78% Lincoln.
- **Nov 15–Dec 21**: Sherman’s March to the Sea (Atlanta → Savannah) – “hard war,” $100M destruction, 60-mile wide path.

### 1865: Collapse & Aftermath
- **Jan 31**: 13th Amendment passes Congress (ratified Dec 6, 1865).
- **Feb 3**: Hampton Roads Conference (Lincoln/Stephens) – no agreement; Lincoln demands reunion + emancipation.
- **Mar 4**: Lincoln Second Inaugural – “with malice toward none, with charity for all…”
- **Mar 25**: Fort Stedman (Lee’s last offensive fails).
- **Apr 1**: Five Forks (Grant breaks Petersburg lines).
- **Apr 2–3**: Fall of Petersburg & Richmond. Davis flees. Lincoln visits Richmond (Apr 4).
- **Apr 9**: Appomattox Court House – Lee surrenders Army of Northern Virginia (28,000 men). Grant’s generous terms.
- **Apr 14–15**: Lincoln assassinated by John Wilkes Booth at Ford’s Theatre. Dies 7:22 AM Apr 15. Andrew Johnson sworn in.
- **Apr 26**: Johnston surrenders to Sherman at Bennett Place (NC) – largest surrender (89,000).
- **May 5**: Davis captured near Irwinville, GA.
- **May 10**: Jefferson Davis captured.
- **May 26**: Kirby Smith surrenders Trans-Mississippi Dept (last major Confederate force).
- **Jun 19**: Juneteenth – Granger announces emancipation in Galveston, TX.
- **Nov 10**: Henry Wirz (Andersonville commandant) executed – only Confederate executed for war crimes.

## III. IMPORTANT PEOPLE

### Union Leadership
#### Abraham Lincoln (1809–1865)
- **Role**: 16th U.S. President, Commander-in-Chief.
- **Goals**: Preserve Union; eventually end slavery.
- **Actions**: Managed war effort, political coalition, emancipation strategy, Reconstruction vision (10% plan).
- **Relationships**: Contentious with McClellan; trusted Grant; Seward (SecState), Chase (Treasury), Stanton (War).
- **Influence**: Central figure; political genius held border states, managed radicals/conservatives, defined war’s meaning.

#### Ulysses S. Grant (1822–1885)
- **Role**: General-in-Chief (Mar 1864); 18th President.
- **Goals**: Destroy Confederate armies, not just capture territory.
- **Actions**: Fort Donelson, Vicksburg, Chattanooga, Overland Campaign, Petersburg, Appomattox.
- **Relationships**: Lincoln’s trust; Sherman (closest friend); raw deal with Meade; staff (Rawlins, Badeau).
- **Influence**: Modern strategist – coordinated multi-theater operations, logistics, total war.

#### William T. Sherman (1820–1891)
- **Role**: Army of the Tennessee / Military Division of the Mississippi commander.
- **Goals**: Break Southern will, destroy economic infrastructure.
- **Actions**: Atlanta Campaign, March to the Sea, Carolinas Campaign.
- **Relationships**: Grant’s alter ego; hated by South; controversial “hard war” practitioner.
- **Influence**: Psychological warfare, logistics, post-war Indian Wars commander.

#### George B. McClellan (1826–1885)
- **Role**: Army of Potomac commander (1861–62), General-in-Chief (Nov 1861–Mar 1862), 1864 Democratic nominee.
- **Goals**: Restore Union with minimal social revolution; opposed emancipation.
- **Actions**: Peninsula Campaign, Antietam (failed to pursue), overestimated enemy strength.
- **Relationships**: Lincoln’s frustration; beloved by soldiers (“Little Mac”); political rival.
- **Influence**: Created professional Army of Potomac; caution prolonged war.

### Confederate Leadership
#### Jefferson Davis (1808–1889)
- **Role**: President of CSA (1861–65).
- **Goals**: Confederate independence, preservation of slavery.
- **Actions**: Micromanaged military, poor delegation, feuded with generals (Johnston, Beauregard), favored Lee.
- **Relationships**: Varina Davis (influential wife); Robert E. Lee (trusted); contentious Congress.
- **Influence**: Centralized authority in theory, but weak executive in practice; states’ rights ideology hampered war effort.

#### Robert E. Lee (1807–1870)
- **Role**: Army of Northern Virginia commander (Jun 1862–Apr 1865), General-in-Chief (Jan–Apr 1865).
- **Goals**: Defend Virginia, win decisive battle to break Northern will, achieve independence.
- **Actions**: Seven Days, Second Manassas, Antietam, Chancellorsville, Gettysburg, Overland, Petersburg, Appomattox.
- **Relationships**: “Old Pete” Longstreet (corps commander, post-war critic), Stonewall Jackson (trusted lieutenant), Jeb Stuart (cavalry), Davis (political master).
- **Influence**: Tactical brilliance, offensive mindset, high casualties unsustainable; post-war “Lost Cause” icon.

#### Thomas “Stonewall” Jackson (1824–1863)
- **Role**: Lee’s most trusted corps commander.
- **Goals**: Aggressive, swift maneuver; religious conviction saw war as divine will.
- **Actions**: Valley Campaign (1862), First Manassas, Seven Days, Cedar Mountain, Second Manassas, Harpers Ferry, Antietam, Fredericksburg, Chancellorsville (flank attack).
- **Relationships**: Lee’s “right arm”; eccentric, secretive, harsh disciplinarian.
- **Influence**: Death at Chancellorsville considered turning point; Lee lost aggressive subordinate.

#### James Longstreet (1821–1904)
- **Role**: Lee’s senior corps commander (“Old War Horse”).
- **Goals**: Defensive tactics, strategic offense; post-war Republican, vilified by Lost Cause.
- **Actions**: Seven Days, Second Manassas (massive flank attack), Antietam, Fredericksburg, Gettysburg (opposed Pickett’s Charge), Chickamauga (breakthrough), Wilderness (wounded by friendly fire).
- **Relationships**: Lee’s “staff officer”; Grant’s West Point friend; post-war apostate to Lost Causers.
- **Influence**: Best defensive tactician; modern understandings vindicate his Gettysburg caution.

### Political Figures
#### William H. Seward (1801–1872) – SecState: Managed foreign relations, prevented European recognition of CSA, Alaska purchase.
#### Edwin M. Stanton (1814–1869) – SecWar (1862–68): Ruthless administrator, telegraph control, military commissions.
#### Salmon P. Chase (1808–1873) – SecTreasury (1861–64), Chief Justice (1864–73): Financial architecture (greenbacks, national banking), radical abolitionist.
#### Thaddeus Stevens (1792–1868) – House Ways & Means Chair: Radical Republican, confiscation, Black suffrage, impeachment leader.
#### Frederick Douglass (1818–1895) – Abolitionist, recruiter (54th MA), advisor to Lincoln, conscience of the war.
#### Harriet Tubman (1822–1913) – Scout, spy, nurse, Combahee River Raid (750+ freed).
#### Robert Smalls (1839–1915) – Escaped enslaved pilot, captured CSS *Planter*, Congressman Reconstruction.

### Military Figures (Additional)
- **George G. Meade** – Gettysburg victor, overshadowed by Grant.
- **George H. Thomas** – “Rock of Chickamauga,” Nashville victor, underrated.
- **Philip H. Sheridan** – Cavalry chief, Shenandoah Valley destruction, Appomattox pursuit.
- **Nathan Bedford Forrest** – “Wizard of the Saddle,” Fort Pillow massacre, KKK founder.
- **J.E.B. Stuart** – Cavalry leader, Gettysburg absence criticized.
- **Braxton Bragg** – Army of Tennessee commander, contentious, incompetent.
- **Joseph E. Johnston** – Defensive master, feuded with Davis, surrendered to Sherman.
- **John Bell Hood** – Aggressive, lost Army of Tennessee at Franklin/Nashville.

## V. MAJOR EVENTS (DETAILED ANALYSIS)

### Fort Sumter (Apr 12–13, 1861)
- **What**: Confederate batteries (Beauregard) bombard Federal fort (Anderson) 34 hours. Anderson surrenders.
- **Why**: Lincoln’s resupply expedition forced Davis’s hand: evacuate (loss of sovereignty) or attack (war).
- **Who**: Beauregard (CSA), Anderson (USA). 4,000 shells fired.
- **Immediate**: Lincoln calls 75,000 volunteers → 4 Upper South states secede. War begins.
- **Long-term**: Unified North, transformed secession from political crisis to armed rebellion.

### First Battle of Bull Run / Manassas (Jul 21, 1861)
- **What**: McDowell (35,000 USA) vs. Johnston/Beauregard (32,000 CSA). Union flank attack initially succeeds, Jackson holds Henry House Hill, Confederate counterattack routs Union.
- **Why**: Political pressure for quick victory (“On to Richmond”).
- **Who**: Civilians picnicked watching. Jackson (“Stonewall”), Stuart, Hampton (CSA); McDowell, Burnside, Sherman (USA).
- **Immediate**: Confederate morale soars; North realizes long war; McClellan summoned to organize Army of Potomac.
- **Long-term**: Myth of short war shattered; both sides mobilize for total war.

### Antietam / Sharpsburg (Sep 17, 1862)
- **What**: Lee’s invasion of Maryland (55,000) vs. McClellan (87,000). Lost Order 191 gives McClellan Lee’s plans. Three phases: Cornfield/West Woods (morning), Bloody Lane/Sunken Road (midday), Burnside Bridge (afternoon). A.P. Hill’s Light Division arrives from Harpers Ferry saves Lee’s right.
- **Why**: Lee seeks decisive victory on Northern soil, European recognition, Maryland recruitment, relieve Virginia.
- **Who**: Lee, Longstreet, Jackson, D.H. Hill, A.P. Hill (CSA); McClellan, Hooker, Mansfield, Sumner, Burnside, Franklin (USA).
- **Immediate**: 22,717 casualties (3,654 dead). Lee retreats. McClellan fails to pursue. Lincoln issues Preliminary Emancipation Proclamation.
- **Long-term**: Ends Confederate hope for foreign intervention; makes emancipation war aim; McClellan removed Nov 7.

### Emancipation Proclamation (Jan 1, 1863)
- **What**: Executive order freeing enslaved people in areas “in rebellion” (exempted border states, occupied territories). Authorized Black enlistment.
- **Why**: Military necessity (deprive South of labor, add manpower); moral purpose; preempt European recognition; satisfy Radical Republicans.
- **Who**: Lincoln, Seward, Stanton, Douglass (recruiter).
- **Immediate**: 180,000+ Black men enlist (USCT); Confederate outrage; copperhead opposition; border states exempted.
- **Long-term**: Transformed war into revolution; 13th Amendment; redefined American freedom; international moral high ground.

### Gettysburg (Jul 1–3, 1863)
- **What**: Lee (75,000) vs. Meade (90,000). Day 1: Buford’s cavalry delays, Reynolds killed, Union driven through town to Cemetery Hill. Day 2: Longstreet’s assault on left (Devil’s Den, Wheatfield, Peach Orchard, Little Round Top – Chamberlain’s 20th ME), Ewell’s demonstrations on right (Culp’s Hill). Day 3: Pickett-Pettigrew-Trimble Charge (12,500 men across ¾ mile) → 50% casualties, “High Water Mark.”
- **Why**: Lee’s second invasion: relieve Virginia, threaten Northern cities, force peace.
- **Who**: Lee, Longstreet, Hill, Ewell, Pickett, Stuart (CSA); Meade, Hancock, Warren, Chamberlain, Buford, Hunt (USA).
- **Immediate**: 51,000 casualties (7,863 dead). Lee retreats; Meade fails to trap at Potomac. Vicksburg falls Jul 4.
- **Long-term**: Confederate offensive capacity broken; Gettysburg Address; “new birth of freedom”; largest battle Western Hemisphere.

### Vicksburg Campaign & Siege (Mar 29–Jul 4, 1863)
- **What**: Grant’s amphibious operations: cross Mississippi below Vicksburg, Port Gibson, Raymond, Jackson, Champion Hill, Big Black Bridge, 47-day siege. Pemberton surrenders 29,495 men.
- **Why**: Control Mississippi River, split Confederacy (Anaconda Plan).
- **Who**: Grant, Sherman, McPherson, McClernand, Porter (USA); Pemberton, Johnston (CSA).
- **Immediate**: Union controls Mississippi; Trans-Mississippi cut off; 30,000 Confederate prisoners paroled.
- **Long-term**: Grant’s reputation secured; Lincoln: “Grant is my man”; Confederacy bisected.

### Atlanta Campaign & March to the Sea (May–Dec 1864)
- **What**: Sherman (100,000) vs. Johnston/Hood (65,000). Flanking maneuvers: Resaca, Kennesaw Mountain, Peachtree Creek, Atlanta (Jul 22), Jonesboro (Sep 1). March to Sea: 62,000 men, 60-mile front, 285 miles to Savannah. “Make Georgia howl.”
- **Why**: Destroy Army of Tennessee, capture rail hub, break Southern will, secure Lincoln re-election.
- **Who**: Sherman, Thomas, Schofield, McPherson, Howard, Logan (USA); Johnston, Hood, Hardee (CSA).
- **Immediate**: Atlanta falls Sep 2 → Lincoln landslide. Savannah captured Dec 21. $100M property destruction (1864 dollars).
- **Long-term**: Psychological collapse of Confederacy; “hard war” doctrine; Sherman’s “war is cruelty” philosophy.

### Appomattox Campaign & Surrender (Mar 29–Apr 9, 1865)
- **What**: Grant’s final offensive: Five Forks (Apr 1) breaks Lee’s right; Petersburg/Richmond fall (Apr 2–3); Lee flees west, cut off at Appomattox Court House. Surrender terms: officers keep sidearms, horses, men paroled, 25,000 rations issued.
- **Why**: Grant’s coordinated pressure; Lee’s army starving, deserting (100/day), outnumbered 3:1.
- **Who**: Grant, Meade, Sheridan, Ord, Custer (USA); Lee, Longstreet, Gordon, Mahone (CSA).
- **Immediate**: Army of Northern Virginia dissolved; Johnston surrenders Apr 26; Kirby Smith May 26.
- **Long-term**: Model of magnanimous victory; “Let ’em up easy”; Lost Cause narrative begins immediately.

## VI. MILITARY

### Army Organization
- **Union**: 2,128,948 total enlistments (1,556,678 individual men). 186,000+ USCT (10% of force). 67% volunteers, 33% draftees/substitutes.
- **Confederate**: 880,000–1,050,000 total (estimates). 75–80% of white male population 18–45 served. Conscription from Apr 1862.
- **Structure**: Army → Corps (2–4 divisions) → Division (2–4 brigades) → Brigade (4–6 regiments) → Regiment (10 companies, ~1,000 authorized, typically 300–500 present) → Company (100 authorized).

### Key Battles – Tactical Details
#### Weapons
- **Infantry**: .58 cal Springfield Model 1861 (USA), .577 Enfield Pattern 1853 (CSA) – rifled muskets, 500 yd effective range. Smoothbores (Model 1842) early war. Breechloaders (Sharps, Spencer 7-shot, Henry 16-shot) – cavalry/USCT later war.
- **Artillery**: 12-lb Napoleon (M1857) – smoothbore, workhorse. 3-in Ordnance Rifle (wrought iron), 10-lb Parrott Rifle (cast iron, reinforcing band). 20-lb Parrott, 30-lb Parrott (siege). Ammunition: solid shot, shell, spherical case (shrapnel), canister (anti-personnel <400 yd).
- **Cavalry**: Carbines (Sharps, Burnside, Spencer), sabers, pistols (Colt 1860 Army .44, Remington .44). Dismounted tactics prevalent.
- **Naval**: Ironclads (Monitor, Virginia, City-class “Pook Turtles”), timberclads, tinclads, rams, submarines (H.L. Hunley – first to sink enemy ship), torpedoes (mines).

#### Strategies
- **Union (Anaconda Plan – Winfield Scott)**: Blockade, Mississippi control, capture Richmond, divide Confederacy. Evolved to Grant’s coordinated offensives (1864): Meade vs. Lee (VA), Sherman vs. Johnston (GA), Sigel (Shenandoah), Butler (James River), Banks (Red River – failed).
- **Confederate**: Defensive war, “offensive-defensive” (Lee), interior lines, King Cotton diplomacy, guerrilla warfare (Quantrill, Mosby, Forrest), commerce raiding (Alabama, Florida, Shenandoah).

#### Troop Movements & Logistics
- **Railroads**: US Military Railroad (Herman Haupt) – 2,000+ miles built/repaired, standardized gauge, prefab bridges. CSA: 9,000 miles but multiple gauges, no central control, deteriorating.
- **Supply**: Union – depot system (City Point, VA – 100 acres, 8 wharves, 300,000 rations/day). CSA – impressment, poor distribution, salt shortage (meat preservation).
- **Medical**: Letterman System (triage, ambulance corps, field hospitals, general hospitals). Anesthesia (chloroform/ether) used 80,000+ times. Sanitary Commission (private relief). Disease killed 2:1 over battle.

### Commanders – Comparative
| Aspect | Grant/Lee |
|--------|-----------|
| **Strategic Vision** | Grant: National coordination, destruction of armies. Lee: Virginia-centric, decisive battle. |
| **Casualty Tolerance** | Grant: High (55k in 40 days) but replaced. Lee: High % (Chancellorsville 22%, Gettysburg 28%) irreplaceable. |
| **Logistics** | Grant: Masterful (Vicksburg, City Point). Lee: Chronic shortages, foraging. |
| **Subordinates** | Grant: Sherman, Sheridan, Thomas, Meade (managed). Lee: Jackson (died), Longstreet (disagreed), Hill (ill), Ewell (cautious). |

## VII. POLITICS

### United States Government
- **Executive**: Lincoln (R) – expanded war powers (suspension of habeas corpus, blockade, emancipation, conscription). Johnson (War Democrat) VP 1864.
- **Congress**: Republican majorities (37th–38th Congress). Key legislation:
  - **Homestead Act (1862)**: 160 acres public land.
  - **Morrill Land-Grant Act (1862)**: Agricultural/mechanical colleges.
  - **Pacific Railway Acts (1862, 1864)**: Transcontinental railroad.
  - **National Banking Acts (1863, 1864)**: National currency, bond sales.
  - **Legal Tender Act (1862)**: Greenbacks ($450M issued).
  - **Revenue Act (1861, 1862, 1864)**: First income tax (3% >$800, 5% >$10k), excise taxes.
  - **Confiscation Acts (1861, 1862)**: Seized rebel property, freed slaves of rebels.
  - **13th Amendment (Jan 31, 1865)**: Abolished slavery.
- **Judiciary**: Taney Court (Dred Scott) → Lincoln appointments (Chase CJ, Miller, Swayne, Davis, Field) → pro-Union court.
- **Elections**: 1862 midterms – Republican losses but hold Congress. 1864 – Lincoln/Johnson (National Union) vs. McClellan/Pendleton (Democrat). Soldier vote critical.

### Confederate Government
- **Constitution (Mar 11, 1861)**: States’ rights emphasis, 6-year presidential term, line-item veto, slavery protected in territories, no protective tariffs, no internal improvements funding.
- **Executive**: Davis (Feb 1861–May 1865). No party system. Weak bureaucracy.
- **Congress**: Unicameral Provisional (Feb 1861–Feb 1862), Bicameral Permanent (Feb 1862–Mar 1865). Factional: Fire-eaters vs. Cooperationists.
- **Judiciary**: Never fully organized (Supreme Court never met).
- **Policies**: Conscription (Apr 1862, Oct 1862, Feb 1864), Impressment (Mar 1863), Tax-in-kind (10% produce), Suspension of habeas corpus (Feb 1864), Arming slaves (Mar 1865 – too late).

### Diplomacy
- **Union Goals**: Prevent recognition, enforce blockade, stop commerce raiders.
- **Confederate Goals**: Recognition, break blockade, alliance.
- **Key Events**:
  - **Trent Affair (Nov 1861)**: USS *San Jacinto* stops RMS *Trent*, removes Mason/Slidell. UK demands release. Lincoln releases – “one war at a time.”
  - **Cotton Diplomacy**: CSA embargoes cotton → “cotton famine” in Lancashire. UK finds alternatives (Egypt, India). Fails.
  - **Emancipation Proclamation**: Makes support for CSA morally untenable for UK/France publics.
  - **Laird Rams (1863)**: UK seizes ironclads building for CSA.
  - **Alabama Claims**: CSS *Alabama* (built UK) sinks 65 US ships. Post-war arbitration (1872) – US awarded $15.5M.
  - **Maximilian in Mexico (1864–67)**: French puppet emperor; US protests (Monroe Doctrine); post-war pressure forces French withdrawal.

### Treaties & Agreements
- **Fort Sumter surrender terms (Apr 14, 1861)**: Salute to flag (50 guns, accidental death – first fatality).
- **Dix-Hill Cartel (Jul 1862)**: Prisoner exchange system (collapsed 1863 over Black POW treatment).
- **Appomattox Terms (Apr 9, 1865)**: Parole, sidearms, horses, rations.
- **Bennett Place Terms (Apr 26, 1865)**: Political terms rejected by Washington; military terms only.
- **13th Amendment (Ratified Dec 6, 1865)**.

## VIII. SOCIETY

### Daily Life
#### Soldiers
- **Union**: Average age 25.8. 48% farmers, 24% mechanics, 16% laborers. $13/mo (privates), $16/mo (1864). Rations: salt pork, hardtack, coffee, sugar, beans. Pastimes: letters, reading, gambling, baseball, religion (revivals 1863–64).
- **Confederate**: Average age similar. 69% farmers. Pay: $11/mo (often months in arrears). Rations deteriorate: cornmeal, peas, sorghum, rare meat. Uniforms: homespun, captured Union, “butternut” (walnut dye). Shoe shortage acute.
- **Black Soldiers (USCT)**: 186,000+ served. Initially $10/mo ($3 clothing deduction) vs. $13 white. Equal pay Jun 1864 (retroactive). 166 regiments. 37,000+ died (mostly disease). Fort Pillow, Poison Spring massacres.

#### Civilians
- **North**: Economic boom (war contracts, tariffs, greenbacks). Inflation ~80% total. Women: nursing (Dorothea Dix, Clara Barton), Sanitary Commission fairs ($50M raised), factory work, teaching. Draft riots (NYC 1863) – class/race violence.
- **South**: Severe privation. Blockade + lost territory + impressment + inflation (9,000% by 1865) → starvation. Richmond bread riot (Apr 1863). Women manage farms, slaves, hospitals (Sallie Tompkins, Phoebe Pember). Refugees flee Union advances.
- **Enslaved People**: “Self-emancipation” – flee to Union lines (“contrabands”). 500,000+ behind Union lines by 1865. Contraband camps (Fort Monroe, Corinth, Helena). Labor for Union Army. Families separated, reunited. Education (Freedmen’s schools).

### Culture
- **Literature**: *Uncle Tom’s Cabin* (1852), *Battle Hymn of Republic* (Howe 1862), *Dixie* (Emmett 1859), *Maryland, My Maryland*, *Bonnie Blue Flag*, *Marching Through Georgia*, *When Johnny Comes Marching Home*. Whitman (*Drum-Taps*, *Memoranda During the War*), Melville (*Battle-Pieces*), Bierce (post-war stories).
- **Photography**: Brady, Gardner, O’Sullivan, Gibson – first extensively photographed war. Wet plate collodion. *The Dead of Antietam* (Brady gallery 1862) shocks public. Stereographs popular.
- **Art**: Winslow Homer (illustrator), Conrad Wise Chapman (Confederate), Eastman Johnson. Landscape painting (Church, Bierstadt) continues.
- **Religion**: Great revivals in both armies (1863–64) – 100,000+ conversions. “Soldier’s Pocket Bible” distributed. Chaplains (5,000+ USA, 1,000+ CSA). Providence debated: punishment for slavery vs. defense of Christian civilization.

### Technology
- **Communications**: Telegraph (15,000 miles Union military lines). Cipher codes. Signal corps (wigwag flags, torches). Balloon corps (Lowe) – aerial reconnaissance.
- **Transportation**: Railroads (standard gauge North, chaos South). Steamships. Ambulance wagons (Rucker/Letterman design).
- **Weapons**: Rifled muskets, repeating rifles (Spencer, Henry), Gatling gun (limited use), ironclads, submarines (Hunley), torpedoes, observation balloons.
- **Medicine**: Anesthesia, bromine for gangrene, pavilion hospitals, ambulance system, nursing professionalization. No germ theory – miasma theory prevails.
- **Industrial**: Interchangeable parts (Springfield Armory), sewing machines (uniforms), canned food (Gail Borden condensed milk), telegraph coordination.

### Economy
| Indicator | Union | Confederacy |
|-----------|-------|-------------|
| **GDP (1860)** | $4.5B | $1.5B (incl. enslaved value) |
| **Revenue Sources** | Tariffs (60%), Excise (20%), Income Tax (10%), Loans (65% of spending) | Tariffs (blockade), Tax-in-kind, Impressment, Loans (cotton bonds), Printing (60%+ of spending) |
| **Inflation** | ~80% total (peak 1864) | 9,000% (1861–65) |
| **National Debt (1865)** | $2.7B | Repudiated |
| **Cotton Exports** | N/A | 3M bales (1860) → 300k (1862) → near 0 |

### Religion (Expanded)
- **Denominational Splits**: Methodist Episcopal (1844), Southern Baptist Convention (1845), Presbyterian Old School (1857/1861).
- **African American Churches**: AME (1816), AME Zion (1821), National Baptist Convention (1895 roots). Independent worship, literacy, resistance.
- **Catholics**: 10% USA (Irish/German immigrants). Archbishop Hughes (NY) supports Union. Sister-nurses (Daughters of Charity). Anti-Catholic nativism (Know-Nothings) fades during war.
- **Jews**: ~150,000 total. 7,000+ Union, 3,000+ Confederate. Grant’s General Order No. 11 (expulsion) revoked by Lincoln. Judah P. Benjamin (CSA SecState/War).
- **Pacifists**: Quakers, Mennonites, Dunkers – conscientious objection, relief work (Friends’ Freedmen’s Aid).

## X. INTERESTING VISUAL DETAILS (FOR DOCUMENTARY IMAGERY)

### Clothing & Uniforms
- **Union**: Dark blue wool frock coat (enlisted), sack coat (fatigue), sky-blue kersey trousers, kepi (forage cap), brogans (pegged shoes), greatcoat (sky blue). Officers: dark blue frock, gold epaulettes/shoulder straps, sash. Corps badges (1863+): I Corps (red sphere), II (blue trefoil), III (red diamond), V (red cross), VI (green cross), XI (blue crescent), XII (red star), XIV (red acorn), XV (red cartridge box), XVI (blue circle), XVII (red arrow), XVIII (red shield), XIX (blue fan), XX (blue triangle), XXIII (red heart), XXIV (red square), XXV (red diamond).
- **Confederate**: Cadet gray / butternut (walnut/copperas dye). Frock coat, shell jacket (short), trousers (gray/blue/brown). Slouch hat (broad brim), kepi. Officers: gold braid (Austrian knots) on sleeves, collar stars (general: 3 stars in wreath). State buttons (SC palmetto, VA “Sic Semper Tyrannis”). Patchwork uniforms late war.
- **Zouaves**: Red fez, blue jacket, red sash, baggy red trousers (114th PA, 5th NY, 146th NY, LA Tigers CSA).
- **USCT**: Standard Union uniform. 54th MA: dress parade at Boston Common (Shaw on horse).
- **Civilians**: Women – hoop skirts (cage crinoline), day/evening dresses, mourning wear (black crepe, jet jewelry). Men – frock coats, top hats, bowlers, beards (Lincoln, Grant, Lee, Chamberlain). Enslaved: osnaburg (coarse linen), cast-off clothing. Contrabands: Union cast-offs, issued clothing.

### Buildings & Architecture
- **White House**: Gaslight, telegraph room, office, family quarters. Lincoln’s bedroom (emancipation draft).
- **Executive Mansion (Richmond)**: Greek Revival, Davis’s office, second floor parlor (Hampton Roads).
- **Tredegar Iron Works**: Brick foundry, rolling mill, cannon boring, casting. Chimneys, water wheel.
- **Capitol (Washington)**: Dome under construction (Statue of Freedom placed Dec 2, 1863). Bakery in basement. Hospital in rotunda.
- **Capitol (Richmond)**: Jefferson design, Houdon Washington statue. Confederate Congress met here.
- **Hospitals**: Pavilion style (Chimborazo – 150 buildings, 8,000 beds; Satterlee – 4,500 beds, Philadelphia). Tents, amputee wards.
- **Prisons**: Andersonville (stockade, 26.5 acres, “deadline,” no shelter, polluted creek). Libby (warehouse, 3 floors, 1,200 officers). Elmira (barracks, “Hellmira”). Camp Douglas (Chicago, 6,000+ Confederate dead).
- **Churches**: Used as hospitals (Fredericksburg, Gettysburg). Pews removed, straw on floors.
- **Plantations**: “Big House,” slave quarters (saddlebag cabins), cotton gin, smokehouse, kitchen separate. Ruins (Sherman’s path).
- **Fortifications**: Earthworks (gabions, fascines, abatis, fraise, bombarde). Petersburg trenches (9 months, zigzag, bombproofs). Washington forts (68 enclosed works, 93 batteries, 20 miles).

### Landscapes
- **Virginia**: Rolling Piedmont, Rappahannock/Rapidan rivers, Wilderness (dense second-growth, tangles), Shenandoah Valley (breadbasket), James River.
- **Western Theater**: Tennessee/Cumberland Rivers, Mississippi River bluffs (Vicksburg), Appalachian foothills (Chattanooga, Lookout Mountain), Georgia red clay, Atlanta rail junction.
- **Border**: Maryland rolling farmland (Antietam – cornfield, sunken road, stone bridge). Pennsylvania ridges (South Mountain, Cemetery Ridge, Little Round Top).
- **Coastal**: Charleston Harbor (Fort Sumter, Morris Island, Battery Wagner). Mobile Bay (torpedoes, Fort Morgan). Gulf blockade running (Nassau, Havana, Matamoros).
- **Seasons**: Mud (Virginia spring), dust (summer marches), autumn colors (Antietam, Gettysburg), winter quarters (log huts, chimneys, 1863–64).

### Vehicles & Transport
- **Rail**: 4-4-0 “American” type locomotives, boxcars, flatcars (artillery), hospital cars, telegraph repair cars. USMRR construction trains (prefab bridges, track). Armored trains (rare).
- **Wagons**: 6-mule army wagon (2,800 lbs), ambulance (2-horse, 4-man litter), pontoon wagons (canvas boats), forge wagons, ordnance wagons. “Wagon train” miles long.
- **River**: City-class ironclads (“Pook Turtles” – 13 guns, 2.5" armor), timberclads (Lexington, Tyler, Conestoga), tinclads (light draft, patrolling), rams (Ellet’s fleet), transports (sidewheel, sternwheel).
- **Ocean**: USS *Monitor* (turret, low freeboard), CSS *Virginia* (casemate, ram), USS *Kearsarge* vs. CSS *Alabama* (Cherbourg), blockade runners (low, fast, anthracite coal, smokeless).
- **Horse/Mule**: 1,000,000+ horses, 500,000+ mules used. Cavalry mounts, artillery teams (6 horses per gun), supply wagons. Fodder requirement: 25 lbs hay + 14 lbs grain/day/animal. Glanders, exhaustion.

### Weapons (Visual Details)
- **Springfield 1861**: 56" long, 9 lbs, bayonet (18" socket), percussion cap. Stack arms, fix bayonets, load in 9 motions.
- **Enfield 1853**: Similar, brass furniture, 3-band. CSA standard.
- **Napoleon 12-pdr**: Bronze tube, 1,227 lbs, 5-ft wheels, limber/chest (32 rounds). Crew of 6+ driver.
- **Parrott Rifle**: Cast iron, reinforcing band at breech, distinctive profile. 10-lb (2.9"), 20-lb (3.67"), 30-lb (4.2").
- **Sharps Carbine**: Breechloading, paper/c linen cartridge, pellet primer (Lawrence). Cavalry standard.
- **Spencer Repeater**: 7-shot tube in buttstock, lever action, .56-56 rimfire. “Horizontal shot tower.”
- **Colt 1860 Army**: .44 cal, 6-shot, creeping loading lever. Remington 1858: topstrap, removable cylinder.
- **Cavalry Saber**: Model 1840 “Old Wristbreaker” (heavy), Model 1860 Light Cavalry (curved). Officers: Model 1850 Foot, 1860 Staff & Field.
- **Torpedoes (Mines)**: Rains “keg” (watertight, contact), Singer (spring), framed (Chesapeake). CSS *Albemarle* sunk by spar torpedo (Cushing).
- **H.L. Hunley**: 40-ft iron cylinder, hand-cranked, spar torpedo. Sank *Housatonic* Feb 1864, then lost. Recovered 2000.

### Flags & Symbols
- **USA**: 33-star (1861), 34-star (Kansas 1861), 35-star (WV 1863), 36-star (NV 1865). National color (stars/stripes), regimental color (blue, eagle, stars). Guidons (swallowtail).
- **CSA**: “Stars and Bars” (7→13 stars, 3 bars – confused with US at Manassas). “Stainless Banner” (1863 – battle flag canton on white field – mistaken for truce). “Blood-Stained Banner” (1865 – red vertical bar added). Battle Flag (Southern Cross): blue St. Andrew’s cross, 13 white stars, red field – army, navy jack. Unit flags: silk, painted battle honors.
- **Symbols**: Eagle (US), Palmetto (SC), Lone Star (TX), Pelican (LA), Rattlesnake (“Don’t Tread on Me”), Phrygian cap (liberty), Broken chains (emancipation), Masonic symbols (officers), Corps badges (see above).

## XI. CONSEQUENCES

### Immediate Consequences (1865–1866)
- **Human Cost**: 750,000 dead, 400,000+ wounded (many amputees – 60,000 Union, 30,000 Confederate). Widows, orphans, psychological trauma (“soldier’s heart” / PTSD).
- **Economic**: South devastated – 2/3 railroads destroyed, 40% livestock gone, farms untilled, currency worthless, enslaved property ($3.5B) gone. North: industrial boom, national debt $2.7B, high tariffs, greenback depreciation (gold $285/100 greenback 1864).
- **Political**: Lincoln assassinated → Andrew Johnson (Democrat, Unionist, racist) President. 13th Amendment ratified Dec 1865. Southern states under military occupation. Black Codes enacted (MS, SC Dec 1865).
- **Social**: 4 million freedpeople – family reunification, education (Freedmen’s Bureau schools), labor contracts, sharecropping begins. Refugees return. Confederate veterans demobilized (paroled, no pensions from US).

### Reconstruction (1865–1877)
- **Presidential (1865–67)**: Johnson’s leniency, pardons, restored property (except slaves). Black Codes → Northern outrage.
- **Congressional / Radical (1867–77)**: Military districts (5), 14th Amendment (citizenship, equal protection, 1868), 15th Amendment (suffrage, 1870), Civil Rights Act 1866/1875, Enforcement Acts (KKK), Freedmen’s Bureau extended. Impeachment of Johnson (1868). Grant elected 1868.
- **Achievements**: 16 Black Congressmen (2 Senators – Revels, Bruce), state constitutions, public schools, railroads, civil rights laws.
- **Failure**: Compromise of 1877 (Hayes/Tilden), troops withdrawn, “Redemption,” Jim Crow, disenfranchisement, lynching, Lost Cause mythology.

### Long-term Historical Impact
- **Constitutional**: 13th, 14th, 15th Amendments – “Second Founding.” Federal supremacy established (Texas v. White 1869 – secession illegal). Due process, equal protection basis for 20th century civil rights.
- **National State**: Income tax, national banking, land-grant colleges, transcontinental railroad, Homestead Act, Department of Agriculture (1862), Pension Bureau (largest federal agency by 1890s).
- **Economy**: Accelerated industrialization, corporate capitalism, Republican dominance (1860–1912, 1896–1932), West opened (homesteads, railroads, Indian Wars).
- **Race Relations**: Emancipation without equality. Sharecropping, convict leasing, Great Migration (1910–70), Civil Rights Movement (1950s–60s) rooted in 14th/15th Amendments. Reparations debate.
- **Memory & Historiography**:
  - *Lost Cause* (1880s–1930s): Slavery benign, states’ rights, Lee/Jackson saints, Reconstruction tragic. UDC, UCV, monuments (1900s–1920s).
  - *Progressive/Revisionist* (1920s–40s): Needless war, economic determinism (Beard), blundering generation.
  - *Modern Consensus* (1960s–present): Slavery central cause, emancipation central outcome, Black agency, total war, gender/environmental/new military history.
  - *Current Debates*: Monument removal, 1619 Project, Critical Race Theory, Confederate base renaming (Fort Bragg → Fort Liberty, etc.).

### Legacy in Culture & Institutions
- **Memorial Day**: Originated as Decoration Day (1868, Logan GAR order). Arlington ceremonies.
- **Veterans Organizations**: GAR (Union, 400,000 peak), UCV (Confederate), USCT veterans. Pensions → first mass welfare program.
- **Battlefield Preservation**: 1890s–1930s (Antietam, Gettysburg, Chickamauga, Shiloh, Vicksburg). National Military Parks. NPS management since 1933.
- **West Point / Annapolis**: Curriculum shaped by war. Generals of WWI/WWII (Pershing, Marshall, Eisenhower, Patton) studied Civil War.
- **Literature/Art**: *The Red Badge of Courage* (1895), *Gone with the Wind* (1936), *The Killer Angels* (1974), *Cold Mountain* (1997). Ken Burns *The Civil War* (1990) – 40M viewers.
- **Language**: “Deadline,” “sideburns” (Burnside), “hookers” (Fighting Joe’s camp followers), “skedaddle,” “greenback,” “carpetbagger,” “scalawag,” “solid South.”
